Power cuts in Nairobi usually last minutes or a few hours, but they can
interrupt work calls, spoil food and knock out the Wi-Fi. There are several
ways to cope, from a small battery for the router to full solar.

## The options

- **A UPS** (small battery backup) - keeps the router and a laptop running for
  an hour or more. Cheap, and the first thing remote workers should buy.
- **An inverter with batteries** - charges from the grid, then powers lights,
  TV, Wi-Fi and the fridge during a cut. Silent, and no fuel.
- **Solar with batteries** - panels charge the batteries and cut your power
  bill as well as covering outages.
- **A generator** - runs almost anything, but is noisy, needs fuel and must be
  used outdoors. Many apartment blocks have one for common areas; houses often
  have their own.

## What solar costs

As a rough guide in 2026, a **5kW hybrid system** with lithium batteries,
enough for many family homes, costs around **KES 350,000 to 650,000**
installed. The price depends mostly on:

- **Battery size** - the biggest cost. More storage covers longer outages and
  the evening.
- **Inverter and panel brands.**
- **The roof and wiring.**

Smaller systems that cover lights, Wi-Fi and the TV cost far less.

## Is it worth it?

- **If you own a house** with good sun on the roof, solar can pay back through
  lower power bills over several years.
- **If you rent a flat**, a UPS or small inverter is usually enough, and you
  can take it with you.
- **Electric showers and water heaters** use a lot of power; a **solar water
  heater** is often the best-value first step.

## Choosing an installer

- **Use a licensed installer.** Solar technicians and contractors are licensed
  by the **Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA)**.
- **Get two or three quotes** based on your actual usage, not just a package.
- **Ask about warranties** for panels, inverter and batteries, and who services
  them.
- **Check the safety** - proper earthing, surge protection and a changeover
  switch.

## Generators safely

Run generators **outdoors**, away from windows, and never in a closed garage -
exhaust fumes can kill. Store fuel safely.

High blood pressure and diabetes are rising fast in Kenya, and many people
find out only when something goes wrong. Most of the tests that catch them
early are quick and cheap. This is general information, not medical advice -
a doctor will tell you what you need and how often.

## Where to go

- **Your SHA-registered primary care facility** - a dispensary, health centre
  or clinic you choose when you [register for SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha).
  SHA's primary healthcare covers basic consultations and checks there; ask
  the facility what is included.
- **Private hospitals and clinics** - most sell **wellness packages**
  bundling a consultation, blood tests and scans. See
  [Hospitals in Nairobi](/guides/hospitals-in-nairobi).
- **Pharmacies** - many check blood pressure and blood sugar on the spot.
- **Free screening days** - hospitals, counties and charities run regular
  campaigns, especially for cancer and diabetes.

If you have private cover, check whether it includes an annual check-up; many
outpatient plans do. See [Health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).

## Tests most adults should talk about

- **Blood pressure** - at least yearly. It has no symptoms until it causes a
  stroke or heart disease.
- **Blood sugar** - for diabetes, especially if you are overweight, inactive,
  or it runs in your family.
- **Cholesterol** and **kidney function**, usually in the same blood test.
- **Weight and waist** - a simple measure of risk.
- **HIV** - testing is free at public facilities and many clinics.
- **Eyes and teeth** - an optician and a dentist, every year or two.

## Cancer screening

- **Cervical cancer** - one of the commonest cancers in Kenyan women, and
  largely preventable. Women should be screened regularly; ask about VIA, Pap
  smear or HPV testing. The HPV vaccine is offered to girls.
- **Breast cancer** - know how your breasts normally feel, and ask about
  clinical examination and mammograms as you get older.
- **Prostate cancer** - men, especially from middle age, should ask a doctor
  about a PSA test.
- **Bowel cancer** - ask about screening from middle age, earlier with a
  family history.

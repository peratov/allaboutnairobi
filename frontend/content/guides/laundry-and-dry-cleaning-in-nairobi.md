Most Nairobi homes don't have a tumble dryer, and many don't have a washing
machine either. Washing is done by hand, by a **mama fua** who comes by the
day, by a machine on the balcony, or by a laundry service that collects and
delivers.

## Mama fua

A **mama fua** ("washing mama") is a woman who washes, dries and irons
clothes in your home, paid by the day or by the load. Many work for several
households in an estate.

- **Find one** through neighbours, your [[caretaker]] or estate WhatsApp
  groups, or through home-services apps.
- **Agree the work and the pay** before she starts - washing only, or
  ironing too, and how many loads.
- **Provide soap**, basins and a line.

If someone works for you regularly, the rules on household staff apply. See
[Hiring household staff](/guides/hiring-household-staff).

## A washing machine

- **Top loaders and front loaders** are sold at electronics shops and
  online. See [Online shopping in Kenya](/guides/online-shopping-in-kenya)
  and [Setting up your home](/guides/setting-up-your-home).
- **Water** - check your flat has enough pressure, and a storage tank if
  supply is irregular. See [Water tanks and boreholes](/guides/water-tanks-and-boreholes).
- **Power** - a machine uses a fair share of your [[tokens]]. See
  [Kenya Power tokens](/guides/kenya-power-tokens).
- **Voltage** - protect it with a surge protector. See
  [Plugs, voltage and appliances](/guides/plugs-voltage-and-appliances-in-kenya).

## Laundromats and pick-up services

Laundromats in malls and estates wash by the kilo or the load, and many
laundry businesses now collect from your door and return clothes washed,
dried and folded, booked by app or WhatsApp. Ask about turnaround time,
what happens if something is lost or damaged, and how they handle whites
and delicate fabrics.

## Dry cleaners

Suits, dresses, curtains, duvets and anything marked "dry clean only" go to
a dry cleaner - there is one in most malls and shopping centres. Check the
price list, get a receipt, and point out stains when you hand things in.
For alterations, see [Fabric and tailors in Nairobi](/guides/fabric-and-tailors-in-nairobi).

## Drying in the rainy season

Clothes take days to dry in the long and short rains, and damp clothes
smell. Dry indoors near a window, use a clothes airer, iron things dry, or
use a laundry service with dryers. See
[Weather in Nairobi](/guides/weather-in-nairobi).

## Ironing

Ironing clothes dried outside is common practice in Kenya - it also kills
the eggs of insects that can lay on wet laundry. Iron everything that has
been dried outdoors, especially underwear and baby clothes.

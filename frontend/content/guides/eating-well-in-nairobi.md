Nairobi is a good place to eat well cheaply. Vegetables, fruit, beans and
fish come in from farms across Kenya every day, and the healthiest Kenyan
food is everyday food. The trap is the same as everywhere: fried snacks,
sugar and white flour are easy and cheap. General information, not medical
advice.

## Where to buy

- **The [[mama mboga]]** - the vegetable seller on your street, with greens,
  tomatoes, onions and fruit at the best prices. Many deliver if you ask.
- **Markets** - Wakulima (Marikiti) market for wholesale fruit and vegetables
  at dawn, City Market in the CBD for fish, meat and flowers, and local
  markets in every estate.
- **Supermarkets** - convenient, with packaged and imported food, usually
  dearer for fresh produce.
- **Farmers' markets and online delivery** - organic and specialist produce
  in the suburbs and by WhatsApp or apps.

What is cheap and good depends on the month: see
[What's in season in Nairobi](/seasons).

## Kenyan food that is good for you

- **Greens** - sukuma wiki (kale), managu, terere (amaranth), kunde (cowpea
  leaves) and spinach, cooked lightly.
- **Beans and pulses** - ndengu (green grams), njahi, beans and lentils, for
  protein and fibre.
- **Githeri** - maize and beans; a whole meal.
- **Fish** - omena (small dried fish) and tilapia.
- **Roots** - sweet potatoes, arrowroots (nduma) and cassava, often eaten at
  breakfast.
- **Ugali** made from whole-grain or millet and sorghum flour, rather than
  sifted white maize.
- **Fruit** - mangoes, pawpaw, bananas, avocados, passion fruit and
  pineapples, cheap in season.

Go easy on **chapati, mandazi, chips and soda**, which are everywhere and
add up fast.

## Washing food and water

- **Wash fruit and vegetables** in clean water, and peel what you can.
- **Do not drink tap water** unless it is boiled or filtered. Most homes use
  a dispenser with refillable bottles or a filter. See
  [Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi).
- Use clean water for ice, salads and brushing teeth if you have a sensitive
  stomach.

## Eating out

Street food is part of Nairobi life. Choose busy stalls where food is cooked
hot in front of you. For more, see
[Kenyan food and eating out](/guides/kenyan-food-and-eating-out).

## Cooking on a budget

Buy greens and tomatoes from the mama mboga, beans and grains in bulk,
and cook a large pot of githeri or ndengu for several days. Use the
[budget planner](/tools/budget-planner) to see what food really costs you each
month.

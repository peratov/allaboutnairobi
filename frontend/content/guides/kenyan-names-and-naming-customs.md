A Kenyan's names often tell a story - the family they belong to, the time of
day or season they were born, or the grandparent they are named after. Most
people have a **Christian or Muslim name** as well as one or more **African
names**, and many communities have their own customs.

## Naming after relatives

In many communities children are named after **grandparents** and other
relatives, honouring them and keeping their names in the family.

- Among the **Kikuyu**, tradition names the first son after the father's
  father and the first daughter after the father's mother; the next son and
  daughter after the mother's parents.
- Similar patterns are found in other communities, with variations.

So many cousins share names - and a child may be greeted as the namesake of
a grandparent.

## Names from the time or circumstances of birth

- **Luo** names often record **when** a child was born - for example
  **Otieno** or **Atieno** (at night), **Odhiambo** or **Adhiambo** (in the
  evening), **Omondi** or **Amondi** (at dawn), and **Ochieng** or
  **Achieng** (when the sun is high). Names starting **O-** are usually for
  boys and **A-** for girls.
- **Kalenjin** names commonly begin with **Kip-** or **Ki-** for boys and
  **Chep-** or **Je-** for girls, followed by a word linked to the time,
  place or circumstances of birth.
- Other communities, including the **Luhya**, **Kamba** and **Kisii**, also
  have names linked to seasons, events and family history.

## Religious and modern names

- **Christian names** - often used as first names, sometimes alongside an
  African name in school and official records.
- **Muslim names** - common at the coast, in north-eastern Kenya and in
  Muslim families across the country.
- **Swahili names** - such as **Baraka** (blessing), **Imani** (faith) and
  **Neema** (grace).

## Surnames

Kenyans don't always use a fixed family surname. A person may use their
father's name, their own African name or a grandparent's name as a
surname, so siblings may have different "surnames". Many married women add
or switch to their husband's name, but not all do. See
[Changing your name in Kenya](/guides/changing-your-name-in-kenya).

## Using names politely

- Use **titles** - Mr, Mrs, Dr, and **Mwalimu** (teacher) - until invited
  to use a first name.
- Older people may be addressed respectfully as **Mzee** (for a man) or
  **Mama** (for a woman). Parents are often called **Mama** or **Baba**
  followed by their first child's name.
- Ask how someone likes to be called; many use a different name at work
  and at home.

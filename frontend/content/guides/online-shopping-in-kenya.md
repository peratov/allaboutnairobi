Online shopping has taken off in Nairobi, from groceries and takeaway to
phones and furniture. Delivery is fast in the city, and most sites take
[[M-Pesa]]. Scams are common too, so it pays to know how to buy safely.

## Where people shop

- **Marketplaces** such as Jumia and Kilimall - electronics, fashion and
  household goods from many sellers.
- **Supermarkets** - Naivas, Carrefour, Quickmart and others deliver
  groceries through their own apps or delivery partners.
- **Food and errands** - Glovo, Uber Eats, Bolt Food and similar apps for
  restaurants, pharmacies and shops.
- **Brands and local businesses** - many sell through their own websites,
  Instagram or WhatsApp.

See also [Shopping in Nairobi](/guides/shopping-in-nairobi).

## Paying

- **M-Pesa** - at checkout, or to a named paybill or till number.
- **Card** - debit or credit card, or a virtual card. See
  [Credit cards](/guides/credit-cards-in-kenya).
- **Pay on delivery** - common, and the safest way to buy from a seller you
  don't know. Inspect the item before paying.

Check the **business name** that appears on the M-Pesa prompt before entering
your PIN.

## Delivery

Delivery within Nairobi is often same-day or next-day. Give a clear location
and landmarks, and tell your building's [[askari]] you are expecting a
delivery. Outside Nairobi, parcels go by courier or bus parcel services.

## Returns and warranties

- **Check the return policy** before buying - marketplaces usually allow
  returns within a few days for faulty or wrong items.
- **Keep the packaging and receipt**.
- **Electronics** - check the warranty, and whether it is valid in Kenya.

Kenya's **Consumer Protection Act** protects buyers from misleading sellers;
complaints can go to the **Competition Authority of Kenya**.

## Buying from abroad

Parcels from international sites can be delivered to Kenya, but may attract
**import duty and VAT**, plus handling fees, collected before delivery. Some
people use forwarding services. See [Importing goods](/guides/importing-goods-into-kenya).

## Avoiding scams

- **Social media sellers** asking for full payment before delivery, especially
  with prices far below the shops.
- **Fake delivery messages** asking you to pay a "release fee".
- **Payments to personal numbers** instead of a business till or paybill.
- **Counterfeit goods**, especially phones, chargers and cosmetics.

When in doubt, pay on delivery or buy from an established shop.

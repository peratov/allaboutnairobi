Sexual harassment at work is against the law in Kenya. The **Employment Act**
protects employees from harassment by an employer, a manager or a
co-worker - and it applies to everyone, including interns, casual workers
and domestic staff.

## What counts

Sexual harassment includes, among others:

- asking for sex or sexual favours in return for a **job, promotion, pay
  or better treatment**, or threatening worse treatment if you refuse
- unwelcome **touching**, comments, jokes or gestures of a sexual nature
- showing or sending **sexual images or messages**
- behaviour of a sexual nature that makes work **hostile or intimidating**

It doesn't matter whether the harasser "meant it as a joke" - what counts
is that it is unwelcome.

## Your employer's duty

- Employers with **20 or more employees** must have a written **sexual
  harassment policy**, agreed with employees, stating that everyone is
  entitled to work free of harassment, how complaints are made and handled,
  and that complaints are kept **confidential**.
- Employers must **act** on complaints and can be held responsible if they
  don't.

## Reporting it

1. **Keep a record** - dates, times, what was said or done, and any
   witnesses. Save messages, emails and screenshots.
2. **Report internally** - follow your workplace's policy, usually to HR or
   a designated person. Put it in writing and keep a copy.
3. **Tell someone you trust** - a colleague, friend or union
   representative.
4. If the employer doesn't act, take it further (below).

You must not be punished or dismissed for making a complaint.

## Taking it further

- **Labour office** - you can report to the county labour office.
- **Employment and Labour Relations Court** - claims for harassment,
  discrimination or unfair dismissal. Strict time limits apply. See
  [Dismissal and redundancy](/guides/dismissal-and-redundancy-in-kenya).
- **Police** - sexual assault and some other harassment are crimes. See
  [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).
- **Free help** - see [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).

complaints, investigate promptly and fairly, and protect complainants. See

Kenya holds its next **general election on Tuesday 10 August 2027**. The
Constitution sets elections for the second Tuesday of August every five
years. The **IEBC** runs them.

## Six votes on one day

Each voter gets **six ballot papers**:

1. **President** (with a running mate as Deputy President)
2. **Governor** of the county
3. **Senator** for the county
4. **Woman Member** of the National Assembly for the county
5. **Member of the National Assembly** (MP) for the constituency
6. **Member of the County Assembly** (MCA) for the ward

In Nairobi, these are for Nairobi City County and its constituencies and
wards. See [How Kenya is governed](/guides/how-kenya-is-governed).

## Winning the presidency

A presidential candidate must win **more than half** of all valid votes
cast **and** at least **25%** of the votes in **more than half of the 47
counties**. If no one does, a run-off is held between the top two.

## Voting day

- Election day is a **public holiday**.
- Go to the **polling station** where you registered, with the **same ID or
  passport** you registered with.
- You are **identified electronically** by your fingerprints or ID.
- Mark each ballot, put each in the right colour-coded box, and your finger
  is marked with ink.
- Polling stations usually open early in the morning and close in the late
  afternoon; anyone in the queue at closing can still vote.

Not registered? See [Registering to vote](/guides/registering-to-vote-in-kenya).

## Results

- Results are counted at each polling station and posted outside, then
  sent electronically and tallied at constituency, county and national
  level.
- The IEBC declares the presidential result within **seven days**.
- Challenges to the presidential result go to the **Supreme Court**.

## Living in Nairobi around the election

- **Campaigns** run for months before - expect rallies, noise and traffic.
- **Stock up** on essentials before voting day; many businesses close.
- **Avoid rallies and crowds** around results announcements, and follow
  official news rather than rumours on social media.
- **Travel** - many people go [[upcountry]] to vote, so roads and buses are
  busy before and after.
- Foreign residents and visitors should check their embassy's advice. See
  [Safety in Nairobi](/guides/safety-in-nairobi) and
  [Protests and demonstrations](/guides/protests-and-demonstrations-in-nairobi).

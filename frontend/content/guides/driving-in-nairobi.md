Kenya drives on the **left**. Nairobi driving is assertive, roundabouts are
contested, and matatus and boda bodas follow their own logic. Give yourself a
few weeks as a passenger before taking the wheel.

## Your licence

- **Visitors** can drive on a valid foreign licence for a limited period.
  Carry an International Driving Permit with it.
- **Residents** should get a Kenyan driving licence. Licences are handled by
  [[NTSA]] through [[eCitizen]]. Holders of licences from many countries can
  apply to have them recognised; otherwise it means driving school and a test.
  Start early, because it takes time.

Kenyan licences are now **smart DLs**, issued as cards.

## Insurance and documents

Third-party insurance is compulsory; comprehensive cover is sensible. Insurance
certificates are digital, and police can check them against the registration
number. Keep your licence with you, and make sure the car's inspection is
current if it is a commercial or older vehicle.

## Rules and police

- The speed limit in built-up areas is generally **50 km/h**, and lower near
  schools. Watch for signs on the highways.
- **Seatbelts** are compulsory for everyone, **phones** are not allowed while
  driving, and police run **breathalyser** checks, especially at night and
  over holidays.
- Traffic police stop cars at roadblocks. Be polite, show your documents, and
  if you are told you have committed an offence, ask for the formal notice
  rather than settling on the spot.

## Parking

Parking in the CBD and most county streets is paid, through Nairobi City
County's system by M-Pesa, and enforced by [[kanjo]] officers who clamp cars.
Malls and office buildings have their own paid parking. At night, park
somewhere guarded.

## Buying a car

Importing one yourself is covered in [Importing a car to Kenya](/guides/importing-a-car-to-kenya).

Most cars on Kenyan roads are imported used from Japan and the UK. When buying
second-hand:

- Run a search of the registration number on the NTSA system to check the
  owner and whether the car has a loan against it.
- Have a mechanic you trust inspect it.
- Complete the transfer of ownership on the NTSA system and get the logbook
  in your name. Do not pay in full until that is under way.

## Security in the car

Keep doors locked and windows up in slow traffic, with bags and phones out of
sight. Smash-and-grab theft through an open window is the most common crime
against drivers.

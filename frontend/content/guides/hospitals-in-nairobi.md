Nairobi has the best-equipped hospitals in East Africa, and people travel
from across the region for treatment here. Quality and prices vary a lot, so
it helps to know the main hospitals before you need one.

This is a guide to well-known hospitals, not a ranking.

## The major private hospitals

- **Aga Khan University Hospital** - Parklands. A teaching hospital with a
  wide range of specialists, a large emergency department and cancer and heart
  centres.
- **The Nairobi Hospital** - off Argwings Kodhek Road, near Upper Hill. One of
  the oldest and largest private hospitals, with most specialties.
- **MP Shah Hospital** - Parklands. A large general hospital.
- **Karen Hospital** - Karen. Known for heart care, and convenient for the
  south-west of the city.
- **Gertrude's Children's Hospital** - Muthaiga, with clinics across the city.
  Specialises in children.
- **Mater Misericordiae Hospital** - South B. A mission hospital known for
  heart surgery and maternity.
- **Nairobi West Hospital** and **Avenue Hospital** - mid-sized hospitals, with
  branches and clinics in several areas.

Most also run **outpatient clinics** in malls and suburbs, which are easier for
routine visits.

## Public hospitals

- **Kenyatta National Hospital (KNH)** - off Ngong Road. Kenya's largest
  referral and teaching hospital, with specialists in almost everything. Much
  cheaper than private hospitals, but very busy.
- **Mama Lucy Kibaki** and **Mbagathi** - county hospitals.

## Emergencies

- Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** or the Kenya Red Cross on
  **{{ RED_CROSS_EMERGENCY }}** for an ambulance. Many private hospitals and
  ambulance services have their own numbers, and some insurers include
  ambulance cover - save the numbers in your phone.
- **Go to the nearest emergency department** that can treat you; big private
  hospitals will stabilise patients first.
- **Carry your insurance or SHA details**, ID, and a list of medicines and
  allergies.

## Paying

- **Private hospitals** usually ask for a **deposit** before admission unless
  your insurer has approved cover.
- **With insurance,** check the hospital is on your insurer's panel, and get
  **pre-authorisation** for planned treatment. See
  [Private health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- **With SHA,** ask what SHA will pay for your treatment at that hospital and
  what you will pay yourself. See [Registering for SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha).
- **Ask for an estimate** before planned treatment, and an itemised bill after.

## Choosing a hospital

- **Location.** In traffic, the nearest good hospital matters most in an
  emergency.
- **Specialists.** Ask your GP or paediatrician which hospital they admit to.
- **Your insurance panel.**
- **Maternity.** See [Giving birth in Nairobi](/guides/giving-birth-in-nairobi).

The **Work Injury Benefits Act, 2007** (WIBA) makes every employer in Kenya
responsible for compensating employees injured at work or who catch an
occupational disease - and requires the employer to **insure** that liability
with a licensed insurer. It applies to a company with hundreds of staff and to
a household with one house manager. This is general information, not legal
advice.

## Who it covers

Anyone you employ under a contract of service, full or part time:

- office and shop staff, casual workers and interns on your payroll
- **domestic workers** - house managers, nannies, gardeners, guards and
  drivers you employ directly

Independent contractors and staff of an agency (where the agency is the
employer) are covered by their own employer.

## What it pays

WIBA sets compensation by formula, based on the worker's earnings:

- **medical costs** for the injury
- **pay while off work** recovering
- a **lump sum for permanent disablement**, scaled by the degree of disability
- **compensation to dependants** and funeral costs if the worker dies

The amounts are set in the Act as multiples of monthly earnings, which is why
insurers price WIBA on your **annual wage bill**.

## Buying cover

- **Businesses** buy a WIBA policy, often with **employer's liability** cover
  on top for claims beyond the Act.
- **Households** usually get it as a section of a
  [domestic package](/guides/home-and-contents-insurance-in-kenya), listing
  each worker and their pay.
- Declare **real wages**. Under-declaring lowers the premium and also the
  payout the insurer will make.

## When someone is hurt

1. Get them **treatment** first.
2. **Report the accident** to the Directorate of Occupational Safety and
   Health Services (DOSH) on the prescribed form, within the time the Act
   sets, and tell your insurer.
3. Keep records of the injury, treatment and time off.

Not insuring does not end the duty: an uninsured employer must pay the
compensation personally, and failing to insure is an offence.

A **real estate investment trust (REIT)** pools investors' money to own or
build property, and passes the income back to them. For Kenyans who want
property in their portfolio without a mortgage, a title deed or a tenant,
REITs are the regulated way to do it. This is general information, not
investment advice.

## Two kinds

- **Income REITs (I-REITs)** own finished, rent-earning property - malls,
  offices, warehouses, student hostels - and pay out most of the rent as
  distributions.
- **Development REITs (D-REITs)** build property to sell or to move into an
  I-REIT. They pay less income while building, and carry building risk.

Every REIT has a **trustee** (a bank or trust company), a **REIT manager**
licensed by the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)**, and a property manager.
The CMA regulates them, and the law exempts the REIT itself from income tax
so that the income reaches investors.

## Kenya's REITs

The market is small. REITs listed or quoted at the **Nairobi Securities
Exchange** include ILAM Fahari I-REIT, the Acorn I-REIT and D-REIT (student
housing), LAPTRUST Imara I-REIT and an industrial REIT from Africa Logistics
Properties, with a few more listed in 2026. Several trade on **restricted
or unquoted segments** open mainly to professional investors, or have high
minimum investments, so not all of them can be bought easily by an ordinary
saver. Check the NSE's current list.

## How to buy

1. Open a **CDS account** with a licensed stockbroker or investment bank -
   the same account as for [shares on the NSE](/guides/investing-on-the-nse).
2. Ask the broker which REITs you are eligible to buy, the minimum, and the
   fees.
3. Units trade like shares. Some REITs also raise money directly when they
   issue new units.

## The risks

- **Thin trading.** Few buyers and sellers means you may not be able to sell
  quickly, or at the price you hoped.
- **Prices below value.** Kenyan REIT units have often traded below the value
  of the property behind them.
- **Vacancies and rent.** An empty mall pays nobody.
- **Distributions are not guaranteed** and can be cut.

## REIT or a plot?

A plot needs a lump sum, a lawyer, searches and patience, and is hard to sell
in a hurry - but you control it. A REIT starts smaller, is diversified and
managed for you, but you depend on the manager and the market. Many people
hold both. Compare either with what
[money market funds](/guides/money-market-funds-in-kenya) and
[Treasury bonds](/guides/buying-treasury-bills-and-bonds) pay with far less risk.

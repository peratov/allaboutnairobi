Before you save, borrow, invest or buy insurance, check that the firm is
licensed by the right regulator. It takes a few minutes on the regulator's
website, and it is the single best protection against losing your money to a
fake or failing business. Kenya has several financial regulators, each with its
own public list.

## Who licenses what

| If the firm is a... | It should be licensed by |
| --- | --- |
| Bank or microfinance bank | **Central Bank of Kenya** (CBK) |
| Digital lender or loan app | **Central Bank of Kenya**, as a digital credit provider |
| Forex bureau, money transfer or payment service | **Central Bank of Kenya** |
| Deposit-taking SACCO | **SACCO Societies Regulatory Authority** (SASRA) |
| Other SACCOs | The **Commissioner for Co-operative Development** |
| Fund manager, money market fund or unit trust | **Capital Markets Authority** (CMA) |
| Stockbroker, investment adviser or online forex broker | **Capital Markets Authority** |
| Insurer, insurance broker or agent | **Insurance Regulatory Authority** (IRA) |
| Pension scheme or administrator | **Retirement Benefits Authority** (RBA) |

## How to check

1. **Find the firm's full legal name.** Look on its website, app store
   listing, contract or receipt. Scammers often use names very close to real
   companies.
2. **Open the regulator's own website** by typing the address yourself,
   not from a link in a message. Each regulator publishes a list of licensed
   firms, usually under "licensed institutions" or "licensees".
3. **Match the name exactly**, and check the licence is for the activity
   you are paying for. A licensed insurance agent is not licensed to run an
   investment scheme.
4. **Check the contact details** match those on the regulator's list. A real
   firm's name with a different phone number or paybill is a common trick.
5. **Look for warnings.** The CMA and CBK publish notices about firms that
   are not licensed or are under investigation.

If a firm is not on any list, do not send it money.

## Collective investment schemes and money market funds

A money market fund should be part of a scheme approved by the CMA, with a
named **fund manager**, **trustee** and **custodian** that are separate
companies. The custodian holds the investments, so the manager cannot simply
spend them. See [Money market funds in Kenya](/guides/money-market-funds-in-kenya).

## Signs it is not what it claims

- **Guaranteed high returns**, such as a fixed amount every week or month.
- Pressure to **recruit friends** or join quickly before a "deadline".
- Payment to a **personal M-Pesa number** or till rather than the firm's
  official paybill or bank account.
- Activity mainly through **WhatsApp or Telegram groups**.
- No physical office, no published accounts, or a licence it cannot show.

Read [Crypto and investment scams in Kenya](/guides/crypto-and-investment-scams-in-kenya)
for the patterns these schemes follow.

Nairobi has every option, from brand shops in the malls to the crowded
electronics stalls of the CBD. Prices can be lower in town, but so is the
protection if something goes wrong. A few checks avoid most problems.

## Where to buy

- **Brand and authorised shops** - in malls such as Sarit, The Junction,
  Two Rivers and Garden City, and on brands' own websites. Highest prices,
  but genuine stock and a proper warranty.
- **Network shops** - Safaricom and Airtel shops sell phones, often with
  instalment plans.
- **Supermarkets and electronics chains** - reliable for laptops,
  televisions and mid-range phones.
- **The CBD** - **Luthuli Avenue** and the streets around Moi Avenue and
  Tom Mboya Street are lined with phone and electronics shops. Good for
  prices, accessories and repairs, but check carefully and keep your receipt.
- **Online** - marketplaces such as Jumia and Kilimall, and sellers on
  social media. Use pay-on-delivery where you can, and see
  [Online shopping in Kenya](/guides/online-shopping-in-kenya).

## Check the phone is genuine

The **Communications Authority of Kenya** keeps a register of devices
approved for use. Counterfeit phones and phones not type-approved can be
blocked from the networks.

1. Dial **\*#06#** on the phone to show its **IMEI** number.
2. **SMS the IMEI to 1555**, or use the IMEI checker on the Communications
   Authority's website.
3. The reply gives the **make and model** registered to that IMEI. If it
   does not match the phone in your hand, do not buy it.

Also check the IMEI on the screen matches the one on the box.

## Refurbished, ex-UK and second-hand

"Ex-UK", "ex-US" and "refurbished" phones and laptops are popular and can be
good value. Before paying:

- check the **battery health** in the settings, and that it charges
- test the screen, cameras, speakers, microphone, buttons and fingerprint
  or face unlock
- make sure the phone is **not locked** to an account (iCloud or Google) or a
  foreign network
- for a laptop, check the battery, keyboard, ports, and that Windows or macOS
  is activated
- ask what the **warranty** is, and get it in writing

Buy a used phone from a stranger only in a public place, and ask for proof
of purchase - stolen phones are common.

## Receipts and warranty

Ask for a proper receipt with the shop's name and KRA PIN; registered
businesses issue an **eTIMS** invoice. You need it for warranty claims,
insurance and, if it comes to that, a police report.

## Paying in instalments

Network shops and several lenders let you pay for a phone daily or weekly.
It is convenient, but the total is often far more than the cash price, and
the phone can be locked if you miss payments. Compare the total with the cash
price first, and see [The true cost of mobile loans](/guides/mobile-loans-true-cost).

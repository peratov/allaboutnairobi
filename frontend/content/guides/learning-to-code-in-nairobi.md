Nairobi is one of Africa's biggest tech hubs, and there are many ways to
learn to code here - from free online programmes to intensive paid
bootcamps. The right one depends on your time, budget and goal.

## Your options

- **Paid bootcamps** - intensive courses of a few months in software
  engineering, data science or cybersecurity, with projects and career
  support. Moringa School and Zindua School are among the Nairobi names.
  Fees run to six figures in shillings, often payable in instalments.
- **Free and sponsored programmes** - programmes such as ALX and others
  funded by companies and foundations offer free or low-cost courses, usually
  online with local hubs. They demand discipline and many hours a week.
- **Universities** - computer science and IT degrees and diplomas. See
  [Applying to university](/guides/applying-to-university-in-kenya).
- **TVET colleges** - ICT certificates and diplomas. See
  [TVET colleges and trades](/guides/tvet-colleges-and-learning-a-trade).
- **Self-study** - free online courses and documentation, with a community
  for support.

## Choosing a course

- **Get the full cost in writing** - fees, instalment plans, laptop and
  internet costs.
- **Check the schedule** - full-time bootcamps are close to a full-time job.
  Part-time and evening tracks suit people already working.
- **Ask about outcomes** - how many graduates found tech jobs, and within
  what time. Talk to past students.
- **Look at the curriculum** - modern languages and tools, real projects,
  and code reviews.
- Be wary of promises of **guaranteed jobs**.

## What you need

- A **laptop** that can run development tools - see
  [Buying a phone or laptop](/guides/buying-a-phone-or-laptop-in-nairobi).
- Reliable **internet** - see [Home internet in Nairobi](/guides/home-internet-in-nairobi).
- **Time** - most people need many months of steady practice.

## Communities

Nairobi has active developer meet-ups, hackathons and user groups for
languages and tools, often hosted at tech hubs and
[coworking spaces](/guides/coworking-spaces-in-nairobi). They are free or
cheap, and the best way to meet people who hire.

## After you learn

- Build a **portfolio** of projects on a public code site.
- Apply for **internships** and junior roles, and consider
  [freelancing](/guides/freelancing-in-kenya).
- See [Working in tech in Nairobi](/guides/working-in-tech-in-nairobi) for
  the job market.

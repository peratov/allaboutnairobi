Many Kenyans work abroad - in the Gulf, Europe, North America and
elsewhere - and send money home. Genuine opportunities exist, but so do
fake agents who take fees and disappear, or send people into exploitative
jobs. A few checks protect you.

## Using a recruitment agency

- Agencies that recruit Kenyans for jobs abroad must be **licensed by the
  National Employment Authority** (NEA).
- **Check the agency** on the NEA's online register before you pay or hand
  over documents - its name, registration number and status. The NEA also
  publishes lists of agencies it has banned or deregistered.
- **Visit the agency's office**, and ask for its licence.

## Before you go

- **Get a written contract** - employer, job, salary, hours, days off,
  accommodation, and who pays for flights and the visa. Read it fully, and
  keep a copy at home.
- **Check the visa** is a **work visa** for that job, not a visitor visa.
- **Medical checks** and documents - you will usually need a passport and a
  certificate of good conduct. See
  [Applying for a Kenyan passport](/guides/applying-for-a-kenyan-passport)
  and [Certificate of good conduct](/guides/certificate-of-good-conduct).
- **Attend pre-departure training** where offered; it covers your rights
  and the country's laws.
- **Register with the Kenyan embassy** in the country when you arrive.

## Gulf jobs

Domestic work, security, hospitality and driving jobs in the Gulf are
common. Kenya has labour agreements with several Gulf countries, but
workers - especially house helps - can face long hours, unpaid wages or
confiscated passports. Use only licensed agencies, keep your passport
copies and family contacts, and know how to reach the embassy.

## Warning signs of a scam

- **Large fees** paid to an individual's M-Pesa number, or "processing fees"
  before any interview.
- **Guaranteed** jobs or visas, or a job offer without any interview.
- Pressure to **travel on a tourist visa** and "change it later".
- An agency not on the **NEA register**, or with no fixed office.
- Contracts you can't keep a copy of.

See [M-Pesa fraud and scams](/guides/mpesa-fraud-and-scams-in-kenya) and
[Applying for visas from Nairobi](/guides/applying-for-visas-from-nairobi).

## Applying directly

Many skilled jobs - nursing, IT, engineering, teaching - are applied for
directly with employers or through official programmes. Check the
employer's website and the destination country's official immigration
site for work visa routes.

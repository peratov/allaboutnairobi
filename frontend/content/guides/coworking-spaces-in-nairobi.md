Nairobi has a large coworking scene, driven by startups, remote workers and
companies that want flexible space. For many people the main reason to pay
for one is simple: **reliable internet and backup power**.

## Where they are

- **Westlands** - the largest cluster, near offices, malls and restaurants.
- **Kilimani and Upper Hill** - central, with several spaces along Ngong Road
  and Argwings Kodhek.
- **Karen** - quieter spaces for people living in the south-west.
- **Gigiri and along Thika Road** - fewer, but growing.

Operators range from international chains to local spaces with a community of
startups and freelancers.

## What you can rent

- **Day pass** - a desk for the day. Good for trying a space or as a fallback.
- **Hot desk** - any free desk, monthly.
- **Dedicated desk** - your own desk, with storage.
- **Private office** - a lockable room for a team.
- **Meeting rooms** - by the hour, often with credits included in
  memberships.
- **Virtual office** - a business address and mail handling, useful for company
  registration and the county permit.

## What to check

- **Internet** - speed, and a backup line from a second provider.
- **Power** - a generator or solar backup that covers the desks.
- **Hours** - 24-hour access if you work with other time zones.
- **Call rooms** - quiet booths for video calls.
- **Parking and security.**
- **What is included** - coffee, printing, meeting-room hours, lockers.

Ask for a **free trial day** to test the connection at the times you work.

## Coworking or home?

A good home setup with fibre and a UPS is cheaper. Many remote workers do both:
home most days, and a day pass when the power or internet is out. See
[Home internet](/guides/home-internet-in-nairobi) and
[Solar and backup power](/guides/solar-and-backup-power).

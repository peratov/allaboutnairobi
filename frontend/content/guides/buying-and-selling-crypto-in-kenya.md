Most Kenyans buy crypto with **M-Pesa** or a bank transfer, either through an
exchange or by trading directly with another person. Both work; both have
traps. This is general information, not financial advice - crypto prices can
fall a long way, fast.

## The three ways in

- **A licensed local platform.** Since the
  [VASP Act](/guides/crypto-in-kenya), Kenyan platforms must be licensed by the
  CMA or CBK. Paying in and out by M-Pesa or bank is simplest here.
- **An international exchange.** Large exchanges serve Kenyans, usually with
  identity checks. Paying in is often done through their peer-to-peer market.
- **Peer-to-peer (P2P).** You buy from or sell to another user, paying by
  M-Pesa or bank, while the platform holds the crypto in **escrow** until the
  seller confirms payment.

## P2P safely

P2P is where most problems happen:

- **Trade only inside the platform's escrow.** Never send crypto or money
  "outside" to save a fee.
- **Confirm M-Pesa money in the M-Pesa app or statement**, not from an SMS
  someone shows you. Fake confirmation messages are the oldest trick.
- **Check the name** on the payment matches the account you are trading
  with. Money from a third party may be stolen, and the real owner can have it
  reversed or report you.
- **Release crypto only when the money has arrived** and cleared.
- Prefer traders with long, verified histories.

## Bank and M-Pesa freezes

Banks sometimes **freeze accounts** that receive many payments from strangers,
which is what heavy P2P selling looks like. Keep records of every trade, use
an account you can explain, and expect questions on large amounts.

## Keeping it

- **On the exchange** - convenient, but the exchange holds the keys. If it
  fails or freezes your account, you wait.
- **In your own wallet** - you hold the keys. Lose the recovery phrase and the
  crypto is gone for good; share it and it can be stolen. Write it on paper,
  keep it offline, never type it into a website or send it to "support".
- Turn on **two-factor authentication** with an app, not SMS - SIM-swap fraud
  is common in Kenya.

## Stablecoins

Stablecoins track a currency, usually the US dollar. Kenyans use them to
hold dollars, get paid by clients abroad, or move money across borders. They
carry the issuer's risk and the platform's risk, and they are not a bank
account. For ordinary remittances, compare the cost with the
[rate checker](/tools/rate-checker) first.

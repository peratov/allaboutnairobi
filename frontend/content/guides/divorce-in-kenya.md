Ending a marriage is hard, and the legal side can feel daunting. In Kenya,
divorce is granted by a **court**, under the **Marriage Act 2014** for civil,
Christian, Hindu and customary marriages. Islamic marriages are dissolved
under Islamic law, through the **Kadhi's courts**.

## Grounds

Courts grant divorce on grounds such as:

- **adultery**
- **cruelty**, physical or mental
- **exceptional depravity**
- **desertion** for a long period
- the marriage having **irretrievably broken down** - for example after a
  long separation

The details vary by type of marriage. The old rule that couples must wait
**three years** after a civil marriage before petitioning has been
challenged and found unconstitutional by the High Court; ask a lawyer how it
applies to you.

## The process

1. **Get advice** - a family lawyer, or free help if you can't afford one.
   See [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).
2. **File a petition** for divorce in court, with your marriage certificate
   and the grounds.
3. **Serve** the petition on your spouse, who can respond.
4. **Hearing** - if uncontested, it can be quick; contested cases take
   longer.
5. **Decree nisi**, and later the **decree absolute**, which finally ends the
   marriage.

Courts may encourage **mediation** first, especially over children and
property.

## Customary marriages

Customary marriages can also be dissolved by a court. Courts may take into
account any customary process followed by the families. See
[Dowry and customary marriage](/guides/dowry-and-customary-marriage-in-kenya).

## Matrimonial property

Under the **Matrimonial Property Act 2013**, property acquired during the
marriage is divided according to each spouse's **contribution** - which
includes money and also work in the home, childcare and managing property.
Keep records of what you paid for and contributed.

## Children

Arrangements for children - where they live, contact and maintenance - are
decided on the **best interests of the child**. See
[Child custody and maintenance](/guides/child-custody-and-maintenance-in-kenya).

## Safety

If you are being abused, you can get a **protection order** without waiting
for a divorce. See
[Domestic violence and protection orders](/guides/domestic-violence-and-protection-orders-in-kenya).

## After divorce

- Update your [will](/guides/making-a-will-in-kenya), insurance and pension
  nominees.
- If you want to revert to a former name, see
  [Changing your name in Kenya](/guides/changing-your-name-in-kenya).

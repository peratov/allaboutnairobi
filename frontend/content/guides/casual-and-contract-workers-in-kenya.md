Many people in Nairobi work as **casuals** - paid by the day - or on
short contracts. The law gives them more protection than many employers
admit, especially once the work continues.

## Who is a casual worker

Under the **Employment Act 2007**, a casual employee is someone paid at the
end of **each day** and not engaged for more than 24 hours at a time. Casual
work suits genuinely short jobs: unloading a lorry, a day's cleaning,
an event.

## When casual work becomes regular

If a casual worker works for a **continuous period, or periods adding up to
a month or more**, or does work that cannot reasonably be finished in three
months, the law **converts** the contract into one paid **monthly**, with
the protections of a regular employee. That means:

- **notice** before the job ends
- **annual leave**, **sick leave** and the other leave entitlements (see
  [Leave entitlements in Kenya](/guides/leave-entitlements-in-kenya))
- protection from **unfair dismissal** (see
  [Dismissal and redundancy](/guides/dismissal-and-redundancy-in-kenya))

The courts look at the reality, not the label: calling someone a "casual"
for years does not keep them one.

## Fixed-term contracts

A contract for a set period - three months, a year - ends on its date. But:

- while it runs, you have the **same rights** as any employee
- ending it **early** needs a valid reason and fair procedure, like any
  dismissal
- **renewing** short contracts again and again for a permanent job can
  create an expectation of renewal, which courts have protected

## Part-time workers

Part-time employees are employees: they are owed leave and other rights in
proportion to the time they work, and at least the
[minimum wage](/guides/minimum-wage-in-kenya) for the hours they do.

## Pay and deductions

- Casual and contract workers must be paid at least the **minimum wage**.
- Deductions for **NSSF**, **SHIF** and the **Housing Levy** apply to them
  too, and the employer must pay its share. See
  [PAYE and taxes](/guides/paye-and-taxes).
- Ask for a **payslip** or written record of each payment, and keep your own
  record of the days you worked.

## Independent contractors

A freelancer or consultant running their own business is not an employee:
they invoice, choose how to do the work, and pay their own taxes. But an
employer cannot avoid employment law by calling a worker a "consultant" if
in practice they work set hours under the employer's control. See
[Freelancing in Kenya](/guides/freelancing-in-kenya).

## For employers

- Use casual work only for short, genuinely casual jobs.
- Once work continues, put the worker on a **written contract**.
- Keep **records** of days worked and pay.
- See [Hiring your first employee](/guides/hiring-your-first-employee) and
  [Hiring household staff](/guides/hiring-household-staff).

## If your rights are ignored

Raise it in writing, then go to the county **labour office**, and if needed
the **Employment and Labour Relations Court** within
**{{ EMPLOYMENT_CLAIM_YEARS }} years**. See
[Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya#when-things-go-wrong).

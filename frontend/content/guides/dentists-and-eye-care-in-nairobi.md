Dental and eye care in Nairobi range from large hospital departments to
small private clinics in the malls and the CBD. Prices vary widely, so it
pays to know what you need and what your cover includes.

## Dentists

- **Where:** private dental clinics across the city, dental departments at
  the large private hospitals, and the **University of Nairobi Dental
  Hospital** next to Kenyatta National Hospital, which is usually cheaper.
- **Check registration:** dentists are registered with the **Kenya Medical
  Practitioners and Dentists Council** (KMPDC). You can check a dentist's
  name, and the clinic's registration, on the council's website.
- **Routine care:** a check-up and cleaning every six to twelve months
  catches problems early, when they are cheaper to fix.
- **Ask for a written quote** before larger work - fillings, root canals,
  crowns, implants or braces - and ask about alternatives.

## Eye care

- **Optical shops** in malls and the CBD test eyesight and sell glasses and
  contact lenses. Prices for frames and lenses vary a lot - compare.
- **Eye doctors (ophthalmologists)** treat eye disease and do surgery, such
  as for cataracts. They work in hospital eye units and private clinics.
- **Eye hospitals and units**, including at Kenyatta National Hospital, the
  large private hospitals, and mission hospitals near Nairobi, treat
  everything from infections to surgery.
- **Get your eyes checked** if your sight changes, and regularly if you have
  diabetes or high blood pressure, or a family history of glaucoma. See
  [Health checks and screening](/guides/health-checks-and-screening-in-nairobi).

## Children

Children's teeth and eyes should be checked early - a child who cannot see
the board may seem to struggle at school. Some schools run screening.

## What insurance covers

- **SHA** covers some dental and eye treatment at contracted facilities.
  Check what is included before treatment. See [Registering for SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha).
- Private **health insurance** often treats dental and optical cover as
  optional extras, with an annual limit and a waiting period. See
  [Health insurance in Kenya](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- Ask the clinic to confirm cover with your insurer **before** treatment.

## Keeping costs down

- Get a second opinion and quote for expensive treatment.
- Ask whether a cheaper material or lens type would do.
- Keep your **prescription** for glasses: you can take it to any optical
  shop.
- Teaching hospitals are often cheaper, though waits can be longer.

## Emergencies

For severe pain, swelling, a knocked-out tooth or a sudden loss of sight,
go the same day to a hospital emergency department. See
[Emergencies in Nairobi](/guides/emergencies-in-nairobi).

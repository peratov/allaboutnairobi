Difficulty conceiving is common and affects men and women alike. Nairobi
has specialist fertility clinics and gynaecologists offering everything
from basic tests to **IVF**. Starting with good information saves time,
money and stress.

## When to seek help

See a doctor if you have been trying for **a year** without success - or
after **six months** if the woman is 35 or older, has irregular periods, or
either partner has a known condition.

## Tests

- **Both partners** should be tested. Male factors explain a large share of
  cases, and a **semen analysis** is a simple first test.
- For women - **blood tests** for hormones and ovulation, an **ultrasound**,
  and a test of the fallopian tubes.
- Tests for **infections**, including STIs, which can affect fertility. See
  [HIV testing and sexual health](/guides/hiv-testing-and-sexual-health-in-nairobi).

## Treatments

- **Lifestyle** - healthy weight, less alcohol, no smoking.
- **Medication** to bring on ovulation.
- **Surgery** for conditions such as blocked tubes, fibroids or
  endometriosis.
- **IUI** - placing prepared sperm in the womb around ovulation.
- **IVF** - eggs are collected and fertilised in a laboratory, and an
  embryo is placed in the womb. Sometimes with donor eggs or sperm.

## Choosing a clinic

- Ask about the clinic's **specialists**, laboratory and **success rates**
  for people of your age.
- Get a **full written quote** - tests, medicines, procedures, embryo
  freezing and follow-up.
- Ask what happens if a cycle is cancelled or fails.
- Get a **second opinion** before expensive treatment.

Kenya does not yet have a specific law licensing fertility clinics; an
Assisted Reproductive Technology Bill has been before Parliament.

## Costs and insurance

- **IVF is expensive** - a single cycle typically costs several hundred
  thousand shillings, and many people need more than one.
- **SHA's general packages** do not cover IVF. Some **public-servant and
  teacher schemes** have added limited IVF cover at contracted facilities.
- A few **private and employer** policies cover part of the cost, often with
  limits and co-payments. See [Health insurance in Kenya](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).

## Emotional support

Fertility treatment can be draining. Counselling and support groups help -
see [Therapy and mental health](/guides/therapy-and-mental-health-in-nairobi).

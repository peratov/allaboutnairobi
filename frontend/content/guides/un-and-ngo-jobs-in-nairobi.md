Nairobi is one of the United Nations' four main headquarters, and hosts the
regional offices of hundreds of international NGOs, donors and foundations.
They are among the city's most sought-after employers, and competition is
intense.

## Who is here

- **The United Nations Office at Nairobi (UNON)** in Gigiri, with the global
  headquarters of **UNEP** (environment) and **UN-Habitat**, and regional
  offices of many other agencies such as UNICEF, UNHCR, WFP and WHO.
- **International NGOs** in humanitarian work, health, development and rights.
- **Donors and foundations**, and **research institutes** such as ILRI and
  ICRAF (CIFOR-ICRAF).

## Kinds of UN posts

- **National Professional Officers (NO)** - professional roles open to
  Kenyan nationals.
- **General Service (G)** - support roles, recruited locally.
- **International Professional (P) and Director (D)** - open to all
  nationalities, with international terms.
- **Consultants and individual contractors** - short-term, often the way in.
- **UN Volunteers (UNV)**, internships and **Junior Professional Officer (JPO)**
  programmes - sponsored by some governments for their nationals.

## Where vacancies are advertised

- **careers.un.org** - the UN Secretariat's jobs site, including UNON, UNEP and
  UN-Habitat.
- **Each agency's own careers site** - UNICEF, UNDP, UNHCR, WFP and others
  recruit separately.
- **ReliefWeb** and other humanitarian job boards for NGO posts.
- **NGOs' own websites** and professional networks.

Set up alerts. Posts close on fixed dates, and late applications are not
accepted.

## Applying

- **Follow the format exactly.** Many systems use their own online profile
  or the UN's personal history form; tailor it to each post.
- **Match the criteria.** Screening is strict on education, years of
  experience and languages. Use the vacancy's language in your application.
- **Show results** - numbers, outcomes and responsibilities.
- **Expect a test** - written assessments are common - and a
  **competency-based interview**, where you answer with specific examples
  (situation, task, action, result).

Recruitment can take months. Keep applying while you wait.

## Scams

The UN and reputable NGOs **never charge fees** to apply or to process
recruitment. Offers asking for payment for visas, training or "processing"
are scams.

## Work permits

Foreign nationals hired by NGOs usually need a work permit. UN international
staff come under separate arrangements. See
[Class D work permit](/guides/class-d-work-permit).

Nairobi can be cheap or very expensive, and the difference is mostly three
things: which neighbourhood you rent in, whether you buy imported brands, and
whether you get around by matatu or by car.

The figures below are **rough 2026 ranges**, not quotes. Prices move with
inflation, fuel prices and the shilling, so build your own budget from real
prices once you arrive.

## Two rough monthly budgets

For a single person, in KES:

| | Modest | Comfortable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | 15,000 - 30,000 | 50,000 - 90,000 |
| Food and household shopping | 12,000 - 20,000 | 30,000 - 50,000 |
| Eating out | 2,000 - 5,000 | 15,000 - 30,000 |
| Transport | 4,000 - 8,000 (matatu) | 15,000 - 30,000 (ride-hailing or car) |
| Electricity, water, gas | 2,500 - 5,000 | 6,000 - 12,000 |
| Internet and phone | 2,000 - 4,000 | 5,000 - 8,000 |
| Service charge, rubbish, security | 0 - 2,000 | 3,000 - 10,000 |

Add health insurance, school fees, household staff and travel home if they
apply to you.

Plan your own with the [budget planner](/tools/budget-planner).

## Food

- **Local produce is cheap**, especially from a [[mama mboga]] or a market:
  sukuma wiki, tomatoes, onions, bananas, avocados and mangoes in season.
- **Supermarkets** such as Naivas, Quickmart, Carrefour and Chandarana are
  convenient. Imported brands cost much more than local ones.
- **Eating out** ranges from a plate of githeri or chapati and beans at a
  kibanda for a couple of hundred shillings to restaurant prices that match
  Europe's.

## Transport

A matatu ride within the city usually costs under KES 100, more in the rain and
at rush hour. Ride-hailing is several times that per trip. Running a car means
fuel, insurance, parking and servicing; the roads are hard on suspension. See
[Getting around Nairobi](/guides/getting-around-nairobi).

## Costs people forget

- **Deposits** - usually one or two months' rent, plus utility deposits.
- **Furniture and appliances** - most rentals are unfurnished, often without a
  fridge or cooker.
- **Water** when the supply fails and you have to buy a bowser.
- **Family and community contributions** - weddings, funerals, [[harambee]]s
  and helping relatives are a real, regular expense for many Nairobians.
- **Deductions from salary** - see the [PAYE guide](/guides/paye-and-taxes)
  for what comes off before you are paid.

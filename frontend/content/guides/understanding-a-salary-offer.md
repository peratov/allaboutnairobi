Job offers in Kenya are usually quoted as a **gross monthly salary**. What
reaches your bank account is noticeably less, and benefits can be worth as
much as a pay rise. This is how to compare offers properly.

## Gross vs take-home

From your gross pay, your employer deducts:

- **PAYE** (income tax), from 10% up to 35% on the top band.
- **NSSF**, {{ NSSF_RATE|percent }}% up to the upper earnings limit.
- **SHIF**, {{ SHIF_RATE|percent }}% for the Social Health Authority.
- **The Housing Levy**, {{ HOUSING_LEVY_RATE|percent }}%.

Put any offer into the [salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator) to see
your net pay. See [PAYE and taxes](/guides/paye-and-taxes) for how each
deduction works.

## What else is in the package

- **Allowances** - house, transport or airtime. Most are taxable, and
  counted in gross pay.
- **Medical cover** - private cover for you and often your family. Check the
  limits; this can be worth hundreds of thousands of shillings a year. See
  [Private health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- **Pension** - an employer pension scheme on top of NSSF, often with the
  employer matching your contributions.
- **Bonus** - ask whether it is guaranteed or discretionary, and how it has
  paid out in the past.
- **Leave** - the legal minimum is {{ ANNUAL_LEAVE_DAYS }} working days a
  year; many employers give more.
- **Staff loans and mortgages** - banks especially offer cheaper loans to
  staff.

## Questions to ask

1. Is the figure **gross or net**, and monthly or annual?
2. Which **allowances** are included, and are they taxable?
3. What does the **medical cover** include, and does it cover my family?
4. Is there a **pension scheme**, and what does the employer contribute?
5. How long is **probation**, and what is the **notice period**?
6. When are **salary reviews**, and how are increases decided?
7. For expatriate roles - who pays for the **work permit**, relocation,
   housing, school fees and flights home?

## Comparing salaries

Salaries vary widely by sector. International organisations, banks, large
technology companies and NGOs usually pay more than local private firms, and
far more than the minimum wage. Useful ways to benchmark:

- **Ask people in the same role**, and recruiters in your field.
- **Look at advertised ranges** on job boards.
- **Use published salary surveys** from recruitment firms, knowing they
  reflect larger employers.

See [Finding a job in Nairobi](/guides/finding-a-job-in-nairobi).

## Get it in writing

Before you resign from your current job, get a **written contract** stating the
salary, benefits, start date, probation and notice. See
[Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya#the-contract).

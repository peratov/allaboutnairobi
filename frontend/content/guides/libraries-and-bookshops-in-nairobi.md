Nairobi has a lively book culture: public libraries being restored, busy
bookshops in the malls, and pavement sellers in the CBD with piles of
second-hand novels. Here is where to find books.

## Public libraries

- **The McMillan Memorial Library**, on Banda Street in the CBD, is
  Nairobi's oldest public library, opened in 1931. The main building
  **closed in 2026 for restoration** - check whether it has reopened before
  you go.
- **Kaloleni** and **Makadara** branch libraries in Eastlands have been
  renovated and reopened, with reading spaces, children's sections and
  events, through the non-profit **Book Bunk** working with Nairobi County.
- **The Kenya National Library Service** (KNLS) runs public libraries
  across the country, including in Nairobi. Membership is cheap, and you
  usually need ID.

## Other libraries

- **University libraries** - mainly for students and staff, though some
  offer paid external membership.
- **Cultural centres** - the Alliance Française and the Goethe-Institut
  have libraries and run talks, readings and events.
- **School and community libraries** - many estates and churches have small
  lending libraries, especially for children.

## Bookshops

- **Mall bookshops** - large bookshops in malls such as Sarit, Yaya Centre,
  The Junction and Two Rivers stock new fiction, non-fiction, children's
  books and school textbooks.
- **CBD bookshops** - long-established shops in the city centre, with a good
  range of African and Kenyan writing.
- **School textbooks** - in January, school bookshops and textbook sellers
  are crowded; buy early. See [Schools in Nairobi](/guides/schools-in-nairobi).

## Second-hand books

Booksellers spread second-hand books on the pavements of the CBD,
especially around **Moi Avenue** and **Tom Mboya Street** - novels, old
textbooks and magazines, at low prices, and negotiable. Some estates and
markets have second-hand stalls too.

## Kenyan writers to look for

Start with **Ngugi wa Thiong'o**, **Margaret Ogola**, **Grace Ogot**,
**Meja Mwangi**, **Yvonne Adhiambo Owuor** and **Binyavanga Wainaina**, and
the anthologies and magazines of new Kenyan writing. Bookshops can point you
to newer names.

## Book events

Look for book launches, readings and literary festivals at the libraries,
cultural centres and bookshops - they are usually free or cheap.

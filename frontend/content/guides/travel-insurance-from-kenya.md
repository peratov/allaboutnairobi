Kenyan insurers and banks sell travel insurance for trips abroad, and for some
visas it is not optional. This is general information, not advice.

## When a visa requires it

- **Schengen visas** need travel medical insurance covering the whole
  Schengen area for the whole stay, with at least
  **EUR {{ SCHENGEN_MIN_MEDICAL_COVER_EUR|shillings }}** of medical cover,
  including emergency treatment and repatriation. Embassies check the
  certificate, so buy it before the appointment and match the dates.
- Several other countries ask for it, or check it at the border. Read the
  embassy's own requirements, not an agent's summary.

## What a policy covers

- **Emergency medical treatment** abroad, and evacuation or repatriation -
  the part that matters most, since a hospital stay in Europe or the US can
  cost more than a car.
- **Trip cancellation or cutting short**, for reasons the policy lists.
- **Lost or delayed baggage** and documents.
- **Personal liability**.

Your Kenyan medical cover, and [SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha), generally do
not pay for treatment abroad. Some international health plans do; check before
buying a second policy.

## What to check

- **Pre-existing conditions** - usually excluded unless declared and accepted.
- **Activities** - skiing, diving or motorbike hire may need an add-on.
- **Age limits and price bands**, which rise steeply for older travellers.
- **The emergency number** - put it in your phone and on paper.
- **Single trip or annual** - frequent travellers often pay less for an annual
  multi-trip policy.

## Visitors to Kenya

Visitors are not covered by SHA. Buy travel insurance at home that covers
Kenya, including **air evacuation** - if you go on safari, an evacuation
membership such as AMREF Flying Doctors is also common. See
[Healthcare in Nairobi](/guides/healthcare-in-nairobi).

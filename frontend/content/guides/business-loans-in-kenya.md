Most small businesses in Nairobi start on savings, family money and the
[[chama]]. When you need more, there are several kinds of lender - each with
its own cost, paperwork and risk.

## Where to borrow

- **Banks** - SME loans, overdrafts and working-capital lines. Usually the
  cheapest formal credit, but they want records and often security.
- **SACCOs** - loans of a multiple of your savings, at lower rates than most
  lenders. Joining early and saving steadily is what unlocks them. See
  [SACCOs in Nairobi](/guides/saccos-in-nairobi).
- **Microfinance banks** - smaller loans, group lending, less paperwork.
- **Government funds** - the **Uwezo Fund**, the **Youth Enterprise
  Development Fund** and the **Women Enterprise Fund** lend to youth, women
  and groups, often through groups and at low cost.
- **The Hustler Fund** - small, short loans on your phone, including for
  groups. See [The Hustler Fund](/guides/hustler-fund).
- **Asset finance** - a loan to buy equipment or a vehicle, secured on the
  thing you buy.
- **Invoice and LPO financing** - an advance against an invoice or a local
  purchase order from a reliable buyer, such as a large company or
  government.
- **Mobile loans** - fast but expensive; fine for a few days' stock, costly
  for anything longer. See [The true cost of mobile loans](/guides/mobile-loans-true-cost).

## What lenders ask for

- **Registration** - a business name or company. See
  [Registering a business in Kenya](/guides/registering-a-business-in-kenya).
- **KRA PIN** and often a **tax compliance certificate**. See
  [KRA tax compliance certificate](/guides/kra-tax-compliance-certificate).
- **Bank and [[M-Pesa]] statements** - usually six to twelve months. Run the
  business's money through one account or till to build a record.
- **Business permit** - see [Nairobi business permit](/guides/nairobi-business-permit).
- **Security** - a logbook, title deed, savings or guarantors.
- **A clean credit record** - see [Checking your CRB status](/guides/checking-your-crb-status).

## Before you borrow

- **Know what the money is for** and how it pays itself back - stock that
  sells in weeks, not a loan for rent.
- **Compare the total cost**, not the headline rate - fees, insurance and
  whether the rate is flat or reducing balance. Use the
  [loan calculator](/tools/loan-repayment-calculator).
- **Check the lender is licensed** - see
  [Checking a financial firm is licensed](/guides/checking-a-financial-firm-is-licensed-in-kenya).
- **Borrow less than you are offered** if the repayments squeeze your cash.

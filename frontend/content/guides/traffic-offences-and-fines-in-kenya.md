Traffic police checks are common on Nairobi's roads. Knowing your
obligations - and how fines are meant to be paid - makes a stop quicker and
protects you from paying anything you should not.

## If you are stopped

- Pull over safely, stay calm and be polite.
- Officers can ask to see your **driving licence**, and check your
  **insurance** and the car's condition.
- Ask what the **offence** is.
- Uniformed traffic officers have **service numbers** you can note.

## Instant fines for minor offences

Under the **Traffic (Minor Offences) Rules**, many minor offences can be
settled with a fixed **instant fine** instead of going to court. Fines run
from **KES {{ INSTANT_FINE_MIN|shillings }}** to
**KES {{ INSTANT_FINE_MAX|shillings }}** depending on the offence. NTSA
began rolling out a digital system for issuing and paying these fines in
2026, with notices sent by SMS. Court challenges and system delays have
affected the rollout, so check the current position on NTSA's website.

Typical minor offences include:

- not wearing a **seatbelt**, or a passenger not wearing one
- **speeding** a little over the limit
- driving without your **licence** on you
- using a **phone** while driving
- **obstruction** - stopping or parking where you should not
- defects such as a broken light or missing reflective triangles

## Paying a fine

- **Never pay cash to an officer.** Fines are paid through official
  channels, against a notice, and you get a receipt.
- You can choose to **go to court** instead of paying an instant fine, and
  argue your case there.
- If you are given a **notice to attend court**, go: missing it can lead
  to a warrant.

## Serious offences

Drink-driving, dangerous driving, driving without insurance and causing
injury are not minor offences. They go to court, with heavy fines,
possible imprisonment and suspension of your licence. Nairobi police run
**alcohol breathalyser checks**, especially at night and at weekends.

## Common reasons people get stopped

- No **seatbelt**, front or back.
- Out-of-date **insurance**.
- A **PSV** (matatu or taxi) without the right licence or badge.
- **Overlapping** traffic or driving on the shoulder.
- Using a phone at the wheel.

## Bribery

Asking for or paying a bribe is a crime. If an officer asks for money, ask
for the official notice and process instead. You can report misconduct to
the **National Police Service** or the **Independent Policing Oversight
Authority** (IPOA). See [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents#complaints-about-the-police).

## Speed cameras

NTSA and the police use speed guns and cameras on major roads. Speed limits
are generally 50 km/h in town and lower near schools, unless signs say
otherwise.

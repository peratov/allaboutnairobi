Many Nairobi businesses import stock from China, Dubai, India, Turkey and
elsewhere. Getting it through customs smoothly depends on the paperwork done
**before** the goods ship.

## The key documents

- **Import Declaration Form (IDF)** - lodged electronically before the goods
  arrive, usually by your clearing agent through the customs system.
- **Certificate of Conformity (CoC)** - from a **KEBS-approved Pre-Export
  Verification of Conformity (PVoC)** agent in the exporting country, for goods
  covered by Kenyan standards. It confirms the goods meet KEBS standards.
- **Commercial invoice and packing list** from the supplier.
- **Bill of lading** (sea) or **air waybill** (air).
- **Your [[KRA PIN]]**, and any sector permits (for example for food, medicines,
  chemicals or electronics).

Goods that should have a CoC but arrive without one are inspected and tested
at the port, at extra cost and delay - and may be refused.

## Duties and taxes

What you pay depends on the goods' **tariff classification**:

- **Import duty** - set by the East African Community Common External Tariff,
  commonly 0%, 10%, 25% or 35% depending on the product.
- **Excise duty** on some goods.
- **VAT** at {{ VAT_RATE|percent }}% on most goods.
- **Import Declaration Fee** at {{ CAR_IDF_RATE|percent }}% and the **Railway
  Development Levy** at {{ CAR_RDL_RATE|percent }}%, both on the customs value.

The **customs value** is usually the cost of the goods plus freight and
insurance to Kenya. KRA can challenge undervalued invoices.

## Clearing agents

Almost all importers use a **licensed clearing and forwarding agent**. They
lodge the IDF, pay duties on your behalf, arrange inspection and release, and
deliver the goods. Check the agent's licence, agree fees in writing, and ask
for KRA's own payment receipts.

## Consolidated shipping

Small importers often use **consolidators** who combine many buyers' goods in
one container and handle clearance, charging by weight or volume. It is
convenient, but check what is included and that duties are properly paid in
your name - otherwise goods can be seized later.

## Common mistakes

- **Shipping before getting a CoC** for regulated goods.
- **Under-declaring value** - leads to penalties and seizures.
- **Wrong classification** - ask your agent before ordering.
- **Counterfeit brands** - seized by the Anti-Counterfeit Authority.
- **Restricted goods** without permits.

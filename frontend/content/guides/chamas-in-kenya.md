A **chama** is a group of people who pool money regularly - friends,
colleagues, a church group, women in an estate. Millions of Kenyans save and
invest this way. A well-run chama builds discipline and can buy land or
start businesses together; a badly run one ends friendships.

## The main types

- **Merry-go-round** - each member contributes a fixed amount every month,
  and one member takes the whole pot in turn.
- **Table banking** - contributions are pooled and lent to members at an
  agreed interest, with the interest shared or kept in the group.
- **Investment group** - members save to buy assets together: land,
  shares, Treasury bills, a money market fund, rental property or a business.
- Many chamas combine these, and some add a **welfare** fund for members'
  emergencies.

## Starting one

1. **Agree the purpose** - saving, lending, investing, or all three.
2. **Choose members carefully** - people you trust, with similar means and
   goals. Smaller groups are easier to manage.
3. **Write a constitution** covering:
   - contributions - how much, when, and the penalty for paying late
   - officials - chair, treasurer, secretary - and how they are elected
   - how money is kept and who can sign
   - how loans work, the interest and what happens if a member defaults
   - how decisions are made - meetings, quorum, voting
   - what happens when a member **leaves or dies**, and how they are paid out
   - how disputes are settled and how the group can be wound up
4. **Keep records** - minutes of meetings and a ledger of every
   contribution, loan and payout, shared with all members.

## Registering

Registration gives the group a legal identity, lets it open a bank account,
and helps it own assets.

- Most chamas register as a **self-help group** with the **Directorate of
  Social Development**, at sub-county offices and Huduma Centres. You will
  need the constitution, minutes electing officials, a member list with ID
  numbers and copies of IDs. Ask the office for the current fee and
  minimum number of members.
- Investment groups buying land or large assets may prefer to register as a
  **company** or **partnership**. See [Registering a limited company](/guides/registering-a-limited-company).
- A group that grows into taking deposits and lending widely may need to
  become a **SACCO**. See [SACCOs in Nairobi](/guides/saccos-in-nairobi).

## Keeping the money safe

- Open a **group bank account** with at least **two or three signatories**,
  so no one person can withdraw alone.
- Collect contributions to a **group paybill or account**, not an official's
  personal M-Pesa.
- Park money you are not lending in a **money market fund** group account to
  earn interest. See [Money market funds in Kenya](/guides/money-market-funds-in-kenya)
  and the [MMF comparer](/tools/mmf-comparer).
- Several apps help chamas record contributions and loans; they do not
  replace a proper bank account and signatories.

## Investing together

- Do proper checks on land: a search, a site visit and an advocate. See
  [Buying land around Nairobi](/guides/buying-land-around-nairobi).
- Register assets in the **group's name**, not an official's.
- Be wary of schemes that target chamas with promised high returns. See
  [Checking a financial firm is licensed](/guides/checking-a-financial-firm-is-licensed-in-kenya).

## Avoiding disputes

Most chama fights are about late contributions, unpaid loans, and members
leaving. Clear rules agreed in advance, written records, regular statements
and rotating officials prevent most of them.

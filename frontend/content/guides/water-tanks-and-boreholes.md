Mains water in much of Nairobi does not flow every day. The city's water
company rations supply by area, and some neighbourhoods get water only a few
days a week. How your building stores and sources water matters as much as the
rent.

## Rationing

**Nairobi City Water and Sewerage Company** publishes rationing schedules by
area, especially in dry seasons. Ask neighbours or the caretaker which days
water flows. See [Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi#water).

## Storage tanks

Most buildings store water in **tanks** on the roof or at ground level, filled
when the supply runs:

- **Plastic tanks** are the most common, in sizes from a few hundred to
  10,000 litres or more.
- **A pump** lifts water from a ground tank to the roof, or pressurises the
  supply - so power cuts can stop the water too.
- **Clean tanks** at least once a year, and keep lids closed.

A family of four uses very roughly 400 to 600 litres a day, so size storage to
cover the days without supply.

## Water bowsers

During long shortages, **water bowsers** (tankers) deliver water to fill tanks.
Use established suppliers: water from unknown sources may be unsafe. Prices
rise sharply in droughts.

## Boreholes

Many newer apartment blocks and houses in Karen, Runda and the outskirts have
their own **borehole**.

- **Permits** - drilling needs authorisation from the **Water Resources
  Authority (WRA)** and, where applicable, NEMA, then a **water abstraction
  permit** to use the water. Approval can take several months.
- **Cost** - a hydrogeological survey, then drilling charged per metre, plus a
  pump, tank and piping. Nairobi's volcanic rock makes it expensive; a
  complete borehole commonly costs well over a million shillings.
- **Quality** - Nairobi groundwater can be high in **fluoride**, which stains
  teeth and can affect bones. Test the water and treat it if needed.

## Drinking water

Don't drink tap or borehole water untreated. Boil it, use a good filter, or
buy bottled water or dispenser refills.

## Before you rent

Ask:

- **How many days a week** does mains water come?
- **How much storage** does the building have?
- **Is there a borehole**, and is the water treated?
- **Is the pump on backup power?**

## Harvesting rainwater

Houses can collect rainwater from the roof into tanks for gardens, washing and
toilets - useful in the rainy seasons.

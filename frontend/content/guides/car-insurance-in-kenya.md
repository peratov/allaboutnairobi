Every car on a Kenyan road must be insured for at least **third-party**
liability. Insurance is issued with a digital certificate you can check online
and that the police can verify on the road.

## Types of cover

- **Third party only (TPO)** - the legal minimum. Covers injury to other people
  and damage to their property. Does not cover your own car.
- **Third party, fire and theft (TPFT)** - adds cover if your car is stolen or
  burnt.
- **Comprehensive** - covers your own car too, for accidents, theft, fire and
  more. Needed if the car is financed, and sensible for any car you could not
  afford to lose.

## What it costs

- **Third party** cover for a private car typically costs somewhere around
  KES 5,000 to 15,000 a year.
- **Comprehensive** cover is priced as a **percentage of the car's value** -
  commonly around 3.5% to 7% a year. Many insurers apply a **minimum premium**,
  often around KES {{ CAR_INSURANCE_MIN_PREMIUM|shillings }}.
- **Levies** set by law are added to the premium.

The rate depends on the car, its value, your age and driving record, where
you park it, and the insurer.

## Add-ons worth knowing

- **Excess protector** - removes or reduces the **excess** (the part of each
  claim you pay yourself). Often worth it in Nairobi traffic.
- **Political violence and terrorism (PVT)** - covers damage during riots and
  protests, which standard policies usually exclude.
- **Courtesy car** - a hire car while yours is repaired.
- **Windscreen and radio** cover, often with separate limits.
- **Tracking** - many insurers require a tracker for valuable cars and may give
  a discount for one.

## Buying cover

- **Through a licensed broker** - compares insurers and helps with claims.
- **Directly** from an insurer or its app.
- **Through your bank**, if the car is financed.

Check that the insurer is licensed by the **Insurance Regulatory Authority**,
and verify the certificate online.

## Valuation

For comprehensive cover, the car is insured at its **current market value**,
set by a valuation. Keep the value up to date: insuring for too much costs more
without paying out more, and too little means a lower payout.

## Making a claim

1. **At the scene** - stay safe, take photos, swap details, and don't admit
   fault.
2. **Report to the police** and get an abstract.
3. **Notify the insurer** as soon as possible, within the policy's deadline.
4. **Assessment** - the insurer's assessor inspects the car and approves the
   repair, usually at an approved garage.

## After buying a used car

The previous owner's policy does not pass to you. Insure it in your name
before driving it away. See [Transferring a logbook](/guides/transferring-a-car-logbook).

Nairobi has a large freelance workforce - designers, developers, writers,
consultants and people working online for clients abroad. Working for yourself
means handling the paperwork an employer would otherwise do.

## Get set up

- **A [[KRA PIN]]** - clients will ask for it before paying you.
- **A business name**, if you want to trade under a name other than your own.
  It costs KES {{ BUSINESS_NAME_FEE|shillings }} through the Business
  Registration Service. See [Registering a business](/guides/registering-a-business-in-kenya).
- **eTIMS.** Invoices must be issued through KRA's electronic tax invoice
  system. There is a free app for small businesses.
- **A separate bank account and M-Pesa till or paybill**, so business money is
  easy to track.

## Kenyan clients and withholding tax

Kenyan companies usually deduct **withholding tax** from professional and
consultancy fees - {{ WHT_PROFESSIONAL_RESIDENT|percent }}% for residents - and
pay it to KRA for you. Collect every withholding certificate: on your annual
return it counts towards the tax you owe. Work out what you will receive with
the [withholding tax calculator](/tools/withholding-tax-calculator).

## Clients abroad

- **Getting paid.** Common routes include payment platforms linked to a Kenyan
  bank account or M-Pesa, international bank transfers, and money transfer
  services. Compare fees and exchange rates - see
  [Opening a bank account](/guides/opening-a-bank-account#sending-money-into-and-out-of-kenya).
- **Tax.** Income from foreign clients is still taxable in Kenya if you are
  resident here. Declare it on your annual return.

## Tax

You file an **annual income tax return** on [[iTax]] by
{{ TAX_RETURN_DEADLINE }}, declaring your freelance income and claiming the
withholding tax already deducted. Keep records of income and expenses, which
reduce your taxable profit. Small businesses may be able to use turnover tax
instead - ask an accountant, as some professional services are excluded.
VAT registration is compulsory once turnover passes KES
{{ VAT_REGISTRATION_THRESHOLD|shillings }} a year.

## Your own safety net

No employer means no automatic pension or health cover:

- **SHIF.** Register with [[SHA]] as a self-employed member and contribute, so
  you and your family are covered.
- **NSSF.** Self-employed people can make voluntary contributions.
- **Savings.** Keep a buffer for slow months - see
  [Saving and investing](/guides/saving-and-investing-in-kenya).

## Contracts and getting paid on time

Agree scope, price, deadlines and payment terms in writing before you start.
Ask for a deposit on larger jobs, invoice promptly, and follow up in writing.

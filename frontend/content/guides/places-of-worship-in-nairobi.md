Faith is central to life for most Kenyans, and Nairobi has places of worship
for almost every tradition - from cathedrals and megachurches to mosques,
temples and small fellowships meeting in classrooms and tents.

## Kenya's faiths

According to the 2019 census, most Kenyans are **Christian** - Protestant,
Catholic, evangelical and independent African churches - and about one in
ten is **Muslim**. Smaller communities follow **Hinduism**, **Sikhism**,
**Judaism**, the **Baha'i** faith and traditional religions.

## Landmark places of worship

- **[All Saints Cathedral](/map#place/all-saints-cathedral)** - the Anglican
  cathedral next to Uhuru Park, a stone building from the colonial era.
- **[Holy Family Basilica](/map#place/holy-family-basilica)** - the Catholic
  cathedral in the CBD, near City Hall.
- **[Jamia Mosque](/map#place/jamia-mosque)** - Nairobi's main mosque, with
  silver domes and minarets, in the heart of the CBD.
- **Hindu temples**, including large Swaminarayan temples, and **Sikh
  gurdwaras**, mainly in Parklands, Ngara and the CBD.
- **The Nairobi Hebrew Congregation** synagogue, one of the oldest buildings
  of its kind in East Africa.

## Finding a congregation

- **Churches** - services in English, Kiswahili and other languages, at
  different times on Sunday. Many large churches stream services and run
  midweek fellowships, youth and children's programmes.
- **International congregations** serve expatriate communities in English,
  French, Korean, Chinese and other languages.
- **Mosques** across the city hold Friday prayers and daily prayers.
- Ask colleagues, neighbours and the community groups linked to your home
  country.

## Visiting etiquette

- **Dress modestly** - covered shoulders and knees; at mosques, women cover
  their hair, and everyone removes shoes. Hindu and Sikh temples ask you to
  remove shoes and cover your head.
- **Ask before taking photos**, especially during services.
- **Visitors are usually welcome** - many churches introduce and welcome
  newcomers during the service.
- **Offerings** are taken in most churches, often by M-Pesa as well as cash;
  giving is voluntary.

## Faith in daily life

- Christian and Muslim holidays are **public holidays**. See
  [Public holidays in Kenya](/guides/public-holidays-in-kenya).
- Weddings, funerals and fundraisers are often church-centred. See

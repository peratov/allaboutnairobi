Rubbish collection in Nairobi is uneven: some estates have a reliable weekly
pickup, others rely on informal collectors or none at all. This is how it
works and what the law expects of you.

## Who collects it

- **In most residential areas**, a private collector licensed by Nairobi
  County collects household rubbish, usually once or twice a week, for a
  monthly fee.
- **In apartments**, collection is often arranged by the landlord or
  management and included in the **service charge**. Ask before you move in.
- **In a house**, you may need to sign up with a collector yourself. Ask the
  neighbours who they use.
- Much of the city's waste ends up at the **Dandora** dumpsite, east of the
  city.

Check your collector is licensed, and do not pay someone who dumps waste
by the road - you can be held responsible.

## Sorting your rubbish

The **Sustainable Waste Management Act 2022** requires waste to be
**separated at source**, so households and businesses must keep at least:

- **organic waste** - food scraps, peelings, garden waste
- **non-organic waste** - plastic, paper, glass, metal

Collectors are meant to keep the streams apart. Not separating waste can be
punished with a fine, though enforcement varies. Separating also makes
recycling and composting possible.

## Plastic bags

Kenya banned plastic carrier bags in **2017**, one of the strictest bans in
the world. Shops use paper, woven or reusable bags. Carry your own. Since
2020, single-use plastics are also banned in national parks, beaches and
forests.

## Recycling

- **Plastic bottles, paper, cardboard, glass and metal** have value. Many
  collectors and private recycling companies take them, sometimes for free.
- Some supermarkets and malls have **drop-off points** for bottles and
  packaging.
- Informal waste pickers collect recyclables across the city; separating them
  helps.

## Composting

Food and garden waste make up much of household rubbish. If you have a
garden, a simple compost heap or bin turns it into fertiliser and cuts what
you throw away. See [Setting up your home](/guides/setting-up-your-home).

## Bulky items and e-waste

- **Old furniture and appliances** - sell or give them away, or ask your
  collector, who may charge extra.
- **Electronics, batteries and bulbs** contain hazardous materials and should
  not go in household rubbish. Use a licensed e-waste recycler; some phone
  shops and brands take old devices back.
- **Medicines** - return unused medicines to a pharmacy rather than throwing
  them away.

## Reporting illegal dumping

Report illegal dumping or uncollected waste to **Nairobi County**, and
serious pollution to the **National Environment Management Authority**
(NEMA). See [Nairobi county services](/guides/nairobi-county-services).

Few Nairobi tenants insure what they own, and most only think of it after a
break-in. Cover is cheaper than people expect, and a **domestic package**
from a Kenyan insurer bundles most of what a household needs. This is general
information, not advice.

## Buildings or contents?

- **Buildings cover** insures the structure - walls, roof, fixed fittings,
  the perimeter wall and gate. If you own the house, you buy it; if you have a
  mortgage, the bank requires it and often arranges it. Tenants do not need
  it: the landlord insures the building.
- **Contents cover** insures what is inside - furniture, appliances,
  clothes, electronics. This is the one tenants need.

## The domestic package

Most insurers sell contents cover as a **domestic package** with several
sections you can switch on:

- **Contents** against fire, burglary, storm, flood and similar events.
- **All risks** for portable items - phones, laptops, cameras, jewellery -
  that are covered away from home too. Each item is usually listed with its
  value.
- **Owner's or occupier's liability**, if someone is hurt at your home.
- **Domestic workers' cover** under the Work Injury Benefits Act - see
  [Work injury insurance](/guides/work-injury-insurance-wiba). If you employ a
  house manager, gardener or guard, this is the part that is a legal duty.

## What it costs

The premium is a percentage of the sum insured, set by each insurer and
usually higher for all-risks items than for contents kept at home. Ask two or
three insurers or a broker for quotes on the same list of items: the
difference between them is often bigger than the difference between levels of
cover.

## Getting the value right

- **Insure for replacement value.** If you insure your contents for half
  what they are worth, most policies apply **average**: the insurer pays only
  half of any claim, because you paid for half the cover.
- **Make a list** of the main items with their value, and keep receipts and
  photos somewhere off the phone.
- **Serial numbers** for phones and laptops make police reports and claims far
  easier.

## What is usually excluded

- Theft **without forced entry**, unless the policy says otherwise - which
  matters when a visitor or worker is involved.
- Items left in an **unattended car**.
- **Wear and tear**, breakdowns and power surges, unless added.
- A house left **empty** for more than a set number of days.
- Cash, beyond a small limit.

Read the exclusions before you pay; they are where most refused claims come
from.

## After a burglary

1. **Report it to the police** at once and get an **OB number** - no insurer
   pays without one. See [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).
2. **Tell the insurer** within the time the policy sets, often a few days.
3. Send the **claim form, OB number, receipts or proof of ownership**, and
   photos of the damage.
4. Expect an **assessor** for larger claims.

## Security discounts

Insurers often ask about grilles, a guard, an alarm or a gated compound, and
some give a better rate for them. See

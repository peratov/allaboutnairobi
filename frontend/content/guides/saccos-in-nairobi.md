**SACCOs** (savings and credit co-operative societies) are one of the main
ways Kenyans save and borrow. Members save regularly, and can borrow several
times their savings at rates usually well below mobile and many bank loans.

## How they work

- **You join** by buying shares (a one-off amount) and paying a registration
  fee.
- **You save every month** into **deposits**. Many SACCOs take contributions
  through your employer's payroll.
- **You borrow** - commonly up to **three times** your deposits, after a
  minimum membership period, with other members as guarantors.
- **You earn** a yearly **interest on deposits** and a **dividend on
  shares**, decided at the annual general meeting.

## Types of SACCO

- **Employer and profession-based** - for teachers, police, civil servants,
  bank staff, doctors and others. Many large Nairobi SACCOs started this way,
  and some have opened to the public.
- **Community and open SACCOs** - anyone can join.
- **Deposit-taking SACCOs** offer bank-like savings accounts too. They must be
  licensed by **SASRA** (the SACCO Societies Regulatory Authority).

## Choosing a safe SACCO

- **Check SASRA's list** of licensed deposit-taking SACCOs.
- **Look at the history** of dividends and interest paid.
- **Read the annual accounts** - SACCOs publish them to members.
- **Ask how quickly loans are processed** and how easy it is to withdraw if you
  leave. Deposits often cannot be withdrawn while you remain a member, and
  leaving can require notice of several weeks.
- **Beware of "SACCOs" promising very high returns** - pyramid schemes have
  used the name.

## Guaranteeing

Loans need guarantors. If you guarantee a loan and the borrower does not repay,
**your deposits can be used to recover it**. Guarantee only people you trust,
for amounts you could afford to lose.

Domestic violence is a crime in Kenya, and the law protects anyone abused by
a partner, spouse, relative or someone they live with. Abuse can be
physical, sexual, emotional or financial. Help is available - and it is not
your fault.

## If you are in danger now

- Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** or go to the nearest police station.
- Call the national **gender-based violence helpline on 1195** - free and
  open around the clock.
- For a child at risk, call **Childline on 116**.
- Go somewhere safe - a friend, relative, neighbour, church or shelter.

## After an assault

Go to a hospital or **gender-based violence recovery centre** as soon as
possible, ideally within **72 hours**, for treatment, emergency
contraception and HIV prevention, and a medical examination that records
evidence. See
[HIV testing and sexual health](/guides/hiv-testing-and-sexual-health-in-nairobi).

## Reporting to the police

- Report at a police station; many have **gender desks**.
- You will get an **OB number**. Keep it.
- The **P3 form**, filled in by a doctor, records injuries for the case.

See [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).

## Protection orders

Under the **Protection Against Domestic Violence Act 2015**, you can ask the
**magistrate's court** for a **protection order** against the abuser:

- The order can stop them from **contacting, threatening or harming** you,
  coming to your home or workplace, or damaging your property.
- The court can grant an **interim order** quickly, even before the abuser
  is heard, if you are at risk.
- Someone else - a relative, neighbour, or an organisation - can apply on
  your behalf, or for a child.
- Breaking a protection order is a **criminal offence**.

You do not need a lawyer to apply, but free help makes it easier. See
[Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).

## Planning for safety

- Keep **copies of ID**, birth certificates, money and a phone in a safe
  place or with someone you trust.
- Agree a **code word** with friends to signal you need help.
- Save evidence - messages, photos, medical records.
- Think about financial independence - an account in your own name. See
  [Opening a bank account](/guides/opening-a-bank-account).

## Support

- **Shelters** and safe houses in Nairobi, run by charities, offer
  emergency accommodation; the 1195 helpline and police can refer you.
- **Counselling** - see [Therapy and mental health](/guides/therapy-and-mental-health-in-nairobi).
- **Legal help** for divorce, custody and property - see
  [Divorce in Kenya](/guides/divorce-in-kenya) and
  [Child custody and maintenance](/guides/child-custody-and-maintenance-in-kenya).

## Helping someone else

Listen without judging, let them decide, help them plan, and keep the
helpline number handy. Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** if they are in
immediate danger.

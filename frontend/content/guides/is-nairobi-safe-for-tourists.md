Millions of people visit Nairobi every year without any trouble, and the
city's hotels, parks and restaurants are used to visitors. Nairobi does have
real crime, though, and a visitor who knows the risks and follows a few habits
is far less likely to have problems.

## The real risks

- **Petty theft** - phone snatching and pickpocketing, especially in the CBD
  and crowded places. This is the most likely problem.
- **Car break-ins and smash-and-grab** in slow traffic.
- **Scams** aimed at visitors.
- **Road accidents** - statistically the biggest danger.
- **Occasional protests**, which can turn violent.

Violent crime against tourists is much less common, and mostly happens at
night on foot, in quiet areas.

## Where and when to be careful

- **The CBD** is busy and fine by day with your wits about you; avoid walking
  there after dark.
- **Walking at night** anywhere is not advised - use ride-hailing even for short
  distances.
- **Westlands and Kilimani** are busy and generally safe, but watch your phone
  around bars at night.
- **Informal settlements** such as Kibera are best visited with a
  community-run tour, not alone.

## Simple habits

- **Keep your phone out of sight** on the street and near car or matatu
  windows.
- **Use Uber, Bolt or Little** at night, and check the car and driver match.
- **Keep car windows up** and bags out of sight in traffic.
- **Use ATMs inside malls or banks.**
- **Carry a copy of your passport**, and leave the original in the hotel safe.
- **Don't flash** expensive jewellery, cameras or large amounts of cash.

## Common scams

- **"I know you from your hotel"** - a friendly stranger leading to a sad story
  or a request for money.
- **Fake tour sellers** on the street or at the airport. Book with an
  established company. See
  [Choosing a safari company](/guides/choosing-a-safari-company).
- **Fake officials** demanding on-the-spot fines. Ask for identification and
  an official receipt.

See [Staying safe in Nairobi](/guides/safety-in-nairobi#scams) for more.

## Safaris and parks

Safaris with licensed operators are very safe. Follow your guide's
instructions, stay in the vehicle except where allowed, and never approach or
feed wild animals. See [Nairobi National Park](/guides/nairobi-national-park).

## Health

- **Yellow fever** vaccination may be required, depending on where you travel
  from.
- **Malaria** is not a significant risk in Nairobi itself, which is high and
  cool, but is present in many other parts of Kenya, including the coast and
  some parks. Ask a travel clinic about prevention.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water.**
- **Get travel insurance** that includes medical evacuation. See
  [Hospitals in Nairobi](/guides/hospitals-in-nairobi).

## Women and solo travellers

Many women travel alone in Nairobi without problems. The same habits apply:
avoid walking alone at night, use ride-hailing, and keep an eye on your drink.

Millions of Kenyans live abroad and keep close ties with home: supporting
family, building, investing and, one day, coming back. Doing it from a
distance brings particular risks.

## Sending money home

- **Money transfer services** and apps are usually cheaper and faster than bank
  transfers, and many pay straight into M-Pesa or a Kenyan bank account.
- **Compare the total cost** - the fee plus the exchange rate against the
  mid-market rate.
- **M-Pesa Global** lets you send from some countries directly to M-Pesa.

See [Opening a bank account](/guides/opening-a-bank-account#sending-money-into-and-out-of-kenya).

## Building or buying from abroad

Property fraud against people abroad is common, and the losses are often
life savings. Family members and agents acting in good faith can still be
deceived.

- **Instruct your own advocate** in Kenya, not one recommended by the seller.
- **Search the title yourself** on Ardhisasa, which can be done online.
- **Pay only into accounts named in the sale agreement**, never to an
  individual.
- **For building projects**, pay a contractor in stages against progress that
  someone independent has checked, with photos and receipts.
- **Use a power of attorney** only for a specific purpose and period, and
  register it.

See [Buying property in Kenya](/guides/buying-property-in-kenya).

## Passports and IDs

- **Passports** are applied for through [[eCitizen]] and processed through
  Kenyan embassies and high commissions abroad, which also handle renewals.
- **National ID and Maisha Card** services for citizens abroad are offered
  through some missions; check with yours.
- **Keep your eCitizen and KRA accounts active**, with a phone number and email
  you can reach from abroad.

## Tax

If you are not resident in Kenya, you are taxed in Kenya only on income from
Kenya, such as rent from a Kenyan property. From July 2026 non-resident
landlords fall under a separate rental income tax regime. If you have a KRA PIN
you may still need to file returns - check your obligations on [[iTax]].

## Saving and investing at home

Many Kenyans abroad invest in Kenyan money market funds, government bonds and
SACCOs. See [Saving and investing in Kenya](/guides/saving-and-investing-in-kenya).

## Coming back

- **Plan the move** - a few months' living costs, and a job or income lined up.
- **Your belongings** - returning residents may be able to import household
  goods and sometimes a car with duty relief; ask KRA before shipping.
- **Everything else** - see [Moving to Nairobi](/guides/moving-to-nairobi) and
  [Your first weeks](/guides/your-first-weeks-in-nairobi).

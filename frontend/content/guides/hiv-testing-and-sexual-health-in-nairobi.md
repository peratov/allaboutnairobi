Sexual health services in Nairobi are widely available, confidential, and
often **free** at public health facilities. Knowing where to go - and that
some services are time-critical - matters.

## HIV testing

- **Free HIV testing** is offered at public hospitals and health centres,
  and at many private clinics and NGO drop-in centres.
- **Self-test kits** are sold in pharmacies, so you can test privately at
  home. A positive self-test must be **confirmed** at a health facility.
- Testing is **voluntary and confidential**. You are entitled to counselling
  before and after.

## Preventing HIV

- **PrEP** (pre-exposure prophylaxis) is medicine HIV-negative people take
  to prevent infection. Oral PrEP is **free at public health facilities** and
  drop-in centres.
- In 2026 Kenya also began rolling out a **six-monthly PrEP injection** at
  selected public facilities.
- **Condoms** are free at many public facilities and sold everywhere.

## After a possible exposure: PEP

**PEP** (post-exposure prophylaxis) can prevent HIV after sex without a
condom, a condom breaking, sexual assault, or a needle injury. It must be
started **as soon as possible, and within 72 hours**. Go to a hospital
emergency department or health facility straight away and ask for PEP.

## Living with HIV

**Treatment** (antiretroviral therapy) is free at public facilities through
their comprehensive care clinics. With treatment taken daily, people with HIV
live long, healthy lives, and someone whose virus is undetectable does not
pass HIV on through sex. Discrimination because of HIV status is against the
law.

## STIs

Sexually transmitted infections - such as gonorrhoea, chlamydia and syphilis
- are common and usually easy to treat. Get tested and treated at a health
facility rather than buying antibiotics over the counter; see
[Pharmacies and medicines](/guides/pharmacies-and-medicines-in-nairobi).

## Contraception

- Public facilities offer **family planning** services - pills, injections,
  implants, coils and condoms - many free or at low cost.
- **Emergency contraception** is sold in pharmacies and works best the
  sooner it is taken after sex.
- Private clinics and NGOs also offer family planning.

## After sexual violence

- Go to a hospital or **gender-based violence recovery centre** as soon as
  possible - ideally within 72 hours - for **PEP**, emergency contraception,
  treatment and a medical examination, which also records evidence.
- Call the national **gender-based violence helpline on 1195**, which is
  free.
- Report to the police when you are ready; the hospital's examination
  form supports the case. See [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).

Nairobi is high - about 1,700 metres above sea level - which keeps malaria
risk low in the city. Travel around Kenya is a different matter. This is
general information: see a doctor or travel clinic for advice for you.

## Yellow fever

- **Arriving in Kenya:** you need a **yellow fever vaccination certificate**
  if you are aged one or over and arrive from, or have transited through, a
  country with a risk of yellow fever. Arriving directly from a country
  without that risk, you generally do not. Your airline and the
  [JKIA airport guide](/guides/jkia-airport-guide) cover what is checked.
- **Leaving Kenya:** Kenya itself is on the World Health Organization's list
  of countries with yellow fever risk, so some countries you travel on to
  will ask for the certificate. Check before you book.
- **Validity:** one dose now gives **lifelong** protection, and certificates
  are valid for life. The vaccination must be at least ten days before travel.
- Keep the original yellow certificate with your passport.

## Malaria

- **Nairobi:** the risk is low, though not zero.
- **Elsewhere:** the risk is higher at lower altitudes, especially on the
  **coast** (Mombasa, Diani, Malindi, Lamu), around **Lake Victoria** and
  in **western Kenya**. Some safari areas also carry a risk.
- **Prevention:** sleep under a treated net, use repellent, wear long
  sleeves in the evening, and ask a doctor whether you need antimalarial
  tablets for your trip.
- **Symptoms:** fever, chills, headache or flu-like illness up to several
  weeks after visiting a malaria area needs a **malaria test the same day**.
  Any clinic or hospital in Nairobi can do it quickly.

## Other vaccinations to consider

Ask a doctor or travel clinic about:

- **routine vaccines** - tetanus, measles and the rest of the usual schedule
- **hepatitis A** and **typhoid** - spread through food and water
- **hepatitis B**
- **rabies** - for long stays, work with animals, or travel far from medical
  care
- **cholera** - sometimes advised during outbreaks

## Children

Kenya's routine childhood vaccinations are given **free** at public health
facilities, and at private hospitals for a fee. Keep the child's
vaccination booklet: schools ask for it. See [Nairobi with kids](/guides/nairobi-with-kids)
and [Giving birth in Nairobi](/guides/giving-birth-in-nairobi).

## Where to get vaccinated in Nairobi

- **Travel clinics** at the large private hospitals give travel vaccines and
  advice.
- **Yellow fever** vaccination is given at designated centres, which issue
  the international certificate.
- **Public health facilities** give routine vaccines.

Wrong charges, a frozen account, a failed transfer or a loan you did not
take: most problems with a bank are sorted out by a clear written complaint.
If the bank does not fix it, the Central Bank of Kenya can step in.

## 1. Complain to the bank in writing

Phone calls and branch visits help, but a written complaint creates a record.
Email the bank's customer care address, use its complaint form, or hand in a
letter and ask for a stamped copy. Include:

- your name, account number and phone number
- what happened, with **dates, amounts and transaction references**
- what you want: a refund, a correction, an explanation
- copies of statements, receipts or messages

Ask for a **reference number**. The industry's Kenya Banking Sector Charter
sets a benchmark of a written acknowledgement within 48 hours and a resolution
within seven working days, with regular updates if it takes longer.

## 2. Escalate inside the bank

If the first reply does not resolve it, write to the bank's complaints or
customer experience manager, quoting your reference number. Most banks name
this contact in their complaints policy on their website.

## 3. Write to the Central Bank of Kenya

If the bank's final answer does not satisfy you, you can complain to the
**Central Bank of Kenya**. Send it:

- a short summary of the complaint
- copies of all your correspondence with the bank
- what you want to happen

The CBK hears from both sides and tries to reach a settlement. It is not a
court: it cannot award damages, but banks take its involvement seriously.

## Other firms the CBK covers

The same route - firm first, then the CBK - applies to:

- **microfinance banks**
- **digital credit providers** (loan apps)
- **mobile money and payment services**, including M-Pesa
- **forex bureaus** and money transfer firms

For M-Pesa errors and fraud, start with Safaricom: see
[M-Pesa fraud and scams](/guides/mpesa-fraud-and-scams-in-kenya).

## Other regulators

| Complaint about | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| An insurer or insurance broker | The Insurance Regulatory Authority - see [Insurance claims and complaints](/guides/insurance-claims-and-complaints-in-kenya) |
| A money market fund or stockbroker | The Capital Markets Authority |
| A deposit-taking SACCO | SASRA |
| A pension scheme | The Retirement Benefits Authority |
| A credit listing you think is wrong | The credit bureau first - see [Checking your CRB status](/guides/checking-your-crb-status) |
| Misuse of your personal data | The Data Commissioner - see [Your data protection rights](/guides/your-data-protection-rights-in-kenya) |

## If you lose money in a failed bank

Deposits in a licensed bank are protected by the **Kenya Deposit Insurance
Corporation** up to its limit if the bank collapses.

## Going to court

For claims up to KES {{ SMALL_CLAIMS_LIMIT|shillings }}, the **Small Claims
Court** is quicker and cheaper than the ordinary courts, and you can present
the case yourself. For larger or more complicated disputes, get advice from an
advocate.

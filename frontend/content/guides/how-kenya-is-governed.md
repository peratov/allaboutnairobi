Knowing who is responsible for what makes it much easier to get things done,
and to understand the news. Kenya's system is set out in the **2010
Constitution**.

## Two levels of government

- **The national government** handles things such as defence, foreign affairs,
  immigration, national roads, tax, police, universities and most of the
  national agencies this site mentions - KRA, NTSA, NSSF and SHA.
- **County governments** - there are 47 - handle local services such as health
  facilities, county roads, markets, business permits, parking, rubbish
  collection and pre-primary education.

## The national government

- **The President** is head of state and government, elected every five years
  with a Deputy President, and appoints the Cabinet Secretaries who run
  ministries.
- **Parliament** has two houses: the **National Assembly**, with members
  elected from the 290 constituencies plus women representatives from each
  county, and the **Senate**, which represents the counties.
- **The Judiciary** is independent, with the **Supreme Court** at the top. The
  Employment and Labour Relations Court and the Environment and Land Court
  handle those specialist disputes.

## Nairobi City County

Nairobi is both the capital and one of the 47 counties. It has:

- **A Governor**, elected every five years, who runs county services.
- **A County Assembly** of Members of County Assembly (**MCAs**), one per ward.
- **17 constituencies**, each with a Member of Parliament - see the
  [map](/map).

County services - parking, business permits, land rates and markets - are
paid for mostly through [[eCitizen]] and the county's own systems.

## Elections

General elections are held every five years in August, when voters choose the
President, governors, senators, MPs, women representatives and MCAs on the
same day. The next is due in **August 2027**. Elections are run by the
**Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission (IEBC)**. Only citizens can
vote.

Election periods can be tense. Follow official news, avoid rallies and protests,
and keep some cash and supplies at home in the days around polling. See
[Staying safe](/guides/safety-in-nairobi#protests).

## Independent offices

Several bodies check the government, including the **Auditor-General**, the
**Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC)**, the **Kenya National
Commission on Human Rights** and the **Independent Policing Oversight
Authority (IPOA)**.

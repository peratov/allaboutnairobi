Nairobi's traffic is the thing everyone talks about, and with reason. Rush
hour runs from about 6:30 to 9:30 in the morning and 4:30 to 8 in the evening,
and rain makes everything slower. Plan around it.

## Matatus

[[Matatu]]s are privately run minibuses and buses on fixed routes, and they
carry most of the city. Routes are numbered, and the destination is painted on
or shown in the windscreen.

- **Where to board:** at a [[stage]]. The big terminals are in the CBD, around
  Tom Mboya Street, Ronald Ngala Street and the railway station, with stages
  along every main road.
- **Paying:** cash or M-Pesa to the conductor. Many matatus now take M-Pesa
  only. Fares depend on distance, time of day and weather; within the city
  they are usually under KES 100.
- **Finding the route:** ask the conductor, or someone at the stage. Apps and
  Google Maps cover the main routes, but asking is faster.
- **Safety:** matatus are fast and crowded. Keep your phone out of sight near
  the window. Some saccos run calmer, newer buses on the same routes.

## Ride-hailing

**Uber**, **Bolt** and the Kenyan app **Little** all operate across Nairobi,
with prices shown before you book. They are the easiest option for newcomers
and at night. Share your trip with a friend, check the plate before getting in,
and sit in the back.

## Boda bodas

A [[boda boda]] (motorbike taxi) weaves through traffic that a car cannot, and
for short trips it is often the fastest option. It is also the riskiest. If you
use one:

- Wear the helmet, and ask for one if none is offered.
- Book through Uber, Bolt or Little where possible, so the ride is traceable.
- Hold on, keep bags light, and avoid boda bodas at night and in heavy rain.

## Trains

- **Nairobi Commuter Rail** - Kenya Railways runs cheap commuter trains from
  the central station to suburbs including Syokimau, Embakasi Village, Ruiru
  and Kikuyu. Services are concentrated at rush hour, so check the timetable.
- **The SGR (Madaraka Express)** - to Mombasa from the Nairobi Terminus near
  Syokimau, in around five to six hours depending on the service. Book on
  Kenya Railways' ticketing site and bring the ID you booked with. It sells out
  at holidays.

## The Expressway

The Nairobi Expressway is an elevated toll road from Mlolongo, past JKIA and
the CBD, to James Gichuru Road in Westlands. It turns a two-hour rush-hour
drive to the airport into half an hour. Tolls depend on the distance and are
paid by card, by M-Pesa or with a prepaid tag. Ride-hailing drivers will ask
before using it, because you pay the toll.

## Walking and cycling

The CBD, Westlands and Kilimani are walkable in daylight, and pavements are
improving. Crossing the big roads is the hard part: use footbridges and
signalled crossings. Cycling is growing, but traffic and potholes make it best
for confident riders or for Karura Forest's trails.

Mental health support in Nairobi has grown quickly, with private counsellors,
psychologists and psychiatrists, hospital mental health units, and online
services. Asking for help is increasingly common.

## Who does what

- **Counsellors** - talking therapy for stress, grief, relationships and
  life changes. Must be registered with the **Counsellors and
  Psychologists Board**.
- **Clinical psychologists** - assessment and therapy for conditions such as
  depression, anxiety and trauma.
- **Psychiatrists** - medical doctors who diagnose, prescribe medication and
  treat more severe conditions. Registered with the **Kenya Medical
  Practitioners and Dentists Council**.
- **Your GP** - a good first stop, who can refer you.

## Where to find help

- **Private practices** across Westlands, Kilimani, Upper Hill and Karen, many
  offering online sessions.
- **Hospitals** - the large private hospitals have psychiatry and psychology
  departments, and some have inpatient units.
- **Kenyatta National Hospital** and **Mathari National Teaching and Referral
  Hospital** - public options.
- **Employee assistance programmes** - many employers offer free, confidential
  counselling sessions. Ask HR.
- **Universities** run counselling services for students.

## What it costs

Private sessions vary widely with the practitioner's qualifications and
experience. Ask about fees before booking, and whether they offer a sliding
scale. Community organisations and some NGOs offer free or low-cost
counselling.

## Insurance and SHA

- **Private health insurance** often covers psychiatric care and sometimes
  therapy, within limits. Check your policy's outpatient cover. See
  [Private health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- **SHA** covers some mental health services at contracted facilities. Ask the
  facility.

## Choosing a therapist

- **Check registration** with the relevant board or council.
- **Ask about their approach** and experience with your concern.
- **Try a first session** - feeling comfortable with the person matters.
- **Language and culture** - many therapists work in English and Kiswahili, and
  some specialise in expatriates or particular communities.

Banks, loan apps, employers, schools, landlords, hospitals and shops all
collect personal data about you. The **Data Protection Act 2019** sets rules
for how they use it, and the **Office of the Data Protection Commissioner**
(ODPC) enforces them.

## Your rights

You have the right to:

- **be told** what data is collected about you, why, and who it is shared
  with
- **see** the data an organisation holds about you
- **correct** data that is wrong or misleading
- **have data deleted** when it is no longer needed, or was collected
  unlawfully
- **object** to your data being used, including for direct marketing
- **move** your data to another provider in a usable format, where that
  applies

An organisation should only collect what it needs for a stated purpose, keep
it secure, and not keep it longer than necessary.

## Common problems

- **Loan apps contacting your friends and family** about your debt, or
  shaming you to your contacts. Digital lenders have been fined for this.
  See [The true cost of mobile loans](/guides/mobile-loans-true-cost).
- **Marketing messages** you never agreed to. You can ask to be removed, and
  marketers must stop.
- **Your ID or photos** being asked for, copied or kept without a good
  reason - for example, at a building's reception.
- **A data breach** where your details were leaked.
- **CCTV, recordings or photos** of you shared without consent.

## Using your rights

1. **Write to the organisation**, addressed to its data protection officer
   if it has one. Say which right you are using: for example, "I request a
   copy of the personal data you hold about me under the Data Protection Act
   2019."
2. Include enough to identify you, such as your name, phone number or
   account number.
3. Keep a copy and note the date.

## Complaining to the ODPC

If the organisation does not respond or refuses, complain to the **Office of
the Data Protection Commissioner**. You can file online on its website, and
complaining is free. Include:

- who the complaint is about
- what happened, with dates
- evidence: screenshots, messages, letters, your request and any reply

The ODPC must investigate and decide a complaint within
**{{ DATA_PROTECTION_DECISION_DAYS }} days**. It can order the organisation
to put things right, award compensation, and fine it up to
**KES {{ DATA_PROTECTION_MAX_FINE|shillings }}**, or 1% of its annual turnover
if that is lower.

## If you run a business

If you collect personal data from customers or staff, the Act applies to you
too. Larger organisations, and those handling sensitive data such as health
records, must register with the ODPC. Collect only what you need, tell people
why, and keep it secure.

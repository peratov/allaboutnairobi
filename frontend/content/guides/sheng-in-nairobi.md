[[Sheng]] is Nairobi's street language - a fast-changing mix of **Swahili**
and **English** with words from Kikuyu, Luo, Luhya and other languages. It
grew up in Nairobi's eastlands estates and is now heard across the city, in
music, on matatus, on radio and on social media.

## How it works

Sheng mostly uses **Swahili grammar** with borrowed, shortened or
rearranged words. Words change quickly - what's fashionable this year may
be old next year - and each estate has its own variations. Don't worry
about getting it perfect.

## Greetings

| Sheng | Meaning | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sasa? / Niaje? | Hi, how's it going? | Poa / Fiti (fine) |
| Mambo? | What's up? | Poa |
| Uko aje? | How are you? | Niko poa |
| Sema! | Hey! / Speak! | Poa, sema |

## On the matatu

Matatu crews use plenty of Sheng: the **makanga** (conductor) collects the
fare, the **dere** (driver) drives, and **"shuka"** means get off. See
[Matatus in Nairobi](/guides/matatus-in-nairobi).

## When to use it

- **With friends and young people** - Sheng is friendly and informal.
- **In shops, offices and with elders** - stick to **Swahili** or
  **English**. Using Sheng with older people or in formal settings can seem
  rude.
- **Visitors** - a few words like *sasa* and *poa* will get a smile.

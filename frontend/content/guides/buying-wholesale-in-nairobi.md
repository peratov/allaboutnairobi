Whether you are stocking a shop, feeding a big family or buying for a
[[chama]], buying wholesale in Nairobi can cut costs sharply. The city's
wholesale districts sit mostly on the east side of the CBD.

## Where to go

- **Nyamakima** - just east of the CBD, the place for **cereals, flour,
  sugar, rice, beans and household goods** in bulk, plus electricals and
  tools. Many [[duka]] owners stock up here.
- **Eastleigh** - a vast trading district of malls and wholesale shops for
  **clothes, shoes, fabric, curtains, carpets, bags and household goods**,
  many imported. Busy from early morning into the evening.
- **Gikomba** - the biggest market for [[mitumba]] (second-hand clothes and
  shoes), sold by the bale and by the piece. See
  [Markets in Nairobi](/guides/markets-in-nairobi).
- **Wakulima (Marikiti) Market** - the wholesale fruit and vegetable market,
  busiest before dawn.
- **Downtown streets** - River Road and nearby streets for **stationery,
  beauty products, electronics and hardware**, often with wholesale shops on
  upper floors.
- **Wholesale supermarkets and distributors** - cash-and-carry stores sell
  groceries and drinks by the carton at fixed prices.

## How to buy

- **Go early** - stock is fullest and crowds thinner in the morning.
- **Compare** a few shops before buying; prices differ for the same item.
- **Ask the wholesale price** and the quantity that unlocks it - it may be
  a carton, a dozen or a bale.
- **Check goods** before paying - expiry dates, sizes, colours, damage.
  Opened mitumba bales are a gamble; buyers often choose sellers they trust.
- **Pay** by [[M-Pesa]] to a [[till number]] or [[paybill]] in the
  business's name, and get a receipt.
- **Transport** - hand carts, pick-ups and boda deliveries wait near the
  markets. Agree the price first.

## Staying safe

- Carry little cash and keep your phone secure in crowded streets.
- Don't accept "help" from strangers who offer to find stock for you.
- Wear comfortable shoes and bring strong bags.

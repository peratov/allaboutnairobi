Losing someone is hard enough without the paperwork. This guide sets out the
practical steps, in the order they usually happen. It is general information,
not legal advice - succession can be complex, and an advocate helps.

## The first days

1. **Medical confirmation.** A doctor confirms the death and issues a
   certificate of the cause of death. If the death happened at home or
   unexpectedly, the police are involved.
2. **The mortuary.** The body is usually moved to a hospital or funeral home
   mortuary.
3. **Burial permit.** A permit for burial or cremation is issued by the civil
   registration authorities, based on the medical certificate. You need it
   before the funeral.

## The death certificate

Register the death and apply for the **death certificate** through the Civil
Registration Services, including on [[eCitizen]]. You will need it for almost
everything that follows: banks, insurers, pensions, NSSF, land and
succession. Order several certified copies.

## Funerals

Funerals in Kenya are often large and held **upcountry** in the family's home
area, and are commonly funded by a family committee and a [[harambee]].
Many employers, SACCOs and insurers have **last expense** cover that pays a
sum quickly for funeral costs.

## Deaths of foreign nationals

Contact the person's **embassy** straight away. They can advise on
repatriation of the body or ashes, which requires permits and an airline's
agreement, and on registering the death at home.

## Succession - dealing with the estate

Under the **Law of Succession Act**, nobody may deal with the dead person's
property - land, vehicles, bank accounts, shares - until a court has granted
authority.

1. **Get a letter from the chief** of the area where the person lived,
   listing the surviving family.
2. **File a petition in court** - for a **grant of probate** if there is a
   will, or **letters of administration** if there is not. Smaller estates can
   be handled in the Magistrates' Courts, with simplified procedures for small
   estates.
3. **Gazettement** - the petition is published in the **Kenya Gazette**, and
   anyone who objects has a set period to do so.
4. **The grant** is issued to the administrators.
5. **Confirmation** - after **six months**, the administrators apply for the
   grant to be confirmed, with an agreed distribution of the property.
6. **Transfer** - property is then transferred to the beneficiaries.

## Who inherits without a will

The law sets out who inherits: first the spouse or spouses and children, then
parents, then brothers and sisters and other relatives. Disputes are common, so
keep good records and get advice early.

## Planning ahead

- **Write a will**, with an advocate, and keep it safe.
- **Name beneficiaries** on pensions, insurance and SACCO accounts.
- **Keep a list** of accounts, policies and property for your family.

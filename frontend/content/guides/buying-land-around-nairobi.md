Buying a plot on the edge of Nairobi is one of the most common investments in
Kenya, and the first step for many people planning to build. It is also where
fraud is most common. This guide covers where people buy, what affects price,
and how to buy safely.

## Where people buy

Land inside Nairobi is scarce and expensive. Most buyers look at the growing
towns around it:

- **Kitengela, Athi River and Syokimau** - south-east, along the Namanga and
  Mombasa roads, close to JKIA and the SGR.
- **Kamulu, Joska and Malaa** - east, along Kangundo Road, often among the
  cheapest.
- **Ruiru, Juja and Thika** - north-east, along the Thika Superhighway.
- **Kikuyu, Limuru and Ruaka** - north-west, closer to the city and pricier.
- **Ngong, Kiserian and Rongai** - south-west.

## What land costs

The common unit is a **50 by 100 foot plot** - about an eighth of an acre.
Prices vary hugely even within one area:

- Plots far from tarmac roads in the outer areas are **advertised from under
  KES 1 million**.
- Plots near town centres, main roads and services in places like Ruiru, Juja
  and Kitengela often cost **several million**.

What pushes prices up: a tarmac road nearby, water and electricity, being in a
gated development, proximity to a town, and a **ready title**. Compare
several listings in the same area, and be suspicious of any plot much cheaper
than its neighbours.

## Land companies and "ready titles"

Many plots are sold by land-selling companies that subdivide larger
parcels. Some are reputable; others sell plots before the subdivision is
approved or titles issued. Ask:

- Is the **individual title** for my plot already issued?
- Has the **subdivision been approved** by the county?
- Are the **beacons** (boundary markers) in place?

## The checks

1. **Do an official search.** Ardhisasa covers Nairobi and some neighbouring
   counties; elsewhere, search at the county land registry. See
   [Land search on Ardhisasa](/guides/land-search-on-ardhisasa).
2. **Visit the land.** Walk the boundaries, and talk to neighbours about who
   owns it.
3. **Check the survey map** with a licensed surveyor to confirm the plot's
   position and size.
4. **Check land rates and rent** are paid, and ask for clearance.
5. **Check the use.** Zoning, road reserves, riparian land near rivers, and
   planned roads can make a plot unbuildable.
6. **Use your own advocate**, and pay into their client account or an escrow
   arrangement - never cash to an individual.

Most Kenyans die without a will, and their families can spend years in
court sorting out land, accounts and property. A clear, valid will makes it
quicker, cheaper and less likely to split the family.

## Who can make one

Anyone aged **18 or over** and of **sound mind** can make a will under the
**Law of Succession Act**. It must be made freely, without pressure or
deception.

## What makes it valid

A written will must be:

1. **signed** by you (or by someone else in your presence and at your
   direction)
2. signed in front of **at least two witnesses**, present at the same time
3. **signed by both witnesses** in your presence

**Do not let a beneficiary, or a beneficiary's spouse, witness the will**:
they can lose their gift.

An **oral will** is only valid in narrow circumstances - made before two
witnesses, with death within three months - and is easily disputed. Write
it down.

## What to include

- **Your full details**, and a statement cancelling any earlier wills.
- **An executor** - one or two trusted people, or a trust company, to carry
  out the will. They can also be beneficiaries.
- **Who gets what** - land (with title numbers), houses, vehicles, bank and
  SACCO accounts, shares, money market funds, businesses and personal items.
- **What happens to the rest** - the "residue" not specifically given away.
- **Guardians** for children under 18.
- Your **funeral and burial wishes**, if you have them.

## Providing for dependants

You can leave your property as you wish, but a spouse, child or other
dependant who is not reasonably provided for can ask the court to make
provision from the estate. Leaving out a dependant without explanation is a
common reason wills are challenged.

## Getting help

You can write a will yourself, but an **advocate** can make sure it is
valid, clear and covers property such as land properly. This is worth it if
you own land, a business or property in more than one country.

## Keeping it safe

- Keep the original somewhere safe - with your advocate, a bank, or the
  **Public Trustee**, which accepts wills for safekeeping.
- Tell your executor where it is.
- **Review it** after marriage, divorce, the birth of a child, or buying or
  selling major property.

## Other things to put in place

- **Name beneficiaries** (nominees) on pensions, life insurance, SACCO
  accounts and money market funds - these often pay out faster than the
  estate.
- **Keep a list** of your accounts, policies and property, with where the
  documents are.
- Make sure **land is in your name** and title documents are in order.

## Without a will

If you die without a will, the Law of Succession Act decides who inherits,
and your family must apply to court for letters of administration. See
[Death and succession in Kenya](/guides/death-and-succession-in-kenya).

Since **August 2017** Kenya has banned plastic carrier bags - one of the
toughest bans in the world. Since **June 2020** single-use plastics such as
water bottles and plates have also been banned in national parks, forests,
beaches and conservation areas. Shops use paper, cloth and woven bags
instead.

## What is banned

- **Plastic carrier bags** - the thin bags shops used to hand out, whether
  for carrying shopping or as flat bags.
- **Making, importing, selling and using** them are all covered.
- **Single-use plastics in protected areas** - bottles, cups, plates,
  cutlery and straws in national parks, reserves, forests and on beaches.

Some plastic packaging is still allowed, such as the plastic that wraps
products from the factory and bags for some industrial and medical uses.

## The penalties

Breaking the carrier-bag ban can bring a fine of **KES {{ PLASTIC_BAG_FINE_MIN|shillings }}
to KES {{ PLASTIC_BAG_FINE_MAX|shillings }}**, up to **{{ PLASTIC_BAG_JAIL_MAX_YEARS }}
years** in jail, or both. The National Environment Management Authority
(NEMA) enforces it, mostly against makers and sellers, but carrying a bag
is not risk-free.

## Visitors

- **Pack without plastic bags.** Don't bring duty-free shopping in plastic
  bags through the airport - they may be taken from you.
- **Zip-lock bags for toiletries** are generally tolerated for travel, but
  keep them in your luggage.
- **On safari and at the beach**, carry a refillable water bottle - lodges
  and camps usually provide refills. See
  [Visiting Kenya's national parks](/guides/visiting-kenyas-national-parks).

## What to use instead

- **Woven and cloth bags** are sold at supermarket tills for a small charge,
  and keep for years.
- **Kiondo** - the woven sisal basket - is the classic Kenyan shopping bag.
- **Paper bags** for small items.
- **Bring your own** to the market. See [Markets in Nairobi](/guides/markets-in-nairobi).

## Has it worked?

The ban cut plastic bag use sharply, but some smuggled bags are still sold
in markets, and NEMA runs periodic crackdowns. Report illegal bags to NEMA.

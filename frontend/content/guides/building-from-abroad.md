Building a house at home is a dream for many Kenyans abroad, and stories of
money lost are painfully common. Often it is not a stranger but a relative, a
friend or a "trusted" contractor. A few safeguards make a large difference.

## How people lose money

- **Money sent for materials that were never bought**, or bought cheaply while
  the full price is claimed.
- **Photos of someone else's building** sent as proof of progress.
- **A plot that was never really yours**, or sold to several people.
- **Work done badly**, so it has to be redone.
- **The project simply stalls**, with the money gone.

## The safeguards

### Own the land properly first

Buy through your own advocate, do an official search, and make sure the title
is in **your name**. See [Buying land around Nairobi](/guides/buying-land-around-nairobi)
and [Land search on Ardhisasa](/guides/land-search-on-ardhisasa).

### Use professionals, not just family

Hire an **architect**, a **quantity surveyor** and an **NCA-registered
contractor**, with a written contract. Family can help and keep an eye on
things, but should not be the only check. See
[Building a house in Kenya](/guides/building-a-house-in-kenya).

### Pay in stages, against verified progress

- Agree **stages** (foundations, walls, roof, finishes) and a price for each.
- Before each payment, have progress **certified by someone independent** -
  your quantity surveyor or engineer - not the person being paid.
- Pay into a **business bank account** in the contractor's name, never to a
  personal account or M-Pesa number.

### Check progress yourself

- Ask for **video calls on site**, walking around the building, rather than
  photos.
- Ask for **dated, geotagged photos**.
- **Visit** at key stages if you can, or send someone independent.

### Keep records

Keep the contract, drawings, approvals, invoices, receipts and bank
statements together. They help if something goes wrong, and when you sell.

## Powers of attorney

If someone needs to sign for you, give a **specific power of attorney** for
that purpose and period, and register it. Avoid a general power of attorney
that lets someone sell or borrow against your land. See
[Kenyans abroad](/guides/kenyans-abroad-managing-things-from-afar).

## Warning signs

- **Pressure to send money quickly**, or "prices have gone up" after you have
  paid.
- **Excuses for not showing** the site on video.
- **Requests to pay a new or personal account.**
- **No receipts** for materials.

## If it goes wrong

Stop further payments, gather your records, and get an advocate in Kenya.
Fraud can be reported to the police and the Directorate of Criminal
Investigations.

An **affidavit** is a written statement of facts that you swear or affirm is
true, in front of someone authorised to take oaths. Kenyan offices ask for
them all the time - and they are quick and cheap to get.

## When you need one

Common reasons include:

- a **lost document** - an ID, logbook, title or certificate - alongside a
  police abstract
- a **name** spelt differently on different documents, or a change of name
  (see [Changing your name in Kenya](/guides/changing-your-name-in-kenya))
- **late registration** of a birth, or adding a parent's details (see
  [Getting a birth certificate](/guides/getting-a-birth-certificate))
- confirming **marital status**, or that a couple live together
- school, university, HELB and bursary applications
- **succession** and court matters (see
  [Death and succession in Kenya](/guides/death-and-succession-in-kenya))

## Who can take an oath

A **commissioner for oaths** - in practice, almost every practising advocate
in Kenya is one. Magistrates can also take oaths. Many advocates' offices,
especially near courts and government offices, will draft and commission
simple affidavits while you wait.

## How it works

1. **Draft the affidavit.** It is written in the first person, in numbered
   paragraphs, stating only facts you know. The office asking for it may have
   a template, and the advocate's office can type it for you.
2. **Take your ID or passport**, and any documents the affidavit refers to.
3. **Sign in front of the commissioner.** You swear (on a holy book) or
   affirm (without one) that the contents are true. The commissioner then
   signs and stamps it.

Never sign an affidavit in advance, or sign one with blank spaces. Lying in
an affidavit is perjury, a criminal offence.

## What it costs

The fee for commissioning an affidavit is set by the Advocates Remuneration
Order, though in practice it varies. Drafting a simple affidavit costs more
than just commissioning one you bring. Ask the price before you start.

## Statutory declarations

A **statutory declaration** is similar - a solemn written statement of
facts - and is used by some offices instead of an affidavit. It is made in
the same way, before a commissioner for oaths.

## Documents for use abroad

A foreign embassy, university or employer may want a document signed by a
**notary public** rather than a commissioner. Notaries are senior advocates
commissioned to certify documents for use abroad. The document may then need
**authentication** by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and sometimes by the
country's embassy. Ask the receiving organisation exactly what it needs
before you start.

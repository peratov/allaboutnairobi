Whether it is a stolen phone or a lost ID, the first step is almost always the
same: report it to the police and get a record of the report.

## In an emergency

Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** or **112** from any phone. The Kenya Red Cross
emergency line is **{{ RED_CROSS_EMERGENCY }}**.

## Reporting at a police station

1. Go to the police station covering the area where it happened.
2. Report what happened at the desk. It is written in the **Occurrence Book**,
   and you are given an **OB number**. Write it down: everything that follows
   refers to it.
3. Ask for a **police abstract** if you need proof of the report - for a lost
   ID, passport or phone, or for an insurance claim.

**A police abstract is free.** Abstracts for lost documents can also be applied
for online through the police service's portal. You should never have to pay to
make a report.

## If you have been assaulted

Get medical care first. For a criminal case, the police issue a **P3 form**,
which a doctor completes to record the injuries. It is free. Keep copies of all
medical records.

## Replacing lost documents

| Lost | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| National ID | Report it and get an abstract, then apply for a replacement at a registration office or [[Huduma Centre]] |
| Kenyan passport | Report it, then apply for a replacement through the immigration service on [[eCitizen]] |
| Foreign passport | Report it, get an abstract, and contact your embassy for an emergency travel document; then ask immigration about your permit or visa stamp |
| Foreigner certificate | Report it and apply for a replacement through the Directorate of Immigration Services |
| Phone or SIM | Call your network to block the SIM, then replace it at a shop with your ID. See [Phones and SIM cards](/guides/phones-and-sim-cards) |
| Bank card | Block it immediately in your bank app or by calling the bank |
| Driving licence | Report it, then apply for a replacement through [[NTSA]] on eCitizen |

## Other help

- **Gender-based violence:** the national helpline is **1195**, free and
  confidential.
- **Children at risk:** Childline Kenya is **116**.
- **Foreigners:** your embassy can help with lawyers, doctors and contacting
  family.

## Complaints about the police

If you are asked for money, refused a report, or mistreated, you can complain
to the **Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA)**.

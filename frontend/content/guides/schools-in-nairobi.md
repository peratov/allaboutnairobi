Nairobi has everything from free public primary schools to international
schools with fees to match the best in the world. Where you live and where the
school is matters as much as the school itself: a school run can mean an hour
in traffic each way.

## The Kenyan system

Kenya has replaced the old 8-4-4 system with **Competency-Based Education
(CBE)**, known until recently as the CBC. It is organised as 2-6-3-3:

| Stage | Years | Ages, roughly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-primary (PP1, PP2) | 2 | 4 - 5 |
| Primary (Grades 1 - 6) | 6 | 6 - 11 |
| Junior school (Grades 7 - 9) | 3 | 12 - 14 |
| Senior school (Grades 10 - 12) | 3 | 15 - 17 |

The first students entered senior school in January 2026, so the system is
still settling in and details change. The Kenya National Examinations Council
(KNEC) runs the national assessments.

The school year runs from **January to November** in three terms.

## Public and private Kenyan schools

- **Public schools** charge little or no tuition at primary level, with
  government capitation, but classes are large.
- **Private schools** follow the Kenyan curriculum with smaller classes and
  more facilities, at a wide range of fees.

## International schools

International schools follow the British (IGCSE and A level), International
Baccalaureate or American curriculum. Their year usually runs from September to
June or July. Well-known Nairobi schools include:

- **International School of Kenya** - IB and American, in Rosslyn
- **Rosslyn Academy** - American, in Rosslyn
- **Braeburn**, **Brookhouse** and **Hillcrest** - British curriculum, with
  several campuses
- **Banda School** and **Kenton College** - British-style preparatory schools
- **German School Nairobi** and **Lycée Denis Diderot** - for German and
  French education

Fees are high and usually come with one-off registration, assessment and
development charges. Ask every school for its full fee schedule, including
transport, lunch and trips.

## Choosing a school

- **Visit** during term, and ask to see a lesson.
- **Check registration** with the Ministry of Education, and accreditation for
  international schools.
- **Think about the commute.** Many families choose the school first and the
  neighbourhood second. See [Nairobi's neighbourhoods](/guides/neighbourhoods-of-nairobi).
- **Apply early.** Popular schools have waiting lists, especially in the lower
  years.

## Universities

Nairobi has the University of Nairobi, Kenyatta University, Strathmore
University, USIU-Africa and Aga Khan University, among others. Most are on the
[map](/map#landmarks-education).

English is an official language and widely spoken in Nairobi, so you can live
here without Swahili. But a few words go a long way - with the
[[mama mboga]], the conductor and the [[askari]] at the gate - and people
notice the effort.

## Greetings

| Swahili | Meaning | Reply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habari? | How are you? (literally "news?") | Nzuri - fine |
| Habari ya asubuhi? | Good morning | Nzuri |
| Mambo? | Hi, what's up? | Poa - cool |
| Sasa? | Hi (Sheng) | Poa or Fiti |
| Shikamoo | Respectful greeting to an elder, more common on the coast | Marahaba |
| Karibu | Welcome | Asante |
| Kwaheri | Goodbye | Kwaheri |

"Jambo" is understood, but it mostly marks you as a tourist.

Prices in Sheng come with their own slang - "a thao" for a thousand shillings,
for example.

## Sheng

[[Sheng]] is Nairobi's street slang, mixing Swahili, English and other
languages, and it changes fast. A few that have lasted:

- **Poa / Fiti** - cool, fine
- **Niaje?** - how's it going?
- **Doh** - money
- **Mathree** - matatu
- **Shags** - the rural home ([[upcountry]])

## Learning more

Swahili is phonetic and regular, which makes it one of the easier languages to
start. Language schools in Nairobi offer short courses, and a weekly lesson
with a tutor plus practice at the market works well.

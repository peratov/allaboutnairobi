A crash is frightening, and what you do in the first hour shapes everything
that follows - the police report, the insurance claim, and any court case.
Here is the order to do things in, whether it is a scrape at a roundabout or
something worse.

## At the scene

1. **Stop.** Leaving the scene of an accident is an offence.
2. **Check for injuries.** If anyone is hurt, call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}**
   for an ambulance and the police. Don't move an injured person unless they
   are in danger where they are. See [Emergencies in Nairobi](/guides/emergencies-in-nairobi).
3. **Make the scene safe.** Hazard lights on, and put out your warning
   triangles behind the car.
4. **Take photos** before the cars are moved: both cars, the number plates,
   the damage, the road and any skid marks, from several angles.
5. **Swap details** with the other driver: name, phone number, ID or driving
   licence number, number plate, and their insurer and policy number from the
   insurance sticker on the windscreen.
6. **Get witnesses' numbers** if anyone saw it happen.
7. **Don't admit fault** or agree to pay on the spot, however friendly the
   conversation. Leave that to the police and the insurers.

A crowd often gathers quickly in Nairobi. Stay calm, keep your phone and
valuables with you, and if the mood turns hostile, stay in the car and call
the police.

## Reporting to the police

Report the accident at the **police station nearest to where it happened**,
as soon as you can and ideally **within 24 hours** - insurers usually insist
on this. Do it even for a minor bump: the other driver may report it later
and tell a different story.

- The police record the accident in the **Occurrence Book (OB)** and give you
  an **OB number**.
- You then get a **police abstract** - the official summary of the accident.
  Your insurer, the other driver's insurer and any court will ask for it.
- If anyone was injured, a **P3 form** records the injuries; it is filled in
  by the police and a doctor.

The police may also inspect the vehicles before they are repaired.

## Your insurance claim

1. **Call your insurer** as soon as you have the OB number - most have a
   claims line and an app.
2. **Fill in the claim form** and send the police abstract, a copy of your
   driving licence, your ID and KRA PIN, the logbook and the photos.
3. **The insurer assesses the car** and tells you which garage to use.
4. **Don't start repairs** until the insurer has approved them.

If the other driver was at fault, you can claim from **their** insurer
instead. With only third-party cover, your own car's damage is not covered
unless you claim from the other side. See
[Car insurance in Kenya](/guides/car-insurance-in-kenya) and
[Insurance claims and complaints](/guides/insurance-claims-and-complaints-in-kenya).

## If you were hurt

Keep every medical receipt and report. Injury claims against the other
driver's insurer can take months; a lawyer can help, and many take road
accident cases. See [Finding a lawyer in Kenya](/guides/finding-a-lawyer-in-kenya)
and [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).

## Hit by a boda or a matatu

The same steps apply. Note the plate and the sacco name painted on a matatu,
and the boda's plate, and report it. See
[Boda bodas in Nairobi](/guides/boda-bodas-in-nairobi).

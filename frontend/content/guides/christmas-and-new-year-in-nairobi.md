December changes Nairobi. Schools close, offices wind down, and from mid-month
much of the city heads [[upcountry]] to spend Christmas with family. The roads
out are packed; the city itself turns quiet and easy to get around - until
everyone comes back in January.

## The holidays

- **Jamhuri Day, 12 December** - independence and the republic.
- **Christmas Day, 25 December.**
- **Utamaduni Day, 26 December** - culture day, formerly Boxing Day.
- **New Year's Day, 1 January.**

When a holiday falls on a Sunday, the Monday is usually a holiday. See
[Public holidays in Kenya](/guides/public-holidays-in-kenya).

## Travelling home

- **Book early.** Buses, the SGR to Mombasa and flights sell out for the
  days just before Christmas, and prices climb. See
  [Nairobi to Mombasa](/guides/nairobi-to-mombasa) and
  [Travelling around Kenya](/guides/travelling-around-kenya).
- **Matatu and bus fares rise** in the peak days - expect to pay more.
- **Leave early in the day** to beat the jams on the roads out of the city.
- **Police checks** are frequent on the highways, including for drink-driving.

## In the city

- **Many small businesses close** from around Christmas until early
  January; supermarkets, malls and hospitals stay open.
- **Security** - homes left empty are a target. Lock up, tell a neighbour or
  your [[askari]], and don't announce your trip online. See
  [Home security in Nairobi](/guides/home-security-in-nairobi).
- **Money** - pay rent and bills before you leave, and top up power
  [[tokens]] for anyone staying behind.

## Christmas Day

- **Church** - many families go to a Christmas morning service, and
  midnight services on Christmas Eve are popular. See
  [Places of worship in Nairobi](/guides/places-of-worship-in-nairobi).
- **Food** - [[nyama choma]], pilau, chapati and a slaughtered goat or
  chicken for a big family meal. See [Kenyan food and eating out](/guides/kenyan-food-and-eating-out).
- **Shopping** - markets and malls are busiest in the week before Christmas.

## New Year's Eve

Hotels, clubs and rooftop bars hold parties, churches hold crossover
services, and there are fireworks over the city at midnight. Book tables
ahead, plan your ride home, and expect surge pricing on ride-hailing apps.
See [Nightlife in Nairobi](/guides/nightlife-in-nairobi).

## January

January is long: school fees, rent and back-to-school costs all land after
the holiday spending. Budget for it before December. See the
[budget planner](/tools/budget-planner).

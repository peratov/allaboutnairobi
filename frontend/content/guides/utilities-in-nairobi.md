## Electricity

Power comes from [[KPLC]] (Kenya Power). Most flats have a **prepaid meter**.
You buy [[tokens]] and type the 20-digit code into the meter.

- **To buy tokens:** in M-Pesa, pay the Kenya Power prepaid [[paybill]]
  (888880) with your meter number as the account number. The token arrives by
  SMS within a minute or two. Bank apps and supermarkets sell tokens too.
- **Postpaid meters** are billed monthly and paid through Kenya Power's
  postpaid paybill (888888) with your account number.
- **Outages** happen, usually for a few hours. Many buildings have a
  generator for common areas and sometimes for flats. Use a surge protector
  for anything expensive, and keep a torch or rechargeable lamp.

Electric water heaters and cookers eat tokens. If your balance vanishes
faster than expected, check the water heater first.

## Water

Mains water comes from Nairobi City Water and Sewerage Company, and in many
areas it does not flow every day. How a building copes decides how much this
matters to you:

- **Storage tanks** on the roof or in the compound fill when the supply runs.
- **Boreholes** give many newer blocks their own supply.
- **Water bowsers** can be hired to fill a tank during long shortages.

Ask about water before you sign a lease. Do not drink tap water unless it has
been boiled or filtered; most people buy bottled water or use a dispenser.

## Internet

Fibre is widely available in the main residential areas. Check which providers
serve your building before you move in.

- **Fibre:** Safaricom Home Fibre, Zuku and Faiba are the most common, with
  monthly plans from about KES 3,000.
- **Mobile data:** bundles on Safaricom, Airtel and Telkom. 4G is good across
  the city, and 5G is available in parts of it.
- **Satellite:** Starlink is licensed in Kenya and useful on the outskirts.

Installation can take anywhere from a day to a couple of weeks. A mobile hotspot
covers the gap.

## Cooking gas

Most kitchens cook on LPG. Cylinders come in 6 kg and 13 kg sizes. Once you
own a cylinder you swap the empty one for a full one at a petrol station, a
supermarket or a gas dealer, and many deliver. Buy from a reputable dealer and
check the seal. Illegally refilled cylinders are a real hazard.

## Rubbish

Collection is usually arranged by the building or estate with a private
collector for a monthly fee, sometimes included in the service charge.

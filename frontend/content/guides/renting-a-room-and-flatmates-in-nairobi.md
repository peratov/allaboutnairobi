Sharing a flat or renting a room is one of the cheapest ways to live in a
good part of Nairobi, especially for students, young professionals and
newcomers. It works well when everyone agrees the money and rules up front.

## Ways to share

- **Renting a room** in someone else's home - the owner or main tenant
  stays in charge.
- **Sharing a flat** - two or more people rent together and split the
  costs.
- **Shared houses and co-living** - rooms with shared kitchens and lounges,
  sometimes furnished, managed by a company.
- **Hostels** for students. See
  [Student accommodation in Nairobi](/guides/student-accommodation-in-nairobi).

## Finding rooms and flatmates

- **Friends, colleagues and church or community groups** - the safest
  route.
- **Social media groups and listing sites** for rooms and flatmates.
- **Notice boards** at universities and workplaces.

Always **see the room in person** and meet the people. Never pay a deposit
for a room you haven't seen. See [Rental scams in Nairobi](/guides/rental-scams-in-nairobi).

## Money

- **Rent** - agree each person's share; bigger rooms or en-suites often pay
  more.
- **Deposit** - who holds it and how it is returned.
- **Bills** - electricity [[tokens]], water, internet, gas, rubbish
  collection and the house help, if any. Agree how they are split and who
  pays. See [Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi).
- **Shared items** - cleaning supplies, drinking water, gas refills.
- Pay by [[M-Pesa]] so there is a record.

## Put it in writing

- If you are renting from the landlord together, everyone should ideally be
  named on the **tenancy agreement**. See
  [Tenancy agreements in Kenya](/guides/tenancy-agreements-in-kenya).
- If you rent a room from a tenant, check the **landlord allows
  subletting**, and write a simple agreement covering rent, deposit,
  notice and house rules.

## House rules

Agree early on: **guests** and overnight visitors, **noise** and quiet
hours, **cleaning**, **cooking** and shared food, **security** - who has
keys and locking up - and **notice** when someone wants to move out.

## Safety

- Know your flatmates - ID copies and emergency contacts for each person.
- Keep valuables and documents locked away.
- Don't hand keys to people you don't know.

See [Home security in Nairobi](/guides/home-security-in-nairobi).

## When things go wrong

- Talk early about late rent or bills.
- Give proper **notice** before moving out, and settle your share.
- For disputes with a landlord, see [Tenant rights in Kenya](/guides/tenant-rights-in-kenya).

A **serviced apartment** is a furnished flat with hotel-like services:
cleaning, linen, Wi-Fi, security and often a gym, pool and backup power. They
are the easiest place to stay while you look for a long-term home in
Nairobi, and many people stay in them for months.

## Where they are

Most are in the areas newcomers want to live in anyway:

- **Westlands** - the most choice, near offices, malls and nightlife. See
  [Living in Westlands](/guides/living-in-westlands).
- **Kilimani and Kileleshwa** - central, with many apartment blocks. See
  [Living in Kilimani](/guides/living-in-kilimani).
- **Lavington** - quieter and residential.
- **Upper Hill** - near offices and hospitals.
- **Gigiri and Runda** - near the UN and embassies.
- **Karen** - for families and a greener setting.

## What they cost

Prices depend on area, size, building and length of stay. Serviced apartments
usually cost **more per month than an unfurnished flat**, but much less than a
hotel for the same period, and they include furniture, utilities and cleaning.

- **Nightly rates** are highest for short stays.
- **Monthly rates** are much lower per night. Always ask for one.
- **Longer stays** of three months or more can be negotiated further.

Check whether the price includes **electricity, water, Wi-Fi, cleaning,
parking** and VAT.

## How to find one

- **Booking and short-let platforms** - good for the first few nights, and for
  reviews.
- **Direct with the building or operator** - usually cheaper for a month or
  more.
- **Relocation agents** and employers' preferred lists.

## What to check

- **Security** - controlled access, guards and CCTV.
- **Backup power and water** - generator and borehole or tanks.
- **Internet speed** - ask for a speed test if you work from home.
- **Cleaning** - how often, and whether linen changes are included.
- **Noise** - especially in Westlands.
- **The commute** at rush hour to where you need to be.
- **Cancellation and deposit terms.**

## Scams

Fake listings are common. Never pay a deposit for a property you have not seen
or through someone you cannot verify; book through a platform with payment
protection, or pay the operator's business account after a viewing. See
[Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing#scams).

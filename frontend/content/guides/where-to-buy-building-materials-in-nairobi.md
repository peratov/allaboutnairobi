Building or renovating in or around Nairobi means buying a lot of materials,
and prices vary widely between suppliers. Buying well - and checking what
arrives - can save a large part of the budget.

## Where to buy

- **Neighbourhood hardware shops** - every estate has them, selling cement,
  nails, timber, paint and plumbing. Convenient for small amounts.
- **Industrial Area** - the city's main cluster of larger suppliers and
  distributors for cement, steel, roofing sheets, tiles and pipes, with
  better prices for bulk.
- **Factories and mills direct** - steel mills, roofing makers and
  cement brands sell through authorised distributors. Buying direct or from
  a distributor is often cheapest for large orders and reduces the risk of
  substandard steel.
- **Building centres and big-box stores** - higher prices, but wide stock,
  fixed prices and returns. Good for tiles, sanitaryware and fittings.
- **Quarries and yards** - **blocks**, **sand** and **ballast** come by the
  lorry from yards on the edges of the city.

## Buying tips

- **Get quotes** from several suppliers, including delivery.
- **Buy in bulk** where you can - many suppliers give better prices for
  larger orders, and some set minimum orders.
- **Check brands and standards** - cement and steel should carry the
  **KEBS** mark. Thin, light steel bars are a sign of substandard steel.
- **Count and measure on delivery** - bags, bars, sheets, blocks. For sand
  and ballast, know the lorry size you paid for.
- **Store cement dry** and use it within a few weeks.
- **Pay** by [[M-Pesa]] [[till number]] or bank transfer to the business,
  and get a receipt.

## Prices

Prices of cement, steel and roofing move with fuel costs, the shilling and
taxes. Check current prices with suppliers before you budget, and add a
margin for increases on long projects.

## Avoiding trouble

- **Don't hand all the cash to a fundi** to buy materials - buy them
  yourself or pay suppliers directly.
- **Keep materials secure** on site, with a lockable store and a record of
  what came in and went out.
- **Watch for diversion** - materials can walk off site.

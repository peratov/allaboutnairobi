Kenya's Constitution protects the right to **assemble, demonstrate, picket
and present petitions** peacefully and unarmed. Protests are part of
Nairobi life - from small pickets to large national demonstrations - and
some have turned violent. Whether you take part or just need to get home,
knowing your rights and the risks helps.

## Your rights

- **Article 37** of the Constitution protects peaceful, unarmed assembly
  and demonstration.
- Organisers are expected to **notify the police** in advance under the
  Public Order Act. Notification is not the same as asking permission.
- Police must respect the right to protest and use force only when
  necessary and proportionate.

## If you join a protest

- **Go with friends** and agree a meeting point if you're separated.
- **Carry ID**, a charged phone, water and a little cash.
- **Write an emergency contact** and a lawyer's or legal aid number on your
  arm or a card.
- **Stay peaceful** and move away from violence, looting or confrontation.
- **Tear gas** - move away upwind, don't rub your eyes, and rinse with
  clean water.
- **Know your exits**, and leave if the mood turns.
- Be careful posting photos that identify other protesters.

## If you're arrested

- Stay calm; don't resist.
- You have the right to be told why, to remain silent, to contact a lawyer
  and family, and to be brought before a court within **24 hours**.
- Note the station and officers' details.
- Legal aid groups often provide free lawyers during protests. See
  [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).

## If you're not protesting

- **Follow the news** and local WhatsApp groups on protest days.
- **Avoid the CBD** and protest routes; work from home if you can.
- **Plan your route home** early - matatus may stop running and roads
  close. See [Getting around Nairobi](/guides/getting-around-nairobi).
- **Businesses** in the CBD often close early on protest days.
- **Visitors** - follow your embassy's advice. See
  [Is Nairobi safe for tourists?](/guides/is-nairobi-safe-for-tourists).

## If someone is hurt

Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}**. The **Kenya Red Cross** and volunteer
medics often assist at protests. See [Emergencies in Nairobi](/guides/emergencies-in-nairobi).

## Reporting police abuse

Complaints about police conduct can be made to the **Independent Policing
Oversight Authority** (IPOA). Human rights organisations also document
cases.

Kenyan law recognises five kinds of marriage under the Marriage Act 2014:
**civil, Christian, Islamic, Hindu and customary**. All must be registered, and
both people must be at least 18 and marrying freely.

## A civil marriage

Civil marriages are handled by the Registrar of Marriages, part of the Office
of the Attorney General, and booked through [[eCitizen]].

1. **Give notice.** Both partners apply online with their details and
   documents, and pay the notice fee.
2. **Wait {{ MARRIAGE_NOTICE_DAYS }} days.** The notice is published so that
   anyone with a legal objection can raise it.
3. **Book the ceremony and interview.** Both partners appear with original
   documents and two witnesses, and pay the marriage fee.
4. **The ceremony** is held before the registrar, and you receive a marriage
   certificate.

Fees and forms change, so check the current ones on eCitizen.

## Documents

Each partner usually needs:

- National ID, or passport for foreigners
- Passport photos
- If previously married, a divorce decree or death certificate

**Foreigners** are usually also asked for proof that they are free to marry,
such as a certificate of no impediment or an affidavit of single status from
their embassy or home country. Some embassies publish a checklist for their
citizens.

## Religious marriages

Christian, Islamic and Hindu marriages are conducted by licensed ministers or
officials of the faith, who register the marriage and issue the certificate.
Speak to the church, mosque or temple well ahead.

## Customary marriages

A marriage under the customs of a community - often including dowry
negotiations - is legally valid, but must be **registered** with the Registrar
of Marriages. Registration protects both spouses and any children, so do not
skip it.

## After the wedding

- A **marriage certificate** is needed to add a spouse to medical cover, to
  apply for a spouse's dependant pass, and to change a surname on an ID or
  passport.
- Foreign couples may need the certificate **authenticated** for use abroad.

Kenya is one of Africa's biggest crypto markets, and for years it had no law
for it at all - only warnings from the Central Bank. That changed with the
**Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025** (the VASP Act). This is general
information, not legal or financial advice.

## Is it legal to own crypto?

Yes. Owning, buying and selling crypto as an individual is not an offence.
But crypto is **not legal tender**: nobody has to accept it, and it is not
protected like money in a bank or M-Pesa. If an exchange collapses or a wallet
is hacked, there is no deposit insurance.

## What the VASP Act does

The Act regulates the **businesses** that serve crypto users - exchanges,
brokers, wallet and custody providers, payment services and token issuers. It:

- requires them to be **licensed** and to have a presence in Kenya
- splits supervision between two regulators:
  - the **Central Bank of Kenya (CBK)** for wallets, payment services and
    stablecoin issuers
  - the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)** for exchanges, brokers and other
    investment-type services
- sets rules on **customer funds**, record-keeping, cybersecurity, and
  anti-money-laundering checks, so expect identity verification (KYC)
- makes running an unlicensed crypto business an offence

The Treasury's regulations under the Act came out in 2026, and businesses
already operating were given a transition period to comply.

## Checking a platform

Before you deposit money anywhere:

- Look it up on the **CMA** and **CBK** websites, which publish who they have
  licensed.
- Check the company has a **Kenyan address and a named entity** you could
  complain to.
- Be wary of any platform that only exists as an app, a Telegram group or a
  WhatsApp number.

A platform that is not licensed in Kenya is not necessarily a scam, but you
have no Kenyan regulator to complain to if things go wrong.

## Tax

The old **Digital Asset Tax** on the value of each transfer was **repealed
from 1 July 2025**. Instead, platforms pay **excise duty at
{{ DIGITAL_ASSET_EXCISE_RATE|percent }}% on the fees** they charge you, which
usually shows up in the fee. Profits and income from crypto can still be
taxable - see [Tax on crypto in Kenya](/guides/tax-on-crypto-in-kenya).

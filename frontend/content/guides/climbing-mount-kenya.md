**Mount Kenya**, Africa's second-highest mountain, is a few hours' drive
north of Nairobi. Most trekkers aim for **Point Lenana**
({{ POINT_LENANA_HEIGHT_M|count }} metres), which needs no technical climbing,
only fitness, warm kit and time to acclimatise. The higher summits, Batian
and Nelion, are for experienced rock climbers.

## The routes

- **Sirimon** - from the north-west, near Nanyuki. A gradual, drier route
  with good acclimatisation; a popular choice for a first climb.
- **Chogoria** - from the east. The most scenic, with gorges and lakes, but
  further to drive.
- **Naro Moru** - from the west. The most direct, but steep, and the
  "vertical bog" can be very wet. Easy to go up too quickly.

A favourite plan is **up Sirimon and down Chogoria** over five or six days.
Allow **at least four days** for any route.

## Guides, porters and permits

- Go with a **guide** licensed by the **Kenya Wildlife Service** (KWS).
  Porters carry most of your kit, and a cook is often included.
- Use a **registered operator**, or hire guides through the park gates or
  established guide associations. A good operator handles park fees, huts
  and transport.
- **Park fees** are charged per day by KWS, with lower rates for Kenyan
  citizens and residents. Pay through official KWS channels. Check current
  fees before you budget.

See [Choosing a safari company](/guides/choosing-a-safari-company) for
checking operators.

## Altitude sickness

- **Go slowly** and drink plenty of water. Most problems come from climbing
  too fast.
- Headaches, nausea and poor sleep are common. **Confusion, breathlessness
  at rest or loss of coordination** are dangerous - descend at once.
- Talk to a doctor before the trip if you have heart or lung conditions.

## Kit

- **Warm layers** - nights below freezing near the top; a down jacket,
  hat and gloves.
- **Waterproofs** and good, worn-in boots.
- **Sleeping bag** rated well below zero.
- **Head torch** - summit day starts in the dark.
- **Sun protection** - the sun is fierce at altitude.

Gear can be hired in Nairobi or at the trailheads.

## When to go

The driest months are generally **January to February** and **July to
October**. Avoid the long rains around April and May. See
[Weather in Nairobi](/guides/weather-in-nairobi).

## Getting there

Drive or take a shuttle from Nairobi to **Nanyuki**, **Naro Moru** or
**Chogoria**. Most operators include transfers. See
[Travelling around Kenya](/guides/travelling-around-kenya).

## Training

Practise on [hikes near Nairobi](/guides/hiking-near-nairobi) - Ngong Hills
and Mount Longonot are good tests - and walk regularly for weeks before.

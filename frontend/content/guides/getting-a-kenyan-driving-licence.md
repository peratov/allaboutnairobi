Driving licences in Kenya are issued by [[NTSA]] (the National Transport and
Safety Authority) as a **smart driving licence** - a card with a chip - and
applied for through [[eCitizen]].

## Learning to drive

1. **Enrol at a registered driving school.** NTSA requires it; there is no
   route to a licence without one. Courses usually take a few weeks.
2. **Get a provisional licence** through your NTSA account on eCitizen, so you
   can drive with an instructor.
3. **Take the test** - a theory test and a practical driving test, booked
   through the school.
4. **Apply for the smart licence** on eCitizen, pay, and go to the NTSA office
   you chose for your photo and fingerprints. The card usually takes a few
   weeks.

## Converting a foreign licence

Foreigners can drive for a short period on a valid licence from home, ideally
with an International Driving Permit. If you are staying longer than about
**90 days**, you are expected to get a Kenyan licence.

What NTSA usually asks for:

- Your NTSA account on eCitizen
- Your passport with your permit or pass
- Your valid foreign licence
- A letter confirming the licence from your embassy or the licensing
  authority that issued it

Holders of licences from some countries can convert without a test; others
have to take the Kenyan test. Ask NTSA which applies to yours before you start.

## Renewing

Smart licences are renewed through eCitizen. Keep your NTSA account details
up to date, and renew before the expiry date on the card.

## Classes

Licences are issued by class - motorcycles, light vehicles, heavier vehicles
and so on. A standard car licence does not cover a motorbike or a matatu.

Matatus - privately run minibuses and buses - carry most of Nairobi's
commuters. They go almost everywhere, cheaply and often, and once you know
how they work they are the easiest way to get around the city.

## How they work

- Each matatu runs a fixed **route**, usually between the **CBD** and an
  estate or suburb, or along a main road. Routes have **numbers**, shown on
  the front and side along with the destination.
- Matatus leave from **stages** - termini in the CBD and in the estates -
  and stop at stages along the way. Many will also stop where you ask, if it
  is safe.
- Every matatu belongs to a registered **SACCO** or company, whose name is
  painted on the side. The SACCO is responsible for the vehicle and crew.
- The **conductor** (the "makanga" or "kange") collects fares and calls out
  the destination; the driver drives.

## Finding the right one

- **Ask.** People at the stage, the conductor, or a colleague will know
  which number goes where. This is still the most reliable way.
- **Map apps** include many matatu routes, based on the Digital Matatus
  mapping of the network, though not every route or change.
- Routes into the CBD end at different stages depending on the direction
  you are coming from - you may need to walk between stages to change.

## Fares

- Fares depend on the **distance** and the **time**. They go up at **rush
  hour**, in the **rain**, when fuel prices rise, and late at night, and come
  down off-peak.
- The conductor will tell you the fare; other passengers will know if it
  sounds wrong.
- Pay in **cash**, ideally with small notes and coins, or by **M-Pesa**
  where the matatu shows a till or paybill number. Check the name on the
  confirmation.

## Matatu culture

Many matatus - especially the bigger buses known as **nganyas** - are
famous for bold art, lights, loud music and screens. They are part of
Nairobi's street culture. If you prefer a quieter ride, plainer vehicles run
the same routes.

## Safety and comfort

- **Avoid rush hour** if you can, when matatus are packed and traffic is
  slow.
- Keep your **phone and wallet** out of sight, especially near the door and
  at busy stages.
- Choose a matatu that is not overloaded and has seatbelts; you are allowed
  to wear one.
- If a driver is reckless, you can get off at the next stage. Note the
  **SACCO and number plate** and report it to the SACCO or the
  **National Transport and Safety Authority** (NTSA).
- At night, ride-hailing is often safer. See [Safety in Nairobi](/guides/safety-in-nairobi).

Kenya has strict tobacco laws. Smoking in public places is banned, **shisha
is illegal**, and selling tobacco to under-18s is an offence. Rules are
enforced unevenly, but fines and arrests do happen - especially in Nairobi's
CBD.

## Smoking in public

- Under the **Tobacco Control Act**, smoking is banned in **enclosed public
  places** - offices, restaurants, bars, public transport, hospitals and
  schools - and in many outdoor public spaces.
- Nairobi has had rules restricting smoking on **streets** in the CBD, with
  smoking allowed only in **designated smoking zones**.
- Venues may have a **designated smoking area**; smoke only there.
- **Fines** apply, and county askaris and police do enforce them.

## Shisha is banned

- The government **banned shisha** in **2017** - its manufacture, import,
  sale, advertising and use.
- Breaking the ban can lead to a fine or jail. New rules with much higher
  penalties have been proposed.
- Some venues still offer shisha, and police raids happen. Smoking it puts
  you at risk of arrest.

## Vaping and nicotine pouches

**E-cigarettes, vapes** and **nicotine pouches** are regulated as tobacco
or nicotine products, and the rules on them are tightening. Treat them like
cigarettes: don't use them where smoking is banned, and don't sell them to
minors.

## Buying tobacco

- You must be **18 or over** to buy tobacco; sellers can ask for ID.
- **Duty-free** limits apply when bringing cigarettes into Kenya.

## Visitors

Don't smoke in hotels, restaurants or public places unless there's a
marked smoking area, and never smoke on matatus or at the airport outside
smoking rooms. See [Culture and etiquette in Kenya](/guides/culture-and-etiquette-in-kenya).

## Help to quit

- **Doctors and clinics** can advise on quitting and nicotine replacement,

- **Counselling** helps - see [Therapy and mental health](/guides/therapy-and-mental-health-in-nairobi).

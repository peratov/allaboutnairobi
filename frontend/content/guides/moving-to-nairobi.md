## Before you fly

### The eTA, or no eTA

Kenya replaced visas with an [[eTA]] (Electronic Travel Authorisation) in
January 2024. Most visitors apply online at **etakenya.go.ke** before
travelling and pay a processing fee. Apply at least a few days ahead; approval
is usually quick but not guaranteed to be.

Since mid-2025, most African nationals no longer need an eTA at all, and
citizens of the East African Community can simply travel. The list changes, so
check the official site for your own passport rather than a blog post.

You will also want:

- **A passport valid for at least six months**, with blank pages.
- **A yellow fever certificate** if you are arriving from, or transiting
  through, a country with yellow fever risk. Airlines check this before you
  board.
- **Proof of where you are staying** and a return or onward ticket, which
  immigration may ask for.

### If you are coming to work

An eTA lets you visit, not work. Working in Kenya needs a **work permit**,
applied for through the Directorate of Immigration Services, normally by your
employer before you start. The common classes are:

| Permit | For |
| --- | --- |
| Class D | Employment with a specific employer |
| Class G | Investors and people running a business or consultancy |
| Class I | Approved religious and charitable work |
| Class K | People of independent means, including retirees |

Spouses and children come in on **dependant passes**. Once you have a permit
you must also register as a foreign national, which gets you a **foreigner
certificate** (the "alien ID"). You will need that card, or the permit, to get a
[[KRA PIN]], a bank account and a long-term lease.

Permit fees are high and processing can take months, so start early and keep
copies of everything you submit.

## Arriving

Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) is in Embakasi, south-east of the
city. Getting to Westlands or Kilimani takes 30 minutes on a clear
[Expressway](/guides/getting-around-nairobi#the-expressway) and well over an
hour at rush hour on Mombasa Road.

- **Ride-hailing** - Uber, Bolt and Little all work at JKIA and quote a price
  up front.
- **Airport taxis** - agree the price before you get in.
- **A hotel pickup** is worth it if you land late at night.

Nairobi sits at around 1,700 metres, so it is cooler than people expect,
especially from June to August. The long rains are roughly March to May and the
short rains October to December.

## The first fortnight

In roughly this order:

1. **A SIM card.** Safaricom, Airtel and Telkom all sell SIMs in malls and at
   the airport. SIM registration is compulsory, so bring your passport. Choose
   Safaricom if you want [[M-Pesa]], which you will.
2. **[[M-Pesa]].** It is registered on the same SIM. Rent, bills, shops and
   matatu fares all run on it. See [M-Pesa and mobile money](/guides/mpesa-and-mobile-money).
3. **Somewhere short-term.** A serviced apartment or short let for the first
   month lets you view flats before signing anything. See
   [Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing).
4. **Your paperwork.** An [[eCitizen]] account, a [[KRA PIN]] and your
   foreigner certificate. See [eCitizen, KRA PIN and IDs](/guides/ecitizen-kra-pin-and-ids).
5. **A bank account.** See [Opening a bank account](/guides/opening-a-bank-account).
6. **Health cover.** Employees are enrolled in [[SHA]]; most expatriates also
   buy private insurance. See [Healthcare in Nairobi](/guides/healthcare-in-nairobi).

## Money on day one

The currency is the Kenyan shilling (KES). Cards work in malls, supermarkets
and restaurants in the better-off areas. Everywhere else it is M-Pesa or cash.
ATMs are everywhere; withdraw inside a mall or bank hall rather than at a
street machine at night.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting work on a visitor's entry.** It is illegal, and it can end in
  deportation.
- **Paying a deposit for a flat you have not seen.** Fake listings are common.
  See [Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing#scams).
- **Underestimating the traffic.** Choose where you live by where you will be
  at 8am. See [Nairobi's neighbourhoods](/guides/neighbourhoods-of-nairobi).

Moving to Nairobi is possible for work, study, family, or any number of reasons. This guide covers the practical steps and what to expect.

## Before you arrive

### Visas and permits

Kenya offers several visa options:

- **Tourist visa** (90 days) — issued on arrival for most nationalities. KES 2,000 (approximately USD 15).
- **Single-entry work permit** — for employed foreigners. Your employer typically handles this.
- **Multi-entry resident permit** — for long-term residence. Requires proof of income or employment.
- **Student pass** — for full-time study at accredited institutions.

Visas are processed at Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA) or online via the eCitizen platform.

### Residence permit

If you plan to stay longer than 90 days, you will need a residence permit. This is handled by the Department of Immigration.

**What you'll need:**
- Passport (valid for 6+ months beyond your intended stay)
- Proof of financial support (bank statements, employment letter)
- Accommodation proof (rental agreement or letter)
- Medical clearance (yellow fever certificate recommended)
- Police clearance from your home country
- Completed application forms

Processing takes 2-4 weeks. Cost varies by permit type, typically KES 5,000-20,000.

### Huduma Number

Get a Huduma Number (national ID) as soon as possible. It simplifies banking, SIM cards, and government services. Register at any Huduma Centre (there are several in Nairobi).

## Arriving in Nairobi

### Getting from the airport

Jomo Kenyatta International Airport is 18 km southeast of central Nairobi. Options:

- **Taxi/Uber** — KES 1,500-2,500 to central areas. Use Uber for fixed pricing.
- **Airport shuttle** — Hotels and hostels often provide pickup services.
- **Bus** — Cheaper but slow. Not recommended with luggage on first arrival.

### First week tasks

1. **Get a SIM card** — Visit any telecom shop (Safaricom, Airtel, Jio). You'll need your passport and Huduma Number.
2. **Open a bank account** — Most banks open accounts in 1-2 days with passport + residence proof.
3. **Register with your embassy** — Many embassies keep a register of citizens.
4. **Find accommodation** — Use Airbnb for the first month while you search. See [finding-housing](/guides/finding-housing) for longer-term options.
5. **Get to know your neighborhood** — Explore on foot during daylight hours.

## Safety and orientation

### Safety basics

Nairobi is generally safe for expats, but as with any major city, follow common sense:

- Avoid walking alone at night
- Don't display expensive items (phones, cameras, jewelry)
- Use official taxis or Uber rather than street taxis
- Keep copies of important documents separate from originals
- Register valuables with the police if you're a resident

### Getting around

- **Matatu** — The local minibus network. Cheap (KES 50-100) but chaotic for newcomers.
- **Uber/Bolt** — KES 400-1,000+ depending on distance. Most reliable for first months.
- **Walking** — Westlands, Kilimani, and Parklands are walkable. Downtown and informal areas less so.
- **Personal vehicle** — Possible but requires international driving permit, Kenyan driving license, and car insurance.

## Cost of living checklist

- **Rent** (one-bedroom, central area): KES 25,000-80,000/month
- **Meals** (local restaurant): KES 200-500
- **Meals** (mid-range restaurant): KES 1,000-2,500
- **Utilities** (basic): KES 3,000-5,000/month
- **Internet** (home): KES 2,000-3,500/month

## Building your network

- **Expat groups** — Several meetup groups, both online and in-person. Ask at your workplace or hotel.
- **Sports clubs** — Football, rugby, golf, tennis clubs welcome newcomers.
- **Coworking spaces** — Good places to meet other professionals: Impact Hub, Nairobi Garage, Britam Tower.
- **Social events** — Pubs, clubs, and restaurants in Westlands and Kilimani host regular expat meet-ups.

## Common pitfalls

- **Trusting unofficial guides** — Official taxis and Uber are safer.
- **Not getting documentation sorted** — Visas and permits matter for everything from banking to employment.
- **Underestimating cost of living** — Expat-friendly areas are expensive. Budget accordingly.
- **Not trying local food** — Nyama choma, ugali, and sukuma wiki are cheap, delicious, and genuine.

Kenya's **Alcoholic Drinks Control Act 2010** - often called the "Mututho
law" after the MP who drove it - sets the national rules, and counties
license bars and set their own rules on top. In practice enforcement varies,
but it is worth knowing where the lines are.

## The drinking age

You must be **{{ ALCOHOL_MINIMUM_AGE }}** to buy alcohol, and selling to
anyone younger is an offence. Supermarkets, bars and clubs may ask for ID,
so carry it.

## When alcohol can be sold

The national law limits **bars** to selling in the evening on weekdays and
from the afternoon at weekends and on public holidays, until late at night.
Nairobi County's own rules and the type of licence - bar, restaurant,
nightclub, hotel - change the exact hours, and nightclubs are licensed to
open later. Supermarkets and wine shops sell during their opening hours
under their own licences.

The hours are often not enforced, but police crackdowns do happen,
especially on bars near schools and in residential areas.

## Where you can't drink

- **In public** - drinking on the street, in parks or in a [[matatu]] can
  get you arrested.
- **Near schools** - bars aren't allowed close to schools.
- **At work and while driving.**

## Drink-driving

Police in Nairobi run breathalyser checks - known as **"alcoblow"** - on
main roads, especially at night, weekends and in December. Driving over the
limit can mean arrest, a night in the cells, a court appearance and a fine,
and your insurer may refuse a claim. Use a ride-hailing app or a designated
driver. See [Ride-hailing and delivery apps](/guides/ride-hailing-and-delivery-apps-in-nairobi)
and [Traffic offences and fines](/guides/traffic-offences-and-fines-in-kenya).

## Illicit and fake drinks

Cheap spirits from unknown makers, and illicit brews, have caused deaths in
Kenya. Buy from licensed shops and bars, check that bottles have an **excise
stamp** from KRA and an unbroken seal, and avoid unlabelled drinks.

## Getting help

The National Authority for the Campaign Against Alcohol and Drug Abuse
(**NACADA**) runs a free helpline, **1192**, for anyone worried about their
own or someone else's drinking. See
[Therapy and mental health in Nairobi](/guides/therapy-and-mental-health-in-nairobi).

Mobile data is the main way most Kenyans get online, and it can eat a
budget quickly. A few habits and settings make a bundle last much longer.

## Choosing bundles

- **Buy bundles** - never browse on airtime without one. **Out-of-bundle**
  data is charged at a much higher rate.
- **Compare validity** - daily, weekly and monthly bundles, and bundles
  that don't expire, suit different users. Bigger bundles are usually
  cheaper per gigabyte.
- **Night and app bundles** - cheaper data at night, or for specific apps,
  can suit heavy users.
- **Check the network's own menus and app** for bundle offers and your
  balance. See [Phones and SIM cards](/guides/phones-and-sim-cards).
- **Stop out-of-bundle browsing** - most networks let you block data when a
  bundle runs out. Turn this on.

## Phone settings

- **Data saver mode** - restricts background data on Android and iPhone.
- **Turn off automatic updates** on mobile data; update apps and the phone
  on Wi-Fi.
- **Turn off auto-play** of videos in social apps, and lower video quality.
- **Limit background data** for apps you don't need constantly.
- **Check data usage** in settings to find the apps using the most.

## App habits

- **Video** uses the most data. Download on Wi-Fi to watch later.
- **Messaging apps** - turn off automatic download of photos and videos.
- **Maps** - download offline maps of Nairobi.
- **Lite apps** - many apps have lighter versions.
- **Cloud backups** - set them to Wi-Fi only.

## Wi-Fi and fibre

- **Free Wi-Fi** in cafés, malls and offices is useful, but avoid banking
  on public networks.
- **Home fibre** is far cheaper per gigabyte if you stream or work from
  home. See [Home internet in Nairobi](/guides/home-internet-in-nairobi).
- **Hotspot** sharing uses your bundle fast.

## Visitors

For short visits, a local SIM or eSIM with a data bundle is usually
cheapest. See [eSIMs and SIM cards for visitors](/guides/esims-and-sim-cards-for-visitors).

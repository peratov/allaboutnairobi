Within two or three hours of Nairobi there are crater rims, forested ridges
and Kenya's highest mountain. Weekend hiking is a big part of Nairobi life,
and hiking groups make it easy without a car. Fees and opening times change;
check with the park or forest before you go.

## The hikes

**Ngong Hills** *(moderate, half a day)* - a ridge of knuckle-shaped peaks
south-west of the city, with views over the Rift Valley on one side and
Nairobi on the other. Managed by the Kenya Forest Service; pay at the gate
and **hike with a ranger or a group**.

**Mount Longonot** *(moderately hard, most of a day)* - a steep climb to the
rim of a volcano above Lake Naivasha, then an optional loop around the crater.
A Kenya Wildlife Service park, with an entry fee. Start early: the climb is
exposed and hot by midday.

**Ol Donyo Sabuk** *(moderate)* - a forested mountain near Thika, in a KWS
national park, often combined with Fourteen Falls.

**Elephant Hill** *(hard, a long day)* - a demanding climb in the Aberdare
range, usually done with an organised group leaving Nairobi before dawn.

**Kijabe and the Rift Valley escarpment** *(easy to moderate)* - forest
walks and viewpoints over the valley floor.

**Sleeping Warrior** *(moderate)* - a ridge above Lake Elementaita, inside a
conservancy, with views of flamingo lakes.

**Mount Kenya** *(hard, three days or more)* - Point Lenana is reached by
fit walkers without climbing gear, on routes such as Sirimon and Chogoria,
with a licensed guide and porters. It is high enough for **altitude
sickness**: go slowly, and go down if symptoms worsen.

See [Day trips from Nairobi](/guides/day-trips-from-nairobi) and
[Weekend getaways](/guides/weekend-getaways-from-nairobi) for the places
around these hikes.

## Hiking groups

Many Nairobi hiking clubs run weekend trips with transport from town, a
guide and park fees in one price. They suit newcomers and anyone without a
car. Check what is included, how hard the hike really is, and that the group
uses official guides and pays park fees.

## What to bring

- **Water** - more than you expect; there is little on most routes
- **Sun protection** - hat, sunscreen, sunglasses; the sun is fierce at
  altitude
- **A warm and waterproof layer** - weather turns fast, especially in the
  long rains
- **Proper shoes** with grip; the volcanic soil on Longonot is loose
- **Cash** for gate fees where M-Pesa fails, and your ID

## Safety

- Hike in **groups** and with **rangers or guides** where the park advises.
- **Start early** and turn back in time to finish before dark.
- Tell someone your plan, and keep your phone charged.

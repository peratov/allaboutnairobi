A **Class D permit** lets a foreign national work for a specific employer in
Kenya. It is the permit most employees on expatriate contracts need. Your
employer applies, but you supply most of the documents, and delays usually
come from missing papers.

## Who qualifies

The permit is for someone offered a specific job by a specific employer, who
has skills or qualifications that are not readily available in Kenya. The
employer has to show that it tried and failed to fill the job locally.

Citizens of East African Community partner states are covered by different,
simpler rules, and do not pay the permit fee.

## Fees

- **Processing fee:** KES {{ CLASS_D_PROCESSING_FEE|shillings }},
  non-refundable, paid when applying.
- **Permit fee:** KES {{ CLASS_D_PERMIT_FEE|shillings }} **per year** of the
  permit, paid after approval - within 30 days, or the approval can lapse.

Employers usually pay both, but check your contract. Some also ask for a
security bond from an insurer.

## Documents

The application goes through the **eFNS** (Foreign Nationals Services) portal
on [[eCitizen]]. It normally includes:

- **Application forms** - Form 25, and Form 27 for the employer.
- **A cover letter** from the employer to the Director General of
  Immigration, explaining the role.
- **Your passport** and current immigration status, and a passport photo.
- **Certified academic and professional certificates**, and your CV.
- **The employer's registration certificate** and its **tax compliance
  certificate**.
- **Details of a Kenyan understudy** - a Kenyan employee who will be trained
  to take over the role - with their qualifications.
- **Evidence the job was advertised locally** and could not be filled.
- **Clearances** from professional bodies, where the job is regulated.

Documents in other languages need certified English translations.

## How long it takes

Plan for **one to three months** from a complete application. Missing
documents, queries and busy periods make it longer. Do not start work until
the permit is approved - working on a visitor's entry is illegal.

## After approval

1. **Pay the permit fee** within the deadline.
2. **Have the permit endorsed** in your passport.
3. **Register as a foreign national** and get your **foreigner certificate**,
   which you need for a [[KRA PIN]], a bank account and a lease. See
   [eCitizen, KRA PIN and IDs](/guides/ecitizen-kra-pin-and-ids).
4. **Apply for [dependant passes](/guides/dependant-passes)** for your spouse and children.

## Renewals and changing jobs

- **Renew** well before expiry - start at least three months ahead.
- **A new employer means a new permit.** The permit is tied to the employer
  named on it.
- **If the job ends**, the permit ends too. Ask Immigration about your status
  before your last day.

## Other permits

- **Class G** - investors and people running their own business.
- **Class N** - digital nomads working for employers or clients abroad. See
  [Working remotely from Nairobi](/guides/working-remotely-from-nairobi).
- **Class K** - people of independent means, including retirees.

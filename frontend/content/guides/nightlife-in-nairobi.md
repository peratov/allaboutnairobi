Nairobi goes out a lot, and late. Weekends start on Thursday for many people,
and a Sunday afternoon of nyama choma and music is an institution of its own.

## Where people go

- **Westlands** - the busiest area, with rooftop bars, lounges and clubs
  within walking distance of each other. Crowded and loud on Friday and
  Saturday nights.
- **Kilimani and Kileleshwa** - bars and restaurants, a little calmer.
- **Karen and Lang'ata** - garden bars and restaurants, better for long
  afternoons than late nights.
- **The CBD** - older bars and live music, busy after work.
- **The estates** - every neighbourhood has its local bars, often with the
  best nyama choma.

Listings change fast. Social media and friends are the best guide to what is
on.

## Music

Nairobi's own music - gengetone, Afro-pop, benga and more - sits alongside
Nigerian Afrobeats, amapiano and East African bongo. Live bands play in many
bars at weekends, and there are regular concerts and festivals. See
[Events](/events).

## The rules

- **The legal drinking age is 18**, and bouncers may ask for ID.
- **Drink-driving is enforced.** Police run breathalyser checks, often at
  night and at weekends and holidays. Do not drive after drinking.
- **Smoking** is banned in most public places; bars with smoking areas signpost
  them.

## Staying safe

- **Watch your drink.** Drink spiking happens, often to rob people. Do not
  leave drinks unattended or accept drinks you did not see poured.
- **Get home by ride-hailing** booked from inside the venue, and check the car
  and plate. Avoid walking at night, even short distances.
- **Carry little.** Keep your phone out of sight on the street and one card
  and some cash, not everything.
- **Agree on the bill** in unfamiliar places before ordering rounds.

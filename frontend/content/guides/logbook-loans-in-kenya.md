A **logbook loan** lets you borrow against a car you own. The lender holds
an interest in the car's logbook until you repay, and you keep driving it.
They are quick - often a few days - but they are expensive, and if you fall
behind you can lose the car.

## How it works

1. **Valuation** - the lender values the car; you can usually borrow only
   part of what it is worth.
2. **Joint registration** - the car is registered jointly in your name and
   the lender's on the NTSA system, so you can't sell it without them.
3. **Tracking and insurance** - most lenders fit a tracker (at your cost)
   and require comprehensive insurance, with the lender noted on the policy.
4. **Repayment** - monthly instalments over months or a few years.
5. **Release** - once you have paid in full, the lender removes their name
   from the logbook. Get this done promptly.

## What it really costs

The interest rate is only part of it. Ask for, in writing:

- the **interest rate**, and whether it is **flat** or **reducing balance**
  (a flat rate costs far more than it sounds)
- **processing, valuation and legal fees**
- the **tracker** and its monthly fee
- **insurance** costs
- **late-payment penalties** and the cost of **repaying early**

Then compare the **total you will repay** with a bank or SACCO loan. Use the
[loan calculator](/tools/loan-repayment-calculator) to see the true cost of
a flat rate.

## Check the lender is licensed

Since a 2024 change to the law, non-deposit-taking lenders - logbook lenders
among them - must be **licensed by the Central Bank of Kenya**. Check the
lender on the CBK's list before you sign. See
[Checking a financial firm is licensed](/guides/checking-a-financial-firm-is-licensed-in-kenya).

## If you can't pay

- **Talk to the lender early.** Many will reschedule rather than repossess.
- **Repossession** - lenders use auctioneers to take the car if you default.
  They must follow the law and your contract, including giving notice.
- **Auctions** - a repossessed car is usually sold for less than its value,
  and you may still owe the difference.
- Default is reported to the credit bureaus. See
  [Checking your CRB status](/guides/checking-your-crb-status).

## Alternatives

- A **SACCO loan** against your savings - usually much cheaper. See
  [SACCOs in Nairobi](/guides/saccos-in-nairobi).
- A **bank personal loan** if you are salaried.
- Avoid stacking mobile loans to cover a logbook loan. See
  [The true cost of mobile loans](/guides/mobile-loans-true-cost).

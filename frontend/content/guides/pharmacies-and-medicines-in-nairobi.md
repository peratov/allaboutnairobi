Nairobi has a pharmacy - a "chemist" - on almost every street. Pharmacists
are a useful first stop for minor illnesses, but quality varies, so it is worth
knowing what to look for.

## Choosing a pharmacy

Pharmacies and the people who run them are regulated by the **Pharmacy and
Poisons Board** (PPB). A registered pharmacy should:

- display its **premises licence** and the practising licence of the
  pharmacist or pharmaceutical technologist in charge
- have a qualified person on duty who can advise you
- give you a **receipt** with the name of the medicine

You can check a premises or a pharmacist on the PPB's website. Pharmacy
chains and pharmacies inside hospitals and malls are generally reliable;
roadside kiosks and hawkers selling medicines are not.

## Prescriptions

By law, many medicines - including **antibiotics**, strong painkillers and
most chronic-disease medicines - need a prescription. In practice some
pharmacies sell them without one. It is better not to:

- the wrong antibiotic, or a short course, can fail and add to drug
  resistance
- a doctor can check whether you need medicine at all

For minor problems - colds, mild pain, indigestion, rashes - a pharmacist can
advise and sell suitable over-the-counter medicine.

## Saving money

- **Ask for the generic**: the same active ingredient from another maker is
  often much cheaper than the brand.
- **Compare prices** between pharmacies; they vary.
- **Check your cover**: [SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha) and many private
  [health insurance](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya) plans pay for
  prescribed medicines at contracted pharmacies.
- For long-term medicines, ask about a **monthly supply**.

## Fake and substandard medicines

Counterfeit and substandard medicines exist in Kenya. Reduce the risk:

- buy from a registered pharmacy, not a market stall or social media seller
- check the packaging: spelling, batch number, expiry date, and that the seal
  is intact
- if a medicine looks, tastes or works differently from usual, stop and ask
- report suspect medicines and bad side effects to the **PPB**

## Late at night

Many pharmacies close in the evening. The pharmacies at the large private
hospitals are usually open around the clock, and some pharmacies in malls and
on main roads stay open late. Several chains also deliver. In an emergency,
call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** or go to an emergency department - see
[Emergencies in Nairobi](/guides/emergencies-in-nairobi).

## Bringing medicines into Kenya

Bring personal medicines in their original packaging, with a copy of your
**prescription** or a doctor's letter, and only a reasonable supply.
Controlled medicines, such as strong painkillers and some sleeping and ADHD
medicines, may need permission from the PPB - check before you travel.

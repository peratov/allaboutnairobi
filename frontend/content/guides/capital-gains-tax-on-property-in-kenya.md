When you sell land, a house or a flat in Kenya, the **seller** owes
**capital gains tax (CGT)** at **{{ CAPITAL_GAINS_TAX_RATE|percent }}%** of
the gain. It has to be paid before the transfer is registered, so it is not a
tax you can deal with later. This is general information, not tax advice.

## Working out the gain

**Gain = what you sell it for - the adjusted cost.**

The **adjusted cost** is what you paid for the property plus the costs of
buying, improving and selling it:

- the purchase price
- [stamp duty](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator), legal fees, valuation and
  survey fees when you bought
- the cost of **improvements** - building a house on the plot, an extension,
  a borehole - but not repairs and upkeep
- costs of selling, such as the agent's commission and legal fees

Keep **every receipt** from the day you buy. Without them KRA can treat most
of the sale price as gain.

**Example.** A plot bought for KES 2,000,000 with KES 150,000 of buying
costs, and sold for KES 3,500,000 with KES 100,000 of selling costs: the gain
is 3,500,000 - 2,250,000 = KES 1,250,000, and the tax is
{{ CAPITAL_GAINS_TAX_RATE|percent }}% of that.

## Exemptions

No CGT is due on:

- your **own home**, where you lived in it for at least the three years before
  the sale
- **inheritance** - transferring a deceased person's property to the heirs
  (see [Death and succession](/guides/death-and-succession-in-kenya))
- transfers between **spouses**, including on divorce, and to **immediate
  family**
- some small **agricultural land** outside towns, within limits in the law
- a **sale at a loss** - there is no gain to tax

Ask a lawyer or tax adviser whether an exemption fits before relying on it;
the conditions are strict.

## Paying it

1. The seller (or their lawyer) files the **CGT return on iTax** and pays,
   on or before the transfer.
2. The payment receipt goes with the transfer documents. The land registry
   will not register a transfer without proof that CGT is dealt with.
3. Paying late brings a **penalty and interest**.

In practice the seller's advocate handles this as part of the sale, often
paying it from the purchase money.

## Selling shares in a property company

Selling shares in a company that owns land is also caught, so this is not a
way around the tax.

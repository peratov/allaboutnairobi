Kenya's routine childhood vaccines are **free** at public health facilities
under the national immunisation programme. They protect against serious
diseases such as measles, polio, whooping cough and pneumonia, and schools
often ask for proof.

## The routine schedule

The usual schedule is roughly:

| Age | Vaccines |
| --- | --- |
| At birth | BCG (tuberculosis) and the first oral polio drops |
| 6, 10 and 14 weeks | Pentavalent (including diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough and hepatitis B), oral polio, pneumococcal and rotavirus |
| Around 14 weeks and later | Injectable polio |
| 9 months | Measles-rubella, and yellow fever in some counties |
| 18 months | Measles-rubella second dose |
| From 10 years (girls) | HPV, to prevent cervical cancer |

The **malaria vaccine** is given in selected high-risk areas. The schedule
is updated from time to time, so follow the dates the clinic writes on
your child's card.

## Where to go

- **Public hospitals and health centres** - free vaccines at the child
  welfare clinic, usually on set days of the week.
- **Private hospitals and clinics** - offer the routine vaccines and some
  extra ones not in the free programme, at a cost.
- **Outreach and campaigns** - mass vaccination drives in schools and
  communities.

See [Healthcare in Nairobi](/guides/healthcare-in-nairobi) and
[Giving birth in Nairobi](/guides/giving-birth-in-nairobi).

## The clinic card

Your child gets a **mother and child health booklet** at birth or the first
visit. Bring it to every appointment - it records vaccines, growth and the
next date. Keep it safe; schools and travel may need it.

## If you miss a dose

Go to the clinic as soon as you can. Missed vaccines can usually be caught
up; you don't start again.

## After vaccination

Mild fever, fussiness or a sore spot are common and pass in a day or two.
Seek care for a high fever, fits, difficulty breathing or a swelling that
gets worse.

## Extra vaccines

Doctors may recommend others - such as chickenpox, hepatitis A, typhoid or
flu - especially for travel. See
[Vaccinations and malaria](/guides/vaccinations-and-malaria-in-kenya).

Nairobi is young for a capital: barely 125 years old. It grew from a railway
camp into the largest city in East Africa, and much of that history is still
visible from its streets.

## The name

The name comes from the Maasai **Enkare Nairobi** - "the place of cool
waters" - for the stream that ran through the swampy plain where the city now
stands.

## A railway town, 1899

In 1899 the **Uganda Railway**, being built from Mombasa to Lake Victoria,
reached this flat, well-watered spot at the foot of the highlands. It became a
depot and camp for the railway, and a town grew around it. The
[Nairobi Railway Museum](/map#place/nairobi-railway-museum), beside the
station, tells the story.

## Colonial capital

In 1905 Nairobi replaced Mombasa as the capital of the British East Africa
Protectorate, which became the colony of Kenya. The city was laid out along
racial lines: Europeans in the western and northern hills, Asians in areas
such as Parklands and the old bazaar, and Africans in the eastern estates.
The pattern still shapes Nairobi's neighbourhoods. Nairobi was granted city
status in 1950.

## The struggle for independence

Nairobi was central to the fight for independence, including the Mau Mau
uprising of the 1950s. Kenya became independent on **12 December 1963**, with
Jomo Kenyatta as its first leader - the date celebrated as
[Jamhuri Day](/guides/public-holidays-in-kenya).

## A regional capital

After independence Nairobi became East Africa's hub for business, diplomacy
and travel. The [KICC](/map#place/kicc) opened in 1973, and the United Nations
Environment Programme made Nairobi its headquarters in the 1970s, one of the
few UN headquarters in the global south - today the
[UN Office at Nairobi](/map#place/un-office-at-nairobi) in Gigiri.

## Hard days

The city has lived through difficult moments, including the 1998 bombing of
the US embassy and the 2013 attack on [Westgate](/map#place/westgate). Both
are remembered with memorials.

## Nairobi today

Under the 2010 Constitution, Nairobi became **Nairobi City County**, one of
Kenya's 47 counties, with its own governor and assembly from 2013. The 2019
census counted over 4.3 million people, and the wider metropolitan area is
home to many more. It is a centre for technology, finance, the media and the
arts - and it keeps growing.

[[M-Pesa]] is how Nairobi pays. Rent, electricity, school fees, the
[[mama mboga]], a matatu fare and splitting a bill all go through it. You will
use it more than your bank card.

## Getting it

M-Pesa comes with a Safaricom line. Register at a Safaricom shop or agent with
your ID, or your passport if you are a foreigner, and set a four-digit PIN. You
can then use:

- **The M-Pesa app**, or the M-Pesa section of the MySafaricom app.
- **The SIM menu** on any phone, or **\*334#**.

Airtel Money, on Airtel lines, works the same way. Fewer businesses accept it.

## Ways to pay

- **Send Money** - to any phone number.
- **Lipa na M-Pesa, [[Paybill]]** - for bills. You enter the business number
  and an account number: your meter number, invoice number or house number.
- **Lipa na M-Pesa, Buy Goods ([[till number]])** - for shops, restaurants and
  taxis. Just the till number and the amount.
- **Pochi la Biashara** - some small traders take payments to a business
  wallet on their personal number.
- **Withdraw cash** at an M-Pesa agent, the green-signed shops found on almost
  every street.

Always check the name the confirmation screen shows before you enter your PIN.

## Limits

For a standard personal account:

- **KES {{ MPESA_MAX_TRANSACTION|shillings }}** per transaction
- **KES {{ MPESA_MAX_DAILY|shillings }}** per day, and the same as your maximum
  balance

Anything bigger goes through a bank.

## Fees

Sending to another M-Pesa user and withdrawing cash are charged on a sliding
scale. Small amounts are free or nearly so, and the fee grows in bands.
Paying a till is free for the customer, and paybill charges depend on the
business. Safaricom publishes the current tariff on its website.

## Built on M-Pesa

- **[[Fuliza]]** - an overdraft that lets a payment go through when your
  balance is short. It charges a daily fee until repaid. Handy in an
  emergency, expensive if you rely on it.
- **M-Shwari** (with NCBA) and **KCB M-Pesa** - savings accounts and small
  loans inside M-Pesa.
- **M-Pesa Global** - send to and receive from some other countries.

## Scams

- **"I sent you money by mistake."** A text that looks like an M-Pesa
  confirmation, then a call asking you to send it back. Check your real
  balance. A genuine mistaken payment can be reversed by Safaricom, and you
  never need to send anything back yourself.
- **Fake Safaricom calls** asking for your PIN or a code sent to your phone.
  Safaricom never asks for your PIN.
- **SIM swaps** - a criminal takes over your number to empty your M-Pesa and
  bank accounts. If your line suddenly loses signal for no reason, call
  Safaricom from another phone straight away.

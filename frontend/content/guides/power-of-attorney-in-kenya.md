A **power of attorney** is a legal document that lets someone - the
"attorney" or "donee" - act on your behalf: sell land, sign documents,
deal with a bank or manage property while you are abroad or unable to do it
yourself. It is a powerful document, so choose carefully.

## Types

- **General power of attorney** - lets the attorney act for you in most
  matters. Broad and risky.
- **Specific (special) power of attorney** - limited to named tasks, such
  as selling one plot, collecting rent from a named house, or dealing with a
  particular bank account. Safer, and what most people need.

## Choosing an attorney

- Someone you trust completely - they can sign in your name.
- Give **only the powers they need**, for a limited time if possible.
- Ask them to keep records and report back.
- Many people abroad appoint a close relative, or an advocate.

See [Kenyans abroad: managing things from afar](/guides/kenyans-abroad-managing-things-from-afar).

## Making one in Kenya

1. An **advocate** drafts it, naming you, the attorney and exactly what they
   can do, with property details such as title numbers.
2. You sign it in front of the advocate, with your ID.
3. **Stamp duty** is assessed and paid through KRA.
4. It is **registered** - with the Registrar of Documents, or at the
   **Lands Registry** if it will be used for land.

## Signing it abroad

Kenya is not part of the Apostille Convention, so a power of attorney
signed abroad must be:

1. signed before a **notary public** in that country, and
2. **authenticated** by the Kenyan embassy or high commission there (some
   embassies witness documents themselves).

It is then stamped and registered in Kenya. Check the deadlines for
stamping and registration with your advocate once the document arrives:
leaving it too long can cause problems.

## Using it for land

Land registries will only accept a power of attorney that is **registered**
and covers the transaction. For a sale or transfer, the registry will check
the attorney's ID and the registered power. See
[Land search on Ardhisasa](/guides/land-search-on-ardhisasa) and
[Building from abroad](/guides/building-from-abroad).

## Revoking it

You can cancel a power of attorney at any time by a **revocation** document,
registered in the same way. Tell the attorney, and anyone who relied on it
- banks, the land registry, tenants - in writing. A power of attorney also
ends when you die; after that, your estate is handled under succession law.

## Fraud warning

Forged powers of attorney are used in land fraud. If you are buying land
from someone acting under a power of attorney, check it is registered, speak
to the owner directly if possible, and use an advocate.

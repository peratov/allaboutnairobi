Small businesses in Kenya with sales between KES
{{ TURNOVER_TAX_LOWER|shillings }} and KES {{ TURNOVER_TAX_UPPER|shillings }}
a year fall under **turnover tax (TOT)** by default. It is simple, but it is
not always the cheapest option. This guide shows how to compare. It is general
information, not tax advice.

## The two systems

**Turnover tax** is {{ TURNOVER_TAX_RATE|percent }}% of **gross sales**, paid
monthly on [[iTax]]. It is a final tax: you cannot deduct any expenses, and it
is due even if the business made a loss.

**Income tax** is charged on **profit** - sales minus allowable business
expenses - filed once a year. A sole trader pays at the same graduated rates as
employees (10% up to 35%), after personal relief. A company pays corporation tax
of {{ CORPORATE_TAX_RATE|percent }}% on its profit.

## The rule of thumb

- **Low profit margins** (for example a shop selling goods bought for resale)
  - income tax is usually cheaper, because TOT taxes sales you mostly spent.
- **High profit margins** (for example consulting or services with few costs)
  - turnover tax is usually cheaper.

## Worked examples

A sole trader with sales of **KES 3,000,000** a year pays turnover tax of
**KES {{ (3000000 * TURNOVER_TAX_RATE / 100)|shillings }}**, whatever the
profit.

Under income tax, what they pay depends on the profit:

- **Profit of KES 300,000** (a 10% margin): roughly **KES 3,000** after
  personal relief. Much less than TOT.
- **Profit of KES 600,000** (a 20% margin): roughly **KES 88,600** after
  personal relief. More than TOT.

These use the individual income tax bands and personal relief, and ignore
other income. If you also have a salary, business profit is taxed on top of it
at your highest rate, which makes TOT relatively more attractive.

Work out your own figures with the
[turnover tax calculator](/tools/turnover-tax-calculator) and
[salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator).

## Choosing income tax instead

A business that qualifies for TOT can **elect to be taxed under the ordinary
income tax rules instead**, by notifying KRA. You then:

- **keep proper books** of sales and expenses,
- **support expenses with eTIMS invoices** - see
  [eTIMS explained](/guides/etims-for-small-businesses),
- **file an annual return**, and pay instalment tax where it applies.

Take advice before switching: the cheaper system on paper is only cheaper if
you keep the records to back it up.

## VAT

Neither system replaces VAT. Businesses with sales over KES
{{ VAT_REGISTRATION_THRESHOLD|shillings }} a year must register for VAT; check
with KRA how VAT registration affects your turnover tax position. See the
[VAT calculator](/tools/vat-calculator).

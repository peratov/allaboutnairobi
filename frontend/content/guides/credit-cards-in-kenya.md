Credit cards are much less common in Kenya than in many countries - M-Pesa and
debit cards do most of the work. But a credit card is useful for travel,
online shopping in foreign currency, hotel and car hire deposits, and
emergencies.

## Who issues them

Most large banks issue Visa and Mastercard credit cards, usually to customers
who already bank with them. You typically need:

- **An account with the bank**, often with your salary paid into it.
- **Payslips and bank statements**, and your ID and [[KRA PIN]].
- **A minimum income**, depending on the card.
- **A clean CRB record.** See [Checking your CRB status](/guides/checking-your-crb-status).

## Interest and fees

- **Interest** on unpaid balances is charged monthly and adds up to a high
  annual rate.
- **Pay the full balance** each month and most cards charge no interest on
  purchases.
- **Cash withdrawals** are charged interest from day one, plus a fee.
- **Foreign transactions** carry a currency conversion charge.
- **Annual fees** apply to many cards, especially premium ones.

## Alternatives

- **Debit cards** from your bank work online and abroad for most things.
- **Virtual cards** - banks and Safaricom offer virtual Visa cards linked to
  your account or to M-Pesa (such as M-Pesa GlobalPay), for paying online
  sites that do not take M-Pesa.
- **Dollar accounts with a debit card** for spending in dollars without
  converting each time. See [Opening a bank account](/guides/opening-a-bank-account).

## Using cards safely

- **Turn on transaction alerts** by SMS or in the app.
- **Don't let a card out of your sight** in restaurants and petrol stations -
  ask for the card machine to be brought to you.
- **Use virtual cards** for online subscriptions and unfamiliar sites.
- **Report a lost card immediately** through the bank's app or line.

## Credit history

Using a credit card and paying on time builds a positive record with credit
reference bureaus, which helps with loans and mortgages later.

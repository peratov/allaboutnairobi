Diabetes and high blood pressure (hypertension) are increasingly common in
Kenya, and many people who have them do not know. Both are easy to check,
treatable, and far less dangerous when caught early.

## Getting checked

- **Blood pressure** can be checked at any clinic, at many pharmacies, and at
  free screening events. Adults should check at least once a year.
- **Blood sugar** is checked with a quick finger-prick test or a lab test
  such as HbA1c.
- **Community health promoters** screen for both in many neighbourhoods.
- Get checked more often if you are overweight, over 40, pregnant, or have a
  family history.

See [Health checks and screening](/guides/health-checks-and-screening-in-nairobi).

## Warning signs

- **Diabetes** - thirst, passing urine often, tiredness, weight loss,
  blurred vision, slow-healing wounds.
- **High blood pressure** usually has **no symptoms** - which is why
  checking matters.
- **Emergencies** - chest pain, sudden weakness on one side, slurred speech,
  confusion or fainting. Call **{{ EMERGENCY_NUMBER }}** or go to a hospital
  straight away. See [Emergencies in Nairobi](/guides/emergencies-in-nairobi).

## Treatment

- **Public hospitals and health centres** run clinics for long-term
  conditions, with regular check-ups and medicines.
- **Private clinics and hospitals** offer specialists and diabetes centres.
  See [Hospitals in Nairobi](/guides/hospitals-in-nairobi).
- Treatment usually means **daily medicines** or insulin, plus regular
  checks of blood pressure, blood sugar, eyes, feet and kidneys.

## Paying for care

- **SHA** covers primary care at contracted facilities, and chronic
  conditions under its benefit packages. Check what your facility offers
  and whether medicines are included. See [Registering for SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha).
- **Private insurance** may cover chronic conditions, sometimes after a
  waiting period. See [Health insurance in Kenya](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- **Generic medicines** from licensed pharmacies cost far less than brands.
  See [Pharmacies and medicines](/guides/pharmacies-and-medicines-in-nairobi).

## Living well

- **Take medicines every day**, even when you feel fine, and don't run
  out - keep a spare week's supply.
- **Eat well** - less salt, sugar and fried food; more vegetables, beans
  and whole grains. See [Eating well in Nairobi](/guides/eating-well-in-nairobi).
- **Move** - a daily walk helps. See [Sport and fitness](/guides/sport-and-fitness-in-nairobi).
- **Limit alcohol** and don't smoke.
- **Check at home** with a blood pressure monitor or glucose meter, sold at
  pharmacies.
- **Keep appointments** for eye and foot checks.

Poultry is one of the most popular side businesses around Nairobi, from a
few birds behind a house in Kitengela or Ruiru to commercial units of
thousands. Demand for eggs and chicken in the city is steady, but feed
costs and disease can wipe out profits, so start informed.

## Choosing your birds

- **Layers** - bred for eggs. They start laying at around five months and
  lay for over a year. Steady income, but a long wait before the first
  sales.
- **Broilers** - bred for meat, ready in about six weeks. Quick returns, but
  you must have buyers lined up for the day they are ready.
- **Improved kienyeji** - hardier local-type birds for eggs and meat, sold
  at higher prices and cheaper to keep, but slower growing.

Buy **day-old chicks** from reputable hatcheries, not unknown sellers.

## Before you start

- **Check local rules** - many estates and gated communities ban livestock,
  and counties may require a permit. Neighbours' complaints about smell and
  noise can end a project.
- **Space** - a well-ventilated, predator-proof house with enough floor
  space, perches and nesting boxes for layers.
- **Water and power** - reliable water, and lighting or heat for chicks.

## Feed and care

- **Feed** is the biggest cost. Use proper commercial feed for each stage -
  chick mash, growers and layers or broiler feeds - from reputable makers.
- **Vaccinate** on schedule against the common diseases. Ask the hatchery
  or a vet for the programme.
- **Biosecurity** - limit visitors, use foot baths, clean and disinfect
  between batches, and isolate sick birds.
- **Records** - feed used, eggs collected, deaths and costs per batch.

A local **livestock extension officer** or vet can visit and advise -
worth it before you buy birds.

## Selling

- **Eggs** - to neighbours, [[duka]] owners, hotels, schools and
  supermarkets; trays sell well if supply is reliable.
- **Broilers** - to butcheries, hotels and restaurants. Agree buyers and
  prices before the batch is ready.
- **Kienyeji** birds and eggs to customers who pay more for them.
- **Spent layers** - old hens are sold for meat at the end of laying.

## Costs and risks

- Chicks, housing, equipment, feed, vaccines, labour and transport all add
  up; feed is usually most of the running cost.
- **Disease** can kill a flock quickly.
- **Feed prices** move with maize prices.
- **Market prices** fall when many farmers sell at once.

Start small, learn, and grow from your own numbers.

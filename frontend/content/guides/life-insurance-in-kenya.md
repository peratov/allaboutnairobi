Life insurance pays out when someone dies - and some policies also pay out as
savings. In Kenya they are sold by insurers, banks and agents, often with a lot
of pressure. Knowing the types helps you buy what you need. This is general
information, not advice.

## The main types

- **Term life** - pays a sum if you die within the term (for example 10 or 20
  years). Nothing if you survive. The cheapest way to protect a family who
  depend on your income.
- **Whole life** - covers you for life, with a payout guaranteed eventually.
  More expensive.
- **Endowment and savings policies** - combine cover with saving, paying out
  at the end of the term. Popular in Kenya, including as **education
  policies** for children's school fees.
- **Last expense cover** - a small sum paid quickly to cover funeral costs.
  Often sold cheaply, and included by some employers and SACCOs.
- **Credit life** - pays off a loan if the borrower dies; banks often require
  it with a mortgage or loan.

## Education policies

Education policies pay out at set dates to fund school or university fees.
Before buying, compare with investing the same monthly amount yourself, for
example in a money market fund - once fees and charges are taken, savings
policies often return less. Their advantages are discipline, the life cover,
and insurance relief. See [Money market funds](/guides/money-market-funds-in-kenya).

## Insurance relief

Resident individuals can claim **insurance relief** on premiums for life,
education and health policies, within an annual limit. Insurers issue a
certificate, and employers usually apply the relief through payroll.

## What to check

- **Is the insurer licensed** by the **Insurance Regulatory Authority**?
- **How much cover** your family would need - enough to repay debts and
  replace your income for some years.
- **Surrender value** - what you get if you stop paying early. It can be
  very little in the first years of savings policies.
- **Exclusions**, such as suicide in the first year or undisclosed illnesses.
- **The fees and the projected returns** on savings policies.

## Tell your family

Make sure the people who would need to claim know the policy exists, the
insurer, and where the documents are. Unclaimed policies are common.

You can live day to day on [[M-Pesa]], but you will want a bank account for
your salary, savings, a card, and anything larger than M-Pesa's limits.

## The banks

The largest banks all have branches across Nairobi, good apps, and M-Pesa
links:

- **KCB** and **Equity** - the two biggest by customers, with branches
  everywhere.
- **Co-operative Bank** and **NCBA** - large local banks. NCBA runs M-Shwari
  with Safaricom.
- **Absa Bank Kenya** (formerly Barclays), **Standard Chartered** and
  **Stanbic** - international banks, popular with expatriates and for
  dollar accounts.
- **I&M** and **Diamond Trust Bank (DTB)** - mid-sized banks with good
  service reputations.

If your employer banks with one of them, opening an account there can make the
paperwork easier.

## What you need

For a **Kenyan citizen**: national ID or Maisha Card, [[KRA PIN]], and
sometimes a passport photo.

For a **foreign resident**, typically:

- Passport
- Work permit, dependant pass or foreigner certificate
- [[KRA PIN]] certificate
- Proof of address: a lease, a utility bill, or a letter from your employer
- An employment letter or payslip, for a salary account
- Passport photos

Non-residents can open accounts at some banks, usually with more paperwork and
a higher minimum balance. Ask the bank first; requirements differ between
branches.

## Types of account

- **Current or salary account** - for your salary, card and bills. Some have a
  monthly fee; salary accounts often waive it.
- **Savings account** - pays a little interest.
- **Foreign currency account** - US dollar, euro and sterling accounts are
  common, useful if you are paid or save in another currency.

## Moving money around

- **PesaLink** - instant transfers between Kenyan banks from your bank app,
  using an account number or a registered phone number.
- **Bank to M-Pesa and back** - every major bank's app sends to M-Pesa. To go
  the other way, use your bank's [[paybill]] with your account number.
- **EFT and RTGS** - slower or larger interbank transfers. RTGS is for large,
  same-day payments.
- **Cheques** still exist, but most landlords and businesses prefer bank
  transfer or M-Pesa.

## Sending money into and out of Kenya

- **International wire (SWIFT)** - reliable but slow, with fees at both ends
  and an exchange-rate margin.
- **Money transfer services** such as Wise, Remitly and WorldRemit are
  usually cheaper and faster. Many can pay straight into M-Pesa.
- **M-Pesa Global** sends to and from M-Pesa in some other countries.

Compare the total cost, meaning the fee plus the exchange rate against the
mid-market rate, rather than the headline fee.

## Saving

For money market funds, Treasury bills and SACCOs, see [Saving and investing in Kenya](/guides/saving-and-investing-in-kenya).

## Cards

Visa and Mastercard debit cards work in malls, supermarkets and online. Use
ATMs inside banks and malls. Tell your bank before travelling abroad, and turn
on transaction alerts.

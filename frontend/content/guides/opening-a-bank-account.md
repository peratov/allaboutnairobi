Banking in Kenya is straightforward. You'll need an account for salary deposits, rent payments, and daily transactions. Mobile money is also huge here — Safaricom's M-Pesa dominates.

## Major banks

**Tier 1 (largest, most reliable):**

- **KCB Bank Kenya** — Largest network, good for expats
- **Equity Bank** — Digital-forward, growing expat customer base
- **Standard Chartered Bank** — International standard, higher fees
- **Stanbic Bank** — Professional services, corporate accounts
- **Barclays Bank Kenya** — Established, reliable

**Online/digital banks:**

- **ABSA Bank** — Good digital platform
- **Safaricom M-Pesa** — Not a traditional bank, but essential
- **Equity Bank's Equitel** — Mobile-first banking

## What you'll need

**Minimum requirements:**

1. Passport (original + photocopy)
2. Huduma Number (national ID) — **Get this first** (see [[moving-to-nairobi]])
3. Proof of address (rental agreement or utility bill)
4. Minimum opening balance: KES 500-2,000 (varies by bank)

**If you're employed:**

- Employment letter (for salary account setup)
- Employer's details
- Expected monthly deposit amount

**If you're self-employed/business owner:**

- Business registration document (if formal business)
- Proof of business address
- Business tax certificate (if applicable)

## Opening an account

### Steps (typical)

1. **Visit a branch** — Go in person or book an appointment
2. **Fill forms** — Account opening forms (2-3 pages)
3. **Provide documents** — Passport, ID, proof of address
4. **Make initial deposit** — Usually KES 500-5,000
5. **Activate account** — 1-3 business days
6. **Get debit card** — Issued within 1-2 weeks (available at branch or mailed)

### Processing time

- **In-person opening:** Same day to 1 business day
- **Documents by mail:** 3-5 business days (not recommended for first account)
- **Online opening:** Some banks allow pre-registration; still requires in-person verification

### Which account type?

**Savings account** — For casual use, minimal fees:
- No minimum balance (usually)
- Low/no monthly maintenance fee
- Interest on balance (typically 0.5-2% annually)
- Limited transactions per month (may have fees above certain number)

**Salary account** — If you're employed:
- Designed for monthly salary deposits
- Low/no fees
- Unlimited transactions
- Free/cheap transfers to other banks
- Mobile and online banking included

**Business account** — If you're self-employed:
- Must register business (sole trader, partnership, limited company)
- Higher minimum balance
- Transaction limits per day
- May require separate business registration

## Monthly fees

Typical bank charges:

| Service | Cost |
|---|---|
| Monthly maintenance | KES 300-1,000 |
| ATM withdrawal (own bank) | Free |
| ATM withdrawal (other bank) | KES 100-200 |
| Bank transfer | KES 200-500 (or % of amount) |
| Mobile transfer (M-Pesa to bank) | Free-KES 200 |
| Overdraft (if approved) | 20% interest annually |

## Using your account

### Deposits

- **Salary:** Automatic from employer
- **Cash deposit:** At any branch, usually free
- **Cheque deposit:** Less common now; can take 3-5 days to clear
- **Mobile money transfer:** Via M-Pesa/Airtel money (see below)

### Withdrawals

- **ATM:** 24/7 at your bank and partner ATMs
- **Over-the-counter:** At branch during business hours
- **Mobile banking:** Withdraw at nominated agents (shops, petrol stations)

### Transfers

- **Bank-to-bank:** Via online banking, 1-2 hours
- **To M-Pesa:** Instant, via online banking or ATM
- **International:** Via SWIFT (slow, expensive; KES 3,000-10,000 fee)
- **Money transfer services:** Western Union, MoneyGram (faster than SWIFT)

## Mobile money (M-Pesa)

**M-Pesa is essential in Kenya.** Most rent, utilities, and casual payments use it.

### Getting M-Pesa

1. Visit Safaricom shop or any telecom retailer
2. Ask for "M-Pesa registration"
3. Provide Huduma Number and phone number
4. Pay registration fee: Usually free (check current promotion)
5. Activate within 10 minutes

### Using M-Pesa

- **Dial *150#** — Main menu
- **Send money:** *150*1*[Recipient phone]#
- **Check balance:** *150*1*00#
- **Withdraw at agent:** Ask at shops/petrol stations

### M-Pesa limits (per day)

- **Send money:** Up to KES 140,000/day
- **Withdraw cash:** Up to KES 250,000/day (cumulative across agents)
- **Receive money:** Unlimited

### M-Pesa to bank

- Transfer M-Pesa to your bank account instantly via USSD or bank app
- Fee: Typically KES 100-200
- Available at most banks

## Interbank transfers

All banks are connected. Transferring between bank accounts is fast:

1. Get recipient's **full account number** and **bank code**
2. Use online banking or mobile app
3. Enter amount and description
4. Confirm (some banks require OTP verification)
5. Transfer completes in 1-2 hours (usually instant)

Fee is typically KES 200-500.

## Online/mobile banking

All major banks offer mobile apps. Features:

- Check balance (instant)
- View transactions
- Transfer money
- Pay bills (utilities, insurance, loans)
- Receive alerts
- Lock/unlock card remotely
- Request statements

**Security tips:**
- Use strong passwords (8+ characters, mixed case)
- Enable two-factor authentication if available
- Never share OTP codes with anyone
- Log out after each session on shared devices
- Don't use public WiFi for banking

## Overdrafts and loans

Banks may offer overdraft facilities (negative balance with interest):

- **Approved overdraft:** Usually 20% interest annually on overdrawn balance
- **Unapproved overdraft:** Higher rate (25%+), immediate fees
- **Salary advance loans:** Some employers partner with banks for quick loans

**Avoid overdrafts if possible** — interest adds up fast.

## SWIFT transfers (international)

Sending money overseas:

1. Provide your **SWIFT code** (usually printed on bank statement)
2. Receive international transfers to your account
3. Fees: KES 3,000-10,000 (receiving), KES 5,000-20,000 (sending)
4. Processing time: 3-7 business days
5. Exchange rate: Usually unfavorable (2-3% markup)

**Alternatives:**
- Wise (TransferWise) — Cheaper, faster international transfers
- MoneyGram/Western Union — Available for cash pickup

## FAQs

**Can I open an account as a foreigner?**
Yes, with valid passport + Huduma Number. Some banks prefer residents to have visa stamps.

**Do I need to start with M-Pesa or a bank account?**
Both eventually. M-Pesa first (easier, no Huduma needed for basic use), then bank account within first month.

**What's the best bank for expats?**
KCB, Equity, or Standard Chartered. They have English-speaking staff and international experience.

**Can I keep a foreign bank account while working in Kenya?**
Yes, but Kenyan banks require you to declare foreign accounts. No legal issue, but transparency matters.

**What if I lose my debit card?**
Call your bank immediately. Card is blocked within minutes. Replacement: KES 500-1,000, takes 1-2 weeks.

**Can I use my bank account for business?**
Yes, but large irregular deposits may trigger KRA scrutiny. Consider a business account if you're self-employed.

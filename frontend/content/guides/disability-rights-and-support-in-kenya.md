Kenya's Constitution protects the rights of persons with disabilities to
dignity, access, education and work. Several kinds of support depend on
being **registered** with the **National Council for Persons with
Disabilities** (NCPWD).

## Registering with NCPWD

1. Get a **disability assessment** at a designated public hospital - the
   medical report confirms the nature of the disability.
2. **Apply to NCPWD** - online through [[eCitizen]] or at NCPWD's offices -
   with the medical report, ID (or a birth certificate for a child) and
   photos.
3. Receive your **registration** and disability card.

Registration opens the door to the support below.

## Tax relief

Registered persons with disabilities who earn an income can apply for
**exemption from income tax** on the first
**KES {{ DISABILITY_TAX_EXEMPTION_MONTHLY|shillings }} a month**.

- Apply through NCPWD and [[KRA]] with your NCPWD registration, [[KRA PIN]]
  and a tax compliance certificate. See
  [KRA tax compliance certificate](/guides/kra-tax-compliance-certificate).
- Once approved, give the exemption certificate to your employer so
  [PAYE](/guides/paye-and-taxes) is adjusted.
- The rules are being updated, including possible relief for parents and
  guardians - check KRA and NCPWD for the current position.

There are also exemptions on duty for some **assistive devices** and
specially adapted vehicles.

## Cash transfers and grants

- **Inua Jamii** - the government's cash transfer for people with
  **severe disabilities** pays **KES {{ OLDER_PERSONS_CASH_TRANSFER|shillings }}
  a month**. Ask at the local social development office.
- **NCPWD** runs grants and funds for education, assistive devices and
  small businesses for registered members.

## Work

- Employers must not discriminate on grounds of disability, and should make
  **reasonable adjustments**.
- The law sets aside a share of **public jobs and tenders** for persons with
  disabilities - see [Government jobs in Kenya](/guides/government-jobs-in-kenya).
- See [Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya) and
  [Free legal aid](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya) if your rights are not
  respected.

## Education

Children with disabilities can attend regular schools with support, or
special schools and units. Ask the county's **educational assessment**
centre for an assessment and advice on placement.

## Getting around Nairobi

Access is improving but uneven. Newer malls, hotels and offices usually have
ramps and lifts; older buildings, pavements and matatus often don't.
Ride-hailing can be easier than public transport - see
[Ride-hailing and delivery apps](/guides/ride-hailing-and-delivery-apps-in-nairobi).

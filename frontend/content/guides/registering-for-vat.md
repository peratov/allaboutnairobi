**VAT** (value added tax) is charged at {{ VAT_RATE|percent }}% on most goods
and services in Kenya. Businesses above a certain size must register, charge
VAT to customers, and pay it over to KRA monthly, after deducting the VAT they
paid on business purchases.

## When you must register

You must register if your taxable sales are, or are expected to be, more than
**KES {{ VAT_REGISTRATION_THRESHOLD|shillings }}** in any 12-month period.
Apply within 30 days of reaching the threshold.

## Registering voluntarily

Smaller businesses can apply to register voluntarily. It can make sense if
your customers are VAT-registered businesses (they can reclaim the VAT you
charge) and you pay significant VAT on purchases. It adds monthly paperwork,
and KRA may ask for evidence of trading.

## How to register

1. Log in to [[iTax]] with the business's [[KRA PIN]] (or your own, for a sole
   trader).
2. Go to **Registration** and choose to **amend PIN details**, adding the
   **VAT obligation**.
3. Provide details of the business activity and expected turnover, and upload
   any documents requested.
4. Once approved, VAT is added to your PIN obligations.

## Charging VAT

- Add {{ VAT_RATE|percent }}% to taxable sales, and show it on the invoice.
- Issue invoices through **eTIMS**, which sends them to KRA. See
  [eTIMS explained](/guides/etims-for-small-businesses).
- Some goods and services are **zero-rated** or **exempt** - check how your
  products are treated.

Work out prices with the [VAT calculator](/tools/vat-calculator).

## Monthly returns

File a **VAT return** and pay by the **20th of the following month**:

- **Output VAT** - the VAT you charged.
- **Input VAT** - the VAT you paid on business purchases, backed by valid
  eTIMS invoices.
- Pay the difference, or carry forward a credit.

Nil returns are required in months with no sales. Late filing and payment
bring penalties.

## Turnover tax

Check with KRA how VAT registration affects a turnover tax obligation. See
[Turnover tax vs income tax](/guides/turnover-tax-vs-income-tax).

Nairobi is cosmopolitan and relaxed, and nobody expects newcomers to know
every custom. A few habits, though, go a long way.

## Greetings come first

Greet before you ask. Launching straight into a question - at a shop counter,
with a guard, in an office - comes across as rude. A "Habari?" or a "good
morning, how are you?" first changes the whole conversation.

- **Handshakes** are normal, and can be long. A light, lingering handshake is
  friendly, not odd.
- **Titles and respect.** Older men are often called **mzee** and older women
  **mama**, as a mark of respect.

See [Swahili for daily life](/guides/swahili-for-daily-life) for the words.

## Time

Social events often start later than the invitation says, and "I'm coming"
can mean anything from now to an hour. Business meetings and interviews are
more punctual - be on time for those, and allow for traffic.

## Dress

Nairobi dresses smartly, especially for work, church and government offices.
Casual is fine in malls and at weekends. Dress more modestly on the coast and
in Muslim areas such as Eastleigh.

## Photographs

Ask before photographing people, and accept a no. Do not photograph
government buildings, police, military sites or State House.

## Religion

Most Kenyans are Christian, with a large Muslim minority and Hindu and other
communities. Religion is part of public life: meetings may open with a prayer,
and Sunday mornings are for church.

## Money and favours

- **Bargaining** is expected in markets, not in shops with printed prices. See
  [Shopping](/guides/shopping-in-nairobi#bargaining).
- **Tipping** is appreciated but modest. See
  [Food and eating out](/guides/kenyan-food-and-eating-out#paying-and-tipping).
- **Bribes.** If someone asks for [[kitu kidogo]], ask for the official receipt
  or the proper process instead.

## Things that surprise newcomers

- **Plastic bags are banned.** Kenya banned single-use plastic carrier bags
  in 2017, with heavy penalties. Bring a reusable bag when you shop.
- **Smoking in public** is banned in most public places.
- **Littering** can bring a fine from county officers.
- **Being called [[mzungu]]** - it is usually descriptive, not hostile.

## Conversation

Kenyans are warm and curious, and talk about family, work, football and
politics easily. Ethnicity is a sensitive subject, especially around
elections; follow the other person's lead.

The **National Transport and Safety Authority** ([[NTSA]]) inspects vehicles
to make sure they are roadworthy. Public service and commercial vehicles
have long needed regular inspection. In 2026, new rules tried to extend
annual inspection to older **private cars** - but enforcement for private
cars has been **suspended by the High Court**.

## Who needs inspection

- **Public service vehicles** - matatus, buses, taxis and school vehicles -
  need regular inspection.
- **Commercial vehicles** - lorries, vans and other goods vehicles.
- **Driving school vehicles** and some other special categories.
- **Vehicles after major changes** - a change of use, body or engine, or
  after serious accident repairs.
- **Imported vehicles** are inspected before registration. See
  [Importing a car to Kenya](/guides/importing-a-car-to-kenya).

## Private cars

The **Traffic (Motor Vehicle Inspection) Rules 2026** would require private
cars older than four years to be inspected every year. In July 2026 the
**High Court suspended** enforcement for private non-commercial vehicles
while challenges are heard, and NTSA told traffic officers not to enforce
it on private cars.

Check NTSA's latest notices before acting - the position may change when
the court rules.

## What is checked

Inspectors check that the vehicle is safe and matches its records,
including:

- **brakes**, **steering** and **suspension**
- **lights**, indicators and wipers
- **tyres**
- **body**, seats and **seatbelts**
- **emissions** and exhaust
- **chassis and engine numbers** against the logbook

## How it works

1. **Book** an inspection through your NTSA account on its online portal,
   and pay the fee.
2. **Take the vehicle** to the designated inspection centre on the date.
3. **Pass** and receive your inspection certificate or sticker - or **fix**
   the listed faults and return for a re-test.

## Preparing

- Check **lights, tyres, wipers and brakes** beforehand - most failures are
  simple fixes.
- Make sure **chassis and engine numbers** match your logbook. See
  [Transferring a car logbook](/guides/transferring-a-car-logbook).
- Use only official NTSA centres and channels. Don't pay anyone to "get a
  pass" - it's illegal and dangerous.

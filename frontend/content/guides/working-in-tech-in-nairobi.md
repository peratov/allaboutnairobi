Nairobi is one of Africa's biggest technology centres, nicknamed
**"Silicon Savannah"**. M-Pesa was born here, and the city is home to
startups, the African offices of global companies, and a large community of
developers, designers and data people.

## Where the jobs are

- **Startups** - in fintech, logistics, agriculture, health, energy and
  more. Fast-moving, often with stock options, but less secure: funding
  rises and falls.
- **Large Kenyan companies** - telecoms, banks, insurers and retailers all
  have big technology teams.
- **Global companies** - several international tech firms have engineering,
  research or regional offices in Nairobi.
- **NGOs and the UN** - Nairobi's large development sector hires data,
  software and IT staff. See [UN and NGO jobs in Nairobi](/guides/un-and-ngo-jobs-in-nairobi).
- **Remote work for companies abroad** - many Kenyan developers work
  remotely for employers in Europe and North America.

## Finding a job

- **LinkedIn** is where most tech hiring happens. Keep your profile and a
  portfolio or GitHub up to date.
- Kenyan **job boards** and company careers pages.
- **Referrals** from communities and former colleagues.
- See [Finding a job in Nairobi](/guides/finding-a-job-in-nairobi) and
  [Understanding a salary offer](/guides/understanding-a-salary-offer).

## Learning the skills

- **University** degrees in computer science, IT and engineering.
- **Bootcamps** - several coding schools in Nairobi run intensive courses in
  software engineering, data and design, some with job placement help.
- **Free and online courses**, often supported by global tech companies.
- **Building things** - a few real projects in a portfolio count for a lot.

Check what a paid course includes, and talk to past students before paying.

## Hubs and communities

- **Tech hubs** and innovation spaces - such as **iHub**, one of the first,
  opened in 2010 - host events and startups.
- **Coworking spaces** across Westlands, Kilimani and the CBD. See
  [Coworking spaces in Nairobi](/guides/coworking-spaces-in-nairobi).
- **Meetups** and developer communities for particular languages and tools,
  women in tech, design and data - many free. Most organise on social media.
- **Konza Technopolis**, a planned technology city south-east of Nairobi,
  is being built as a long-term tech and research hub.

## Working remotely for a foreign company

- You are either an **employee** (often through an "employer of record") or
  a **contractor**. As a contractor, you invoice, and you are responsible for
  your own tax. See [Freelancing in Kenya](/guides/freelancing-in-kenya).
- Being paid: see [Receiving PayPal, Payoneer and Wise payments](/guides/receiving-paypal-payoneer-and-wise-payments).
- Reliable internet and backup power matter. See
  [Home internet](/guides/home-internet-in-nairobi) and
  [Solar and backup power](/guides/solar-and-backup-power).

## Tax

Employees pay PAYE through payroll. Freelancers and contractors must file
their own returns with KRA, and may need to register for turnover tax or
VAT. Work out your take-home pay in the [salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator).

## Foreigners in tech

Foreign tech workers need a work permit, or a digital nomad permit if
working for employers abroad. See [Class D work permit](/guides/class-d-work-permit)
and [Class N digital nomad permit](/guides/class-n-digital-nomad-permit).

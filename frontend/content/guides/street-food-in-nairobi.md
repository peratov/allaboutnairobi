Some of Nairobi's best food costs little and is sold from a roadside grill,
a handcart or a small stall. Evenings are the best time, when the grills are
lit outside stages, estates and nightspots.

## What to try

- **Mutura** - Kenyan sausage of minced meat, offal, blood and spices in a
  casing, grilled over charcoal and sliced, often with kachumbari. A Nairobi
  favourite with a strong flavour.
- **Smokie pasua** - a smoked sausage split open and filled with
  **kachumbari** (tomato, onion and chilli), sold from handcarts everywhere.
- **Mayai pasua** - a boiled egg split and stuffed the same way.
- **Mahindi choma** - maize roasted over charcoal, rubbed with lemon and
  chilli salt. Boiled maize is sold too.
- **Mshikaki** - skewers of grilled meat.
- **Samosas** and **bhajia** - fried pastries and spiced potato fritters,
  a legacy of Kenya's Indian community.
- **Viazi karai** - potato pieces fried in a spiced batter.
- **Chapati** and **mandazi** - flatbread and lightly sweet fried dough,
  for breakfast and tea time.
- **Fruit** - mango, pineapple and watermelon cut while you wait, mango
  often with chilli and salt. **Sugarcane** cut into pieces to chew.
- **Groundnuts** - roasted peanuts in paper cones.
- **Uji** - thin, warm porridge of millet or sorghum, sold in the morning.

## Where to find it

- **Around matatu stages and markets** in the CBD and in every estate.
- **Outside bars and nightspots** in the evening, for mutura and mshikaki.
- **River Road and the streets around it** in the CBD.
- **Markets** such as Kenyatta Market for nyama choma. See
  [Markets in Nairobi](/guides/markets-in-nairobi).

For sit-down Kenyan food, see [Kenyan food and eating out](/guides/kenyan-food-and-eating-out).

## Eating safely

- **Choose busy stalls** where food sells quickly.
- **Food cooked hot in front of you** - grilled, roasted or fried - is the
  safest choice.
- **Fruit you peel**, or fruit cut fresh with a clean knife, is safer than
  fruit cut and left out.
- Be careful with **raw salads** and kachumbari if your stomach is not used
  to it.
- Drink **bottled** or boiled water, and be wary of ice in drinks from
  stalls.
- Carry **hand sanitiser**.

## Paying

Street food is cheap. Carry **coins and small notes**, though many sellers
take M-Pesa. Prices are usually fixed for food; you do not need to bargain.

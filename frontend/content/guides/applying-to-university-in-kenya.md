There are two main routes into a Kenyan university: **placement through
KUCCPS**, which is how most school leavers get a government-supported place,
and **applying directly** to a university as a self-sponsored student. Nairobi
has the country's largest concentration of universities and colleges.

## KUCCPS placement

The **Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service** (KUCCPS)
places students in public and private universities, and in TVET colleges, on
government-supported programmes.

1. After **KCSE results** are released, KUCCPS opens its **online
   application portal**.
2. You choose programmes and institutions in order of preference. Each
   programme has **minimum subject requirements** and, for competitive
   courses, a cut-off.
3. KUCCPS places you based on your grades, choices and the places
   available. There is usually a **revision window** for those not placed.
4. You receive an admission letter from the institution.

Use only the official KUCCPS portal, and watch for dates: application
windows are short.

## Applying directly

You can also apply straight to a university for a **self-sponsored** place
(sometimes called "module II" or parallel programmes), usually paying higher
fees. Private universities admit most of their students this way. Check each
university's requirements and intakes - many have several a year.

## Check accreditation

Make sure both the **university** and the **programme** are accredited by the
**Commission for University Education** (CUE), which publishes lists of
accredited institutions. Professional degrees such as law, medicine,
engineering and nursing also need recognition by their professional body.
See [Studying in Nairobi](/guides/studying-in-nairobi).

## Paying for it

- Government-sponsored students have been funded through a mix of
  **scholarships** from the **Universities Fund** and **loans** from **HELB**,
  based on a means assessment.
- This funding model has been challenged in the courts, and a bill to merge
  the funding bodies is before Parliament. **Check the current rules** with
  HELB, the Universities Fund and your university before you budget.
- **Bursaries** from counties, constituencies (NG-CDF), companies and
  foundations help many students.
- Self-sponsored students pay fees directly; some universities allow
  instalments.

See [HELB loans, repayment and clearance](/guides/helb-loans-repayment-and-clearance).

## Not ready for university?

**TVET colleges** offer diplomas and certificates that lead to good jobs, and
can lead on to degrees later. See
[TVET colleges and learning a trade](/guides/tvet-colleges-and-learning-a-trade).

## For international students

Foreign students apply directly to the university, then need a **student
pass** from the Department of Immigration. See [Studying in Nairobi](/guides/studying-in-nairobi).

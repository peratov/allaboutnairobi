Most of Nairobi's apartment areas have **fibre** to the building, and prices
have fallen sharply as providers compete. Where fibre is not available, **5G
or 4G routers** and **Starlink** fill the gap.

## Your options

**Fibre.** The best choice where available: fast, unlimited, and reliable.
The big home fibre providers include **Safaricom Home Fibre**, **Zuku**,
**JTL Faiba**, and smaller networks such as Vilcom and Poa Internet. Coverage
is building by building.

**5G and 4G home routers.** Safaricom and Airtel sell routers with monthly
data plans. No installation, and you can take it when you move - but speeds
vary with the signal and time of day.

**Starlink.** Satellite internet that works almost anywhere. You buy the kit,
then pay monthly. It suits houses in areas without fibre, and people who need
a backup.

## What it costs

As a rough guide in 2026:

- **Budget plans** from low-cost providers such as Poa Internet start at under
  KES 2,000 a month for slow speeds.
- **Typical home fibre** is around KES 3,000 to 6,500 a month for speeds
  suitable for streaming and video calls.
- **The fastest plans** reach around KES 20,000 a month.
- **Starlink** plans are in a similar monthly range to mid-level fibre, plus
  the cost of the kit.

Providers change speeds and prices often. Check their websites, and ask about
installation fees and router costs.

## How to choose

1. **Ask the building.** The caretaker or other tenants know which providers
   are connected and how reliable they are.
2. **Check coverage** on the providers' websites or by calling them, using
   your exact building name.
3. **Match the speed to your use.** Working from home with video calls, or
   several people streaming, needs more than one person browsing.
4. **Look at upload speed** if you work remotely.
5. **Ask about fair use, installation time and contract length.**

## Before you sign a lease

If you work from home, ask **which fibre providers already reach the
building** before signing - it can take weeks to bring a new provider in. See
[Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing).

## Backup

Power cuts also cut Wi-Fi. A small **UPS** (battery backup) for the router
keeps you online for a few hours, and a mobile data bundle is a useful
fallback. See [Working remotely from Nairobi](/guides/working-remotely-from-nairobi).

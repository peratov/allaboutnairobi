Paying school fees overseas, supporting family abroad, paying a foreign
supplier: Kenya has no limit on how much you can send abroad, but large
transfers need paperwork, and the cost varies a lot between options.

## Your options

- **A bank transfer (SWIFT).** From your bank's app or branch, to a bank
  account abroad. Good for large amounts and fees paid to institutions.
  You need the recipient's **name, account number or IBAN, bank name and
  SWIFT code**.
- **Money transfer companies** - such as Western Union and MoneyGram,
  through banks, forex bureaus and agents - for sending to a person who
  collects cash or receives to a bank or wallet.
- **M-Pesa Global** - send from M-Pesa to mobile wallets in some countries, or
  through Western Union, from the M-Pesa menu. Quick for smaller amounts in the
  region.
- **Paying a card bill or invoice online** - school and university fees can
  sometimes be paid by card. See [Paying for online services](/guides/paying-for-online-services-from-kenya).

## What it really costs

The fee is only part of the cost. Compare:

1. **The transfer fee** charged by your bank or provider.
2. **The exchange rate** - the margin between the rate you get and the
   market rate is often the biggest cost. Check it in the
   [rate checker](/tools/rate-checker).
3. **Intermediary and receiving fees** - a SWIFT transfer can pass through
   other banks, each taking a fee, and the receiving bank may charge too.
   Ask your bank whether you can pay all charges ("OUR") so the full amount
   arrives.

Compare the **amount that arrives** for the same shillings, not the
advertised fee.

## Large amounts

- There is **no cap** on sending money abroad, but banks must keep
  documents for foreign exchange transactions above the equivalent of
  **USD 10,000**. Expect to show the purpose - an invoice, fee statement,
  contract or similar - and where the money came from.
- Banks must report large and unusual transactions under anti-money
  laundering law. This is routine; have your documents ready.
- Carrying **cash** of more than USD 10,000 (or the equivalent) out of Kenya
  must be declared at the border.

## Paying school and university fees abroad

- Get the institution's **official bank details** from its website or fee
  invoice, and confirm them by phone if they arrive by email - invoice
  fraud is common.
- Send early: international transfers can take several working days.
- Keep the transfer confirmation (the "MT103" or SWIFT copy) as proof of
  payment.

## Holding dollars

If you pay regularly in dollars, a **dollar account** at a Kenyan bank lets
you buy when the rate suits you. A [dollar money market fund](/guides/dollar-money-market-funds-in-kenya)
can earn interest on dollar savings.

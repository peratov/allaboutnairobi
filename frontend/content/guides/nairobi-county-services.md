Nairobi City County runs many of the services that shape daily life. Most can
now be paid for online, mainly through [[eCitizen]] and the county's own
systems.

## Parking

Street parking in the CBD and many commercial areas is paid by the day or
hour, through the county's system by M-Pesa, and enforced by [[kanjo]]
officers who clamp cars. Pay before you walk away, and keep the confirmation
message. See [Driving in Nairobi](/guides/driving-in-nairobi#parking).

## Business permits

Anyone trading from premises needs the county's **Unified Business Permit**,
renewed every year. See
[Registering a business](/guides/registering-a-business-in-kenya#the-nairobi-county-permit).

## Land rates

Owners of land and buildings in Nairobi pay annual **land rates** to the
county. Unpaid rates build up with penalties and block a sale, because a
buyer's advocate will ask for a rates clearance certificate. See
[Buying property](/guides/buying-property-in-kenya).

## Rubbish

Household rubbish is usually collected by private collectors arranged by the
building, estate or residents' association, for a monthly fee. Illegal
dumping and littering can bring fines.

## Markets and hawking

The county runs many public markets and licenses traders. Hawking is allowed
only in designated areas and times.

## Health and other services

The county runs public health facilities, including Mama Lucy Kibaki and
Mbagathi hospitals and many health centres, as well as early childhood
education, fire and ambulance services, and some sports grounds.

## Water

Water and sewerage are supplied by **Nairobi City Water and Sewerage
Company**, owned by the county. See
[Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi#water).

## Complaints and help

- Start with the relevant county office or its official social media
  accounts, and keep reference numbers.
- Your **ward's MCA** can raise local problems with the county.
- For service failures, public-sector complaints can also go to the
  **Commission on Administrative Justice (the Ombudsman)**.

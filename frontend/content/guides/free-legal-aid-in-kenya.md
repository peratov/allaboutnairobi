Lawyers are expensive, but legal help in Kenya is not only for those who can
pay. The **Constitution** guarantees access to justice, and the **Legal Aid
Act 2016** set up a national service to provide it.

## The National Legal Aid Service

The **National Legal Aid Service** (NLAS) gives free legal advice, and in
some cases representation in court, to people who cannot afford a lawyer.

**Who qualifies:** you must be unable to pay for legal services and live in
Kenya, and be one of:

- a **Kenyan citizen**
- a **child**
- a **refugee**
- a victim of **human trafficking**
- an **internally displaced** or **stateless** person

For representation in a criminal or civil case, your **net monthly income**
after tax must be no more than **KES {{ LEGAL_AID_INCOME_LIMIT|shillings }}**.
Companies and organisations do not qualify.

**How to apply:** go to the nearest NLAS office, or one near the court where
your case is heard. Bring your ID and any documents about your case. NLAS
also runs legal aid clinics and help desks at some courts.

## In criminal cases

If you are accused of a crime, you have the right to be told of your rights
and to have an advocate. If you cannot afford one and **substantial
injustice** would result otherwise, the state should provide one - ask the
court. Children in conflict with the law are entitled to representation.

## Other sources of free help

- **The Law Society of Kenya** (LSK) runs free legal clinics and pro bono
  programmes, especially during its annual legal awareness weeks.
- **University law clinics** - law schools run legal aid clinics staffed by
  students under supervision.
- **Non-profit organisations** - groups such as **Kituo cha Sheria** and
  **FIDA Kenya** (for women and family matters) give advice and sometimes
  representation.
- **Court help desks** explain procedures at many court stations.
- The **county labour office** helps with employment disputes for free. See
  [Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya#when-things-go-wrong).

## Doing it yourself

Some courts and bodies are designed for people without lawyers:

- the [Small Claims Court](/guides/small-claims-court-in-kenya) for money
  claims
- regulators' complaint processes - see
  [Complaining about a bank](/guides/complaining-about-a-bank-in-kenya) and
  [Consumer rights](/guides/consumer-rights-in-kenya)
- the [Data Commissioner](/guides/your-data-protection-rights-in-kenya)

## When you need a paid advocate

For land and property purchases, succession, serious criminal charges and
large disputes, a qualified advocate is worth it. Check an advocate is
admitted and has a current practising certificate with the **LSK**, and ask
for fees in writing before you start.

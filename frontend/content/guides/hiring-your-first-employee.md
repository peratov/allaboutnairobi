Taking on your first employee turns a business into an employer, with
monthly duties to KRA, NSSF and SHA. None of it is difficult once set up.
For staff working in your home, see
[Hiring household staff](/guides/hiring-household-staff).

## 1. Register as an employer

- **PAYE** - add the PAYE obligation to your business's (or your own)
  [[KRA PIN]] on [[iTax]].
- **NSSF** - register as an employer and register the employee if they are not
  already a member.
- **SHA** - register as an employer for SHIF contributions.
- **Housing Levy** - declared and paid alongside PAYE on iTax.
- **NITA** - the National Industrial Training Authority levy, a small monthly
  amount per employee.

## 2. The contract

The Employment Act requires a **written contract** for anyone employed for
three months or more. It should set out the job, pay, hours, leave, probation,
notice and place of work. See the
[employment contract checklist](/docs/employment-contract-checklist) and
[Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya).

Pay at least the **minimum wage** for the job. See
[Minimum wage in Kenya](/guides/minimum-wage-in-kenya).

## 3. Monthly payroll

From each month's gross pay:

- **PAYE** - income tax, after personal relief.
- **NSSF** - {{ NSSF_RATE|percent }}% from the employee and
  {{ NSSF_RATE|percent }}% from you, up to the upper earnings limit.
- **SHIF** - {{ SHIF_RATE|percent }}% from the employee.
- **Housing Levy** - {{ HOUSING_LEVY_RATE|percent }}% from the employee, and
  the same again from you.

Work out the figures with the [salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator).
Your cost is the gross pay **plus** your share of NSSF and the Housing Levy.

## 4. Deadlines

PAYE, the Housing Levy and SHIF are due by the **9th of the following month**,
and NSSF contributions monthly too. Late payment brings penalties and interest,
and unpaid deductions you took from an employee's pay are treated seriously.

Give the employee a **payslip** every month, and a **P9** after the end of the
year for their tax return. See
[Filing your return with a P9](/guides/filing-your-kra-return-with-a-p9).

## 5. Insurance

Employers must insure employees against injury at work under the **Work Injury
Benefits Act (WIBA)**. Buy a WIBA policy from a licensed insurer before the
employee starts.

## 6. Records

Keep contracts, payslips, leave records and proof of payments for at least
several years. Labour officers and KRA can ask for them.

## Payroll help

For one or two employees, a spreadsheet and iTax are enough. As you grow,
payroll software or an accountant saves time and mistakes.

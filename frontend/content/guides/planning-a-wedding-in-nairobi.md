Weddings in Nairobi range from small civil ceremonies to celebrations with
hundreds of guests, and often include more than one event: a traditional
ceremony, a church or civil wedding, and a reception. Planning early and
budgeting carefully makes all the difference.

## Making it legal

A wedding is only legally binding if the marriage is registered - civil,
religious, customary or Hindu. Book the registrar or officiant early and give
notice as required. See [Getting married in Kenya](/guides/getting-married-in-kenya).

## Traditional ceremonies

Many Kenyan weddings begin with **customary ceremonies** between the two
families - for example the negotiation and payment of **dowry** (such as
*ruracio* among the Kikuyu). These are family occasions with their own
customs, often held at the bride's family home.

## Venues

- **Garden venues** - especially in **Karen**, Runda and along the outskirts;
  very popular for outdoor ceremonies and receptions.
- **Hotels** - convenient, with catering included, in the city and Westlands.
- **Churches and places of worship** for the ceremony, with a separate
  reception venue.
- **Destination weddings** - Naivasha, Nanyuki and the coast.

Book popular venues months ahead, especially in the dry seasons. Have a
**tent or rain plan** - weather in the long and short rains is unpredictable.

## The main costs

- **Venue and catering** - usually the largest cost, per guest.
- **Tents, décor and chairs**.
- **Photography and video**.
- **Attire** - the couple, the bridal party, and often matching outfits.
- **Music** - DJ, band or choir, and the sound system.
- **Cake, flowers and transport**.
- **Registrar or officiant fees** and a **wedding planner** if you use one.

Set the **guest count first** - it drives almost every other cost. Use the
[budget planner](/tools/budget-planner) to plan.

## Wedding committees

Many couples form a **wedding committee** of family and friends who help plan
and raise contributions, meeting weekly in the months before. Contributions
often come through a [[harambee]] or M-Pesa. Keep clear records of money in
and out, and thank contributors.

## Choosing vendors

- **See their past work** and ask for references.
- **Get everything in writing** - price, what is included, times and payment
  schedule.
- **Pay deposits to business accounts**, with receipts.
- **Confirm a week before** the day.

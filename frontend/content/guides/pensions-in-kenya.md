Most Kenyans retire with far less than they need. Saving early, and using the
tax relief the law allows, makes a large difference. This is general
information, not financial advice.

## The three layers

1. **[[NSSF]]** - the national scheme. Employees pay
   {{ NSSF_RATE|percent }}% of pensionable pay, matched by the employer, up to
   the upper earnings limit. See
   [NSSF statement and benefits](/guides/checking-your-nssf-statement).
2. **Occupational schemes** - pension or provident funds run by an employer,
   usually with the employer contributing too.
3. **Personal pension plans** - individual plans from insurers and fund
   managers, open to anyone, including the self-employed.

All are regulated by the **Retirement Benefits Authority (RBA)**, which
publishes the list of registered schemes.

## Tax relief

Contributions to a registered scheme are deducted from taxable income, up to
the lowest of:

- **KES {{ PENSION_DEDUCTION_MONTHLY|shillings }} a month** (KES 360,000 a
  year),
- **30% of pensionable pay**, or
- what you actually paid in.

NSSF contributions count towards the same limit. Contributions to a registered
**post-retirement medical fund** get their own deduction of up to KES
{{ PRMF_DEDUCTION_MONTHLY|shillings }} a month.

For someone paying tax at 30%, every KES 10,000 put into a pension saves about
KES 3,000 in tax. Employers usually apply the relief through payroll; if you pay
into a personal plan yourself, claim it on your annual return.

## Choosing a personal plan

- **Registered with RBA?**
- **Fees** - management and administration charges eat into returns over
  decades.
- **Where the money is invested**, and past returns.
- **Flexibility** - can you vary contributions or pause them?
- **Access before retirement** - most plans lock money in, with limited
  exceptions.

## Retiring and leaving

- **Retirement age** in most schemes is between 50 (early) and 60 or 65.
- At retirement, part of the benefit can usually be taken as a **lump sum**,
  with the rest paid as a pension or income drawdown. Part of the lump sum is
  tax-free, within limits.
- **Leaving a job** before retirement: your benefits can usually be moved to a
  new scheme or a preservation fund. Taking cash early means losing tax
  benefits and your retirement savings.

## For expatriates

Foreign employees contribute to NSSF and may join employer schemes. Ask what
happens to benefits when you leave Kenya - NSSF has an emigration benefit.

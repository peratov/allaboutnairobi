Cancer is one of the leading causes of death in Kenya, and many cases are
found late. Screening and getting symptoms checked early make treatment far
more likely to work. Nairobi has the country's main cancer centres, public
and private.

## Screening

- **Cervical cancer** - regular screening for women; the HPV vaccine is
  offered to girls.
- **Breast cancer** - breast checks and, from middle age or with risk
  factors, mammograms.
- **Prostate cancer** - men over about 40 should discuss screening with a
  doctor.
- **Colorectal cancer** - screening for older adults or with symptoms.

See [Health checks and screening](/guides/health-checks-and-screening-in-nairobi).

## Signs not to ignore

A lump, unusual bleeding, a sore that doesn't heal, a persistent cough or
hoarseness, difficulty swallowing, changes in bowel habits, or unexplained
weight loss. See a doctor - most of these turn out not to be cancer, but
checking early matters.

## Diagnosis and treatment

- **Kenyatta National Hospital** has a large cancer centre offering
  chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and other public referral hospitals around
  Nairobi also treat cancer.
- **Private hospitals** have oncology departments and cancer centres. See
  [Hospitals in Nairobi](/guides/hospitals-in-nairobi).
- Treatment may include **surgery**, **chemotherapy**, **radiotherapy** and
  newer drug treatments, planned by a team of specialists.
- Ask for a **written treatment plan** and costs, and consider a **second
  opinion** before major treatment.

## Paying for care

- **SHA** has a dedicated **oncology package** covering chemotherapy,
  radiotherapy and related care up to an annual limit, at contracted
  facilities. Before starting, confirm with the hospital's SHA desk that it
  is contracted for your specific treatment and that pre-authorisation is
  done. See [Registering for SHA](/guides/registering-for-sha).
- **Private insurance** may cover more - check limits and exclusions. See
  [Health insurance in Kenya](/guides/health-insurance-in-kenya).
- **Family and community support** - a [[harambee]] or fundraising is
  common for costs beyond cover.
- **Charities and patient groups** sometimes help with medicines,
  transport and accommodation.

## Support

- **Palliative care** relieves pain and symptoms at any stage, not just at
  the end. Ask your hospital about its palliative care team and hospice
  services in Nairobi.
- **Counselling and support groups** help patients and families. See
  [Therapy and mental health](/guides/therapy-and-mental-health-in-nairobi).
- **Blood** - cancer patients often need transfusions. See
  [Donating blood in Nairobi](/guides/donating-blood-in-nairobi).

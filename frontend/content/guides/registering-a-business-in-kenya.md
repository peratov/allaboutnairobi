Starting a small business in Nairobi is mostly online now. The order below is
the order you will do things in.

## 1. Choose the structure

- **Business name (sole proprietorship or partnership).** The quickest and
  cheapest. You and the business are legally the same, so you are personally
  liable for its debts.
- **Private limited company.** A separate legal person, with at least one
  director and one shareholder. Needed if you want investors, to limit your
  liability, or to bid for many corporate and government contracts.
- **Limited liability partnership (LLP).** A middle option used by some
  professional firms.

## 2. Register with the Business Registration Service

Registration is through the **Business Registration Service (BRS)** on
[[eCitizen]].

- **A business name** costs KES {{ BUSINESS_NAME_FEE|shillings }} for the
  name search and registration together. You propose names, and the
  certificate comes as a download.
- **A company** needs a name reservation, then an application with the
  directors' and shareholders' details, IDs and [[KRA PIN]]s. The company's own
  KRA PIN is issued with the incorporation certificate.

## 3. Tax

- **KRA PIN.** A company gets its own. A sole proprietor uses their personal
  PIN.
- **eTIMS.** Businesses must issue invoices through KRA's electronic tax
  invoice system, eTIMS, whether or not they are registered for VAT. There is
  a free app and a web version for small traders.
- **Turnover tax.** Small resident businesses below the VAT threshold usually
  pay turnover tax at {{ TURNOVER_TAX_RATE|percent }}% of gross sales, filed
  monthly on [[iTax]].
- **VAT.** Registration is compulsory once turnover passes KES
  {{ VAT_REGISTRATION_THRESHOLD|shillings }} in twelve months. VAT is
  {{ VAT_RATE|percent }}%; see the [VAT calculator](/tools/vat-calculator).
- **Corporation tax** for companies is {{ CORPORATE_TAX_RATE|percent }}% of
  profit.

Thresholds and rates change with Finance Acts, so confirm with KRA or an
accountant before relying on them.

## 4. The Nairobi county permit

To trade from premises in Nairobi you need the county's **Unified Business
Permit (UBP)**, which replaced a stack of separate licences. Apply and pay
through the county's services on eCitizen. The fee depends on the type and
size of the business and is renewed every year. The county has waived fees for
some new startups, so check before you pay.

Some businesses need more: a food hygiene licence and health certificates for
anything serving food, and sector licences for things like pharmacies,
transport and alcohol.

## 5. If you employ people

Register as an employer with **NSSF**, **SHA** (for [[SHIF]]) and, for the
[[Housing Levy]] and PAYE, on iTax. Deduct and pay over every month. See
[Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya) and the
[salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator).

## Foreigners

Foreigners can own Kenyan businesses and companies, but working in one needs a
permit - usually a **Class G** permit for investors. See
[Moving to Nairobi](/guides/moving-to-nairobi#if-you-are-coming-to-work).

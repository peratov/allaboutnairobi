The Employment Act 2007 sets the floor for every job in Kenya. A contract can
give you more than the Act, never less.

## The contract

A job that lasts three months or more must have a **written contract**. It
should set out your job title, pay, working hours, leave and notice. Before you
sign, check what counts as basic pay and what as allowances, because
[[NSSF]] is worked out on pensionable pay. See the
[employment contract checklist](/docs/employment-contract-checklist).

[Public holidays in Kenya](/guides/public-holidays-in-kenya).

## Probation and notice

- **Probation** can last up to {{ PROBATION_MAX_MONTHS }} months, and can be
  extended once by up to six more months with your agreement. During probation
  either side can end the job on seven days' notice.
- **Notice** after probation is at least {{ NOTICE_DAYS_MONTHLY_PAID }} days for
  someone paid monthly, unless the contract gives more. An employer can pay you
  in lieu of notice.

## Hours and overtime

The general wage regulations set a normal week of 52 hours, or 60 for night
workers. Overtime is paid at one and a half times the normal rate on ordinary
days, and double on rest days and public holidays. Many office contracts set
shorter hours; the contract applies if it is better for you.

## Minimum wage

Minimum wages are set by occupation and area. In Nairobi, Mombasa and Kisumu a
general labourer - a cleaner, gardener, house worker or day watchman - must be
paid at least **KES {{ NAIROBI_MIN_WAGE_GENERAL_LABOURER|shillings }} a month**
from 1 May 2026. If the employer does not provide housing, it must add a
housing allowance of 15% of basic pay on top.

## Redundancy and dismissal

- **Redundancy** must be notified to you and the local labour officer at least
  a month in advance. Severance is at least **15 days' pay for each completed
  year** of service.
- **Dismissal** needs a valid reason and a fair procedure, including the
  chance to be heard. Summary dismissal without notice is only for gross
  misconduct.

## Employing someone at home

Many households employ a house manager, nanny, gardener or [[askari]]. You are
their employer:

- Pay at least the minimum wage, plus housing allowance if they do not live in.
- Put the terms in writing.
- Register them for [[NSSF]], [[SHIF]] and the [[Housing Levy]], and pay your
  share.
- Give them their annual leave and the public holidays.

## When things go wrong

1. Raise it in writing with your employer, and keep copies of your contract,
   payslips and messages.
2. Report it to the **county labour office**, which can call both sides to a
   conciliation meeting.
3. If that fails, claims go to the **Employment and Labour Relations Court**.

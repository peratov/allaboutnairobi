A [[good conduct certificate]] - officially a police clearance certificate -
comes from the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI). Employers,
immigration, universities abroad and some landlords ask for one. It costs
**KES {{ GOOD_CONDUCT_FEE|shillings }}**.

## Step by step

1. Log in to [[eCitizen]] and open **DCI**, then **Police Clearance
   Certificate**.
2. Fill in the form. Foreign residents apply with a passport and permit
   details.
3. Pay KES {{ GOOD_CONDUCT_FEE|shillings }} by M-Pesa or card.
4. Download and print the **invoice** and the **C24 fingerprint form**.
5. Take them, with your original ID or passport, for fingerprinting.
6. Wait for the SMS or email, then download the certificate from eCitizen.

## Where to have fingerprints taken

- **DCI headquarters on Kiambu Road**, the main centre, with long queues on
  weekday mornings.
- **Selected [[Huduma Centre]]s**, including the GPO centre in the CBD.

Go early. Fingerprints are taken free of charge; do not pay anyone to "fast
track" them.

## How long it takes

Usually two to six weeks after fingerprinting, longer at busy times. If you
need it for a job offer with a deadline, apply as soon as you know.

## How long it is valid

Most organisations treat a certificate as valid for **one year**, or ask for
one issued within the last six months. Check what yours wants.

## Applying from abroad

Kenyans and former residents abroad apply on eCitizen in the same way. Instead
of going to DCI, you have fingerprints taken on the C24 form by a police
station or authorised fingerprinting service where you live, then send the
form to DCI as instructed on eCitizen.

## Common problems

- **Stuck "in progress"** for many weeks: ask at DCI headquarters with your
  invoice number.
- **A past case** shows up: you may need court documents showing how it ended.

Nairobi is East Africa's biggest job market, with regional headquarters, the
UN, NGOs, banks and a busy tech and startup scene. It is also competitive, and
many jobs are filled through contacts before they are advertised.

## Where jobs are advertised

- **LinkedIn** - the main channel for professional, NGO and corporate jobs.
- **Kenyan job boards** such as BrighterMonday, Fuzu and MyJobMag.
- **Company and organisation websites**, especially banks, NGOs and
  multinationals, which often advertise only on their own careers pages.
- **The UN** - UN agencies in Gigiri advertise on their own careers sites.
- **Government jobs** - through the Public Service Commission and county
  public service boards.
- **Your network.** Tell people you are looking. Referrals carry a lot of
  weight.

## The documents employers ask for

Have these ready as PDFs:

- CV and academic certificates
- National ID or passport
- [[KRA PIN]] certificate
- [[Good conduct certificate]]
- NSSF and SHA numbers, once you have them
- For some sectors, a clearance from the Higher Education Loans Board or the
  credit reference bureaus

## CVs and interviews

- **Keep the CV to two pages**, with the most recent job first and
  achievements rather than duties.
- **Referees matter.** Two or three, with phone numbers, who have agreed to be
  called.
- **Interviews** are often in panels, sometimes with a written or practical
  test. Arrive early and allow for traffic.

## Salary

Offers are usually quoted as a **gross monthly** figure. Work out what you will
actually take home with the [salary calculator](/tools/salary-calculator), and
check the split between basic pay and allowances before you accept. See the
[employment contract checklist](/docs/employment-contract-checklist).

## Job scams

- **Never pay to get a job.** Requests for "processing", "medical" or
  "training" fees are the most common scam.
- Be wary of offers that come without an interview, or overseas jobs promised
  through an agency that is not licensed by the National Employment Authority.
- Check the email domain and call the organisation on a number from its own
  website.

## Working as a foreigner

Employers must apply for a **Class D work permit**, and have to show the job
cannot readily be filled by a Kenyan. Do not start work before it is issued.
See [Moving to Nairobi](/guides/moving-to-nairobi#if-you-are-coming-to-work).

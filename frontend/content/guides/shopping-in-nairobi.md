## Supermarkets

The big chains are **Naivas**, **Quickmart**, **Carrefour** and
**Chandarana FoodPlus**, with branches in every mall and most neighbourhoods.
All take M-Pesa and cards. Local brands are much cheaper than imports, and
bakery, butchery and fresh produce counters are good.

## Markets and the mama mboga

For fruit and vegetables, the [[mama mboga]] down the road is often fresher and
cheaper than the supermarket, and will set things aside for you once you are a
regular. Bigger markets:

- **City Market** in the CBD - produce, flowers, meat and crafts.
- **Wakulima (Marikiti) Market** - the wholesale produce market near the
  railway, for buying in bulk early in the morning.
- **Gikomba** and **Toi** - huge markets best known for [[mitumba]] (second-hand
  clothes), shoes and household goods. See both on the [map](/map#landmarks-shopping).

## Malls

Nairobi has many malls. On the map are Two Rivers, Village Market, Westgate,
Sarit Centre, Garden City and The Hub Karen. Others include Yaya Centre, Junction
and Prestige Plaza on Ngong Road. Malls have supermarkets, banks, pharmacies,
cinemas and food courts, and you will pass a bag check at the entrance.

## Crafts and the Maasai Market

The **Maasai Market** is a travelling crafts market: beadwork, kikoys, kiondo
baskets, carvings and soapstone. It moves between locations on different days
of the week, often in or near malls, so ask where it is this week. Bargain, and
start well below the first price.

## Clothes

- **[[Mitumba]]** - good-quality second-hand clothes are a Nairobi habit, not
  just a budget choice. Go early for the best pick.
- **Tailors and fundis** - fabric shops sell kitenge and kikoy fabrics, and a
  tailor will make clothes to measure cheaply. See [[fundi]].
- **Malls** for new clothes, at prices at or above European levels.

## Online and delivery

- **Jumia** and **Kilimall** for general goods.
- **Glovo**, **Bolt Food** and supermarket apps for groceries and takeaway.
- Delivery riders are paid by M-Pesa or card on the app. Tip in cash or by
  M-Pesa if you want to.

## Bargaining

Expect to bargain in markets and with street traders, and with a [[fundi]].
Do not bargain in supermarkets, malls or anywhere with printed prices.
Bargaining is friendly and expected; smile, name a lower price, and settle in
the middle.

## Eating out

Try **[[nyama choma]]** with kachumbari and [[ugali]] on a weekend afternoon,
chapati and beans or githeri at a kibanda, pilau and biryani from the coast,
and the Indian food of Parklands and Ngara. Westlands and Kilimani have the
densest spread of cafés and restaurants.

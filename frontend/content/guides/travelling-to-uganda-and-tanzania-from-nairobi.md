Nairobi is a good base for East Africa. Kampala, Arusha, Kilimanjaro,
Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar are all within a day's travel, by road or a short
flight.

## Documents

**Kenyan citizens:**

- **Uganda** (and **Rwanda**) - you can travel with your **national ID**
  card instead of a passport. Make sure it is valid and in good condition.
- **Tanzania** - take your **passport**, which is the safe choice; ID-only
  travel has not been reliably extended to Tanzania.
- No visa is needed for Kenyans visiting Uganda or Tanzania.

**Foreign nationals** usually need a **visa** for each country, generally
applied for online before travel. Check the requirements for your
nationality on each country's official immigration website. If you live in
Kenya on a permit, check that leaving and re-entering does not affect your
status.

**Yellow fever:** Kenya is a yellow fever risk country, so you may be asked
for your certificate. See [Vaccinations and malaria](/guides/vaccinations-and-malaria-in-kenya).

## To Uganda

- **By bus** - overnight and day buses run from Nairobi to **Kampala**, a
  journey of around twelve hours or more, crossing at **Busia** or
  **Malaba**. Book with established companies, and choose daytime travel if
  you can.
- **By air** - frequent flights from JKIA to **Entebbe**, about an hour.
- **Driving** - possible with the right vehicle documents and insurance
  valid for Uganda; ask your insurer about a COMESA yellow card.

## To Tanzania

- **Arusha and Moshi** - **shuttle buses** run daily from Nairobi via the
  **Namanga** border, taking around five to six hours to Arusha. Good for
  Kilimanjaro, Serengeti and Ngorongoro trips.
- **Dar es Salaam** - long-distance buses take most of a day; most people
  fly.
- **Zanzibar** - fly direct from Nairobi, or via Dar es Salaam.
- **By air** - flights from JKIA (and from Wilson for some routes) to
  Kilimanjaro, Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar.

## At the border

- Many crossings are **one-stop border posts**, where both countries'
  officials work in the same building.
- Have your documents, onward details and any vaccination certificate
  ready.
- **Change money** only at banks and licensed bureaus. Watch out for touts
  and informal money changers at the border.
- Declare goods you are carrying for sale; personal items are fine.

## Money and phones

- Uganda and Tanzania have their own **shillings**. Bureaus in Nairobi and
  at the border change money; cards and ATMs work in cities.
- **Mobile money** - some services let you send M-Pesa to Uganda and
  Tanzania. See [Sending money abroad](/guides/sending-money-abroad-from-kenya).
- **Roaming** on a Kenyan SIM can be expensive; check your network's East
  Africa roaming bundles, or buy a local SIM with your passport.

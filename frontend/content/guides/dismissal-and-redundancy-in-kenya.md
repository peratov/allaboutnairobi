Under the **Employment Act 2007**, an employer in Kenya needs a **valid
reason** and a **fair procedure** to end your job. If either is missing, the
dismissal can be unfair, even if you were paid notice. Add up what you are
owed with the [final dues calculator](/tools/final-dues-calculator).

## A valid reason

An employer can only dismiss you for a reason related to:

- your **conduct**
- your **capacity** to do the job, including performance or ill health
- the **operational needs** of the business - which is redundancy

If you challenge a dismissal, the employer must prove the reason was valid
and genuine.

## A fair procedure

Before dismissing you for misconduct or poor performance, your employer must:

1. **explain the reason** to you, in a language you understand
2. give you a chance to **respond** - a hearing
3. allow **another employee or a union representative** of your choice to be
   present

A letter saying "your services are no longer required" without any of this
is a common route to an unfair dismissal claim.

## Summary dismissal

Dismissal without notice is only allowed for **gross misconduct**, such as
theft, being drunk at work, fighting, or refusing a lawful instruction. Even
then, you should be heard first.

## Notice

After probation, you are owed at least **{{ NOTICE_DAYS_MONTHLY_PAID }} days'
notice** if you are paid monthly, unless your contract gives more. The
employer can pay you in place of notice.

## Redundancy

Redundancy is when your job ends because the business no longer needs it.
The employer must:

- give **at least one month's written notice** to you (or your union) and to
  the county **labour officer**, with the reasons and how many will go
- choose who goes fairly, considering **seniority, skill, ability and
  reliability**
- pay any **leave** you have not taken
- give **notice**, or a month's pay in place of it
- pay **severance** of at least **{{ SEVERANCE_DAYS_PER_YEAR }} days' pay for
  each completed year** of service

## Your final dues

Whichever way the job ends, ask for a written breakdown of:

- salary up to your last day
- unused annual leave
- notice pay, if notice was not worked
- severance, if it is redundancy
- any other pay in your contract, such as a gratuity

Your employer must also give you a **certificate of service** stating your
job and the dates you worked. It cannot be refused.

## Bringing a claim

1. Write to your employer setting out what you think is wrong. Keep copies of
   your contract, payslips, letters and messages.
2. Report it to the county **labour office**, which can call both sides to
   conciliation. This is free.
3. If that fails, file a claim at the **Employment and Labour Relations
   Court**. You must file within **{{ EMPLOYMENT_CLAIM_YEARS }} years**. A
   union, an advocate or a legal aid organisation can help.

If the court finds the dismissal unfair, it can order reinstatement or award
compensation of up to **{{ UNFAIR_DISMISSAL_MAX_MONTHS }} months' gross pay**,
on top of the dues you were owed.

Leaving is easier with a month's head start. Work through this in roughly this
order.

## Two months before

- [ ] **Give notice** to your landlord in writing, as the lease requires, and
      to your employer. See the [final dues calculator](/tools/final-dues-calculator)
      for what you are owed.
- [ ] **Book the movers** and get quotes for shipping anything you are taking.
- [ ] **Sell what you are not taking** - cars and furniture sell well through
      expatriate groups and online marketplaces.

## The last month

- [ ] **The deposit.** Ask the landlord for a joint inspection, and agree how
      and when the deposit will be refunded, in writing.
- [ ] **Utilities.** Close or transfer the internet contract, and settle any
      postpaid electricity and water accounts.
- [ ] **Household staff.** Pay everything owed, including untaken leave and
      notice, and give a certificate of service and a reference.
- [ ] **Your car.** Transfer the logbook to the buyer through [[NTSA]] before
      you hand over the keys.
- [ ] **Pets.** Export needs a veterinary health certificate and whatever your
      destination country requires. Start early.

## Money and tax

- [ ] **Your final tax return.** Keep your P9 and file your return for the
      year on [[iTax]], or ask a tax adviser to.
- [ ] **NSSF.** Members leaving Kenya permanently can apply for their
      benefits. Ask NSSF what documents they need.
- [ ] **Bank accounts.** Decide whether to keep one - useful for pensions,
      property or coming back - or close it. Update your address and
      contact details either way.
- [ ] **M-Pesa and your SIM.** Move your M-Pesa balance out. If you keep the
      number, use it now and then so it is not recycled.

## Permits

- [ ] **Foreigners:** your work permit usually ends with the job. Ask your
      employer to cancel it properly, and return your foreigner certificate as
      the Directorate of Immigration Services requires. Overstaying a permit
      or pass can lead to fines and trouble on a future visit.

## The last week

- [ ] Photograph the empty flat, meters and keys at handover.
- [ ] Keep copies of your lease, receipts, payslips and tax records.
- [ ] Say goodbye properly. Nairobi has a way of pulling people back.

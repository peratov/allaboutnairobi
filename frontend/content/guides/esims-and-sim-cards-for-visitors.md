You will want mobile data from the moment you land: for ride-hailing, maps and
messaging. Kenya has good 4G coverage in towns and along main roads, and
patchy coverage in remote parks.

## Your options

**A local SIM card.** Cheapest for data, and the only way to get
[[M-Pesa]]. Buy at the airport or any Safaricom or Airtel shop with your
passport - registration is compulsory. Then buy a data bundle.

**A local eSIM.** Safaricom and Airtel offer eSIMs for compatible phones,
issued in a shop after the same registration. Useful if you want to keep your
home SIM in the phone.

**A travel eSIM.** Bought online before you leave from an international eSIM
seller, and activated by QR code. Convenient - you land connected - but data
usually costs more than a local bundle, and it does not include a Kenyan phone
number or M-Pesa.

**Roaming.** Easy, but often very expensive. Check your home network's Kenya
rates first.

## Which to choose

- **A short trip or safari:** a travel eSIM is simplest.
- **More than a week, or you want M-Pesa:** get a local Safaricom SIM or eSIM.
- **Both:** many people land on a travel eSIM, then add a local SIM for
  M-Pesa and cheaper data.

## Which network

- **Safaricom** has the widest coverage, including in many parks and along
  safari routes, and M-Pesa.
- **Airtel** is often cheaper for data and works well in towns.

## Before you travel

- **Check your phone is unlocked** and supports eSIM if you want one.
- **Download offline maps** for parks and remote areas.
- **Keep your passport handy**; you need it to register a local SIM.

Supermarkets are everywhere, but much of Nairobi still shops in its markets,
where food is fresher and cheaper and almost anything can be found. Each has
its speciality.

## Food markets

- **Wakulima Market (Marikiti)**, near Muthurwa in the CBD - Nairobi's main
  wholesale fruit and vegetable market. Lorries arrive overnight, and it is
  busiest and cheapest **very early in the morning**. Many mama mbogas and
  restaurants buy here.
- **City Market**, on Muindi Mbingu Street in the CBD - a covered market
  from the colonial era, known for **flowers, fish, meat** and crafts.
- **Kenyatta Market**, off Mbagathi Way - famous for its **nyama choma**
  and butcheries, with salons and stalls around them.
- **Neighbourhood markets** - most estates have one, from Kangemi to
  Githurai, as well as the [[mama mboga]] kiosks on every street.

## Clothes and mitumba

- **Gikomba**, east of the CBD - East Africa's best-known market for
  **mitumba** (second-hand clothes), shoes and bedding, as well as furniture
  and timber. Bales are opened early in the morning, when the best pieces go.
- **Toi Market**, off Ngong Road near Kibera - mitumba and household goods,
  closer to the west of the city.
- **Eastleigh** - a dense shopping district of malls and stalls selling new
  clothes, fabric, shoes and household goods, wholesale and retail, often
  cheaper than the CBD.

## Crafts and leather

- **Kariokor Market**, off Racecourse Road - leather sandals, bags and beadwork, often
  made on the spot.
- **The Maasai Market** - a travelling crafts market that sets up at
  different malls and open spaces on different days of the week. Check its
  current schedule before you go.
- **City Market** also has curio and souvenir stalls.

## Tips

- **Go early** for the freshest produce and the best mitumba.
- **Carry small notes and M-Pesa.** Most stalls take M-Pesa; check the name
  on the confirmation screen.
- **Bargain politely.** Prices for crafts and clothes are expected to be
  negotiated; food prices less so.
- **Watch your phone and wallet** in crowded markets, and keep bags in front
  of you. See [Safety in Nairobi](/guides/safety-in-nairobi).
- **Wear closed shoes** - markets can be muddy in the rains.
- **Wash produce** well at home.

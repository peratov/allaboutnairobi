Tipping in Kenya is appreciated rather than expected, and amounts are
modest. Many service workers earn low wages, so a tip for good service
makes a real difference. Tips can be given in cash or by M-Pesa.

## Restaurants and bars

- **Check the bill first.** Some restaurants add a **service charge**; if
  so, you need not tip more.
- Otherwise, **about 10%** in a sit-down restaurant is generous. Rounding up
  is fine in a casual place.
- In bars, leaving the change is common.
- Give the tip directly to the person who served you if you want them to
  get it.

## Hotels

- **Porters** - a small tip per bag.
- **Housekeeping** - a little at the end of your stay, left with a note.
- Many hotels and lodges have a **staff tip box** that is shared among all
  staff, including those you do not see.

## Getting around

- **Ride-hailing** - not expected; round up or tip in the app for good
  service.
- **Taxis** - round up.
- **Matatus and bodas** - not expected.
- **Hired drivers** - a tip at the end of a day or trip is usual.
- **Car guards** at parking spots - a few coins when you leave.

## Salons, barbers and services

A small tip for a hairdresser, barber or nail technician is common if you
are happy. Movers, delivery riders and fundis appreciate a little extra for
hard or careful work, or a cold drink.

## Safaris and tours

Tipping guides and drivers is an established part of safari travel, and an
important part of their income.

- Tip your **driver-guide** at the end of the trip, and the **camp or lodge
  staff** through the tip box.
- Ask your safari company for its **suggested amounts** - they usually give
  a guide per day.
- Tips can be in shillings or dollars.

See [Choosing a safari company](/guides/choosing-a-safari-company).

## Household staff

Tipping is not the way to pay household staff: they are employees, owed at
least the [minimum wage](/guides/minimum-wage-in-kenya) and their leave. Many
employers do give a **bonus or gift at Christmas** or for special effort,
and pay for extra hours. See [Hiring household staff](/guides/hiring-household-staff).

## When not to tip

- **Officials** - police, immigration or council staff. Offering money to
  an official can be treated as a bribe.
- **Anyone who demands it** before doing their job.

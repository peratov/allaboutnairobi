Most M-Pesa fraud does not break any system: it persuades you to send money
or share your PIN. Knowing the handful of tricks fraudsters repeat is the best
defence.

## The common scams

- **The fake M-Pesa message.** You get an SMS that looks like an M-Pesa
  confirmation saying someone sent you money, followed by a call: "I sent it
  to you by mistake, please send it back." Real M-Pesa messages come from
  **M-PESA**, not from a phone number. Check your actual balance before
  doing anything.
- **The fake Safaricom call.** Someone claims to be from Safaricom or your
  bank and says your line will be blocked, or you have won a prize, and asks
  for your PIN or a code sent to your phone. Safaricom will never ask for
  your M-Pesa PIN.
- **SIM swap.** A fraudster gets a replacement SIM in your name to receive your
  codes and empty your accounts. If your phone suddenly loses network for no
  reason, call your provider from another phone at once.
- **Fake jobs, loans and tenders.** You are asked to pay a "registration",
  "processing" or "medical" fee before you get a job or a loan. Genuine
  employers and licensed lenders do not charge this way.
- **Online shopping and rentals.** A seller or "agent" asks for a deposit to
  hold a phone, car or house you have not seen. See
  [Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing).
- **Investment groups.** A WhatsApp or Telegram group promises daily returns.
  See [Crypto and investment scams](/guides/crypto-and-investment-scams-in-kenya).

## Protect yourself

- Never share your **M-Pesa PIN**, bank PIN or one-time codes, with anyone.
- Set a **SIM PIN** on your phone and lock the M-Pesa app.
- Check a business's **paybill or till name** on the confirmation screen
  before you enter your PIN.
- Be suspicious of urgency: "today only", "your account will be closed".
- Hang up and call the official number yourself if in doubt.

## If you sent money to the wrong number

Ask Safaricom to reverse it. You can **forward the M-Pesa confirmation
message to 456**, use the M-Pesa or MySafaricom app, or call customer care.
Safaricom contacts the recipient, and if they do not dispute it the money is
returned. Safaricom does not charge to reverse a transaction and will not
ask you to go to an agent to do it.

If the recipient has already withdrawn the money or refuses, Safaricom may
ask you to report it to the police.

## Reporting fraud

- **Forward a fraud SMS to 333** - it is free and helps Safaricom block the
  number.
- **Report it to the police** at your nearest station and get an **OB
  number**. This matters for reversals, bank claims and insurance. See
  [Reporting a crime and lost documents](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).
- **Tell your bank** straight away if card or bank details were involved.
- For larger frauds and cybercrime, the **Directorate of Criminal
  Investigations** has a cybercrime unit.

## If you have been scammed

Act fast: the sooner the provider is told, the better the chance of freezing
the money. Then change your PINs and passwords, and warn friends if your
WhatsApp or social accounts were taken over - fraudsters use them to ask your
contacts for money.

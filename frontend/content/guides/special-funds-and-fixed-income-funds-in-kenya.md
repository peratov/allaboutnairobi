Money market funds no longer hold most of the money in Kenyan unit trusts.
**Special funds** and **fixed income funds** have grown fastest, attracted by
higher returns. They can be worth having - but they are not money market
funds, and the difference matters. This is general information, not advice.

## The family of unit trusts

Kenya's collective investment schemes held about
**KES {{ CIS_TOTAL_ASSETS_BN }} billion** at the end of June 2026, roughly
half in money market funds. The rest is mainly in:

- **Fixed income funds** - longer-term bonds, mostly Government of Kenya
  Treasury bonds and some corporate bonds.
- **Special funds** - funds the CMA approves for strategies outside the
  standard categories, often investing **offshore**, in foreign currencies,
  or in a mix of assets.
- **Balanced** and **equity funds** - shares and bonds together, or shares
  alone, for long-term growth.

## Fixed income funds

They hold bonds that pay a fixed coupon for years. That has two effects:

- When **interest rates fall**, the bonds they hold become more valuable, so
  the fund's unit price rises - which is why fixed income funds paid well as
  Kenyan rates came down.
- When **rates rise**, the price falls, and the fund can lose money over a
  few months.

Unlike most shilling MMFs, the **unit price moves**. Many have a minimum
holding period or charge for early withdrawal, and a minimum investment above
an MMF's.

## Special funds

Special funds can do things an MMF cannot - hold dollar or other foreign
assets, use more complex strategies, or concentrate in fewer holdings. Some
have become very large. Before investing, ask:

- **What exactly does it hold?** Read the factsheet and information
  memorandum, not the advert.
- **What currency** are the assets in, and is that risk hedged?
- **How and when can you withdraw**, and at what notice?
- **What are all the fees**, including any performance fee?
- **What happened in its worst month?** A fund with only good months has not
  been tested.

## Why they pay more

Higher returns come from taking more risk: longer-dated bonds, foreign
markets, currency exposure, or less liquid investments. That is not a reason
to avoid them, but it means they are **not a substitute for an emergency
fund**. Money you may need next month belongs in an MMF; money you can leave
for a few years can take more risk.

## Checking a fund

- It must be approved by the **Capital Markets Authority** as part of a
  collective investment scheme - check the CMA's list.
- Its manager, trustee and custodian should be named, and different
  companies.
- Be wary of "funds" sold through WhatsApp groups, promising fixed high
  returns, or without published factsheets. See
  [Crypto and investment scams](/guides/crypto-and-investment-scams-in-kenya).

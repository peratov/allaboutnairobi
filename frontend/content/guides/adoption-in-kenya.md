Adoption gives a child a permanent family and makes the adoptive parents
the child's legal parents. In Kenya it is tightly regulated under the
**Children Act 2022**, and it must go through a **licensed adoption
society** and the **High Court**. Informal "adoptions" give no legal
security to anyone.

## Types of adoption

- **Local adoption** - by Kenyan citizens living in Kenya.
- **Kinship adoption** - by a relative, such as a grandparent, aunt or
  uncle, or a step-parent adopting a spouse's child.
- **Foreign and intercountry adoption** - by foreigners or Kenyans living
  abroad. Kenya has had a **moratorium** on most of these since 2014, with
  kinship adoptions treated differently. Check the current position with
  the National Council for Children's Services before starting.

## Who can adopt

The law sets conditions on age, health, means and the age gap between the
adopter and the child, and assesses whether you can provide a stable home.
Single applicants and couples can adopt, with some restrictions. The adoption
society will explain what applies to you.

## The process

1. **Contact a licensed adoption society.** Only licensed societies can
   place children for adoption. Ask the National Council for Children's
   Services for the list.
2. **Assessment** - home visits, interviews, medical reports, police
   clearance, and references. See
   [Certificate of good conduct](/guides/certificate-of-good-conduct).
3. **Approval and matching** - the society's committee approves you and
   matches you with a child who has been declared free for adoption.
4. **Foster care period** - the child lives with you under supervision
   before the court application.
5. **Court application** - your advocate files in the **High Court**. A
   **guardian ad litem** and the children's officer report to the court.
6. **Adoption order** - once granted, the child is legally yours.
7. **New birth certificate** - the Registrar of Births enters the child in
   the adopted children register. See
   [Getting a birth certificate](/guides/getting-a-birth-certificate).

The whole process usually takes many months.

## Consent

The consent of the child's parents or guardians is generally required,
unless the child has been abandoned or the court dispenses with it. Older
children's views are taken into account.

## Avoiding illegal adoption

- **Never pay for a child** or accept a child from a hospital, home or
  individual outside the legal process. Child trafficking dressed up as
  adoption is a serious crime.
- Work only with **licensed societies** and an advocate experienced in
  adoption.

## Fostering and guardianship

If adoption isn't right, **foster care** and **legal guardianship** are
other ways to care for a child; the county children's office can advise.

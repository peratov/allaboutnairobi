Every SIM card in Kenya must be registered to an ID. Fraudsters sometimes
register lines using other people's ID details - then use them for scams,
loans or [[M-Pesa]] fraud that can be traced back to you. Checking takes a
minute.

## How to check

1. From a line on the network you want to check, dial **\*106#**.
2. Choose the option to **check numbers registered** to your ID.
3. You will get a list of lines on **that network** registered to your ID.

Each network shows only its own lines. To check all three - **Safaricom,
Airtel and Telkom** - dial \*106# from a line on each, or borrow a phone on
that network.

## If you find a number you don't know

- Use the **report** option in the same \*106# menu to flag it.
- Visit the network's **shop** with your **ID** and ask them to remove the
  line from your details.
- If you think it has been used for fraud, **report to the police** and get
  an OB number. See [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).

## Protecting yourself

- **Don't share photos or copies of your ID** casually - with "agents",
  online lenders or strangers. Write the purpose across copies you hand
  over.
- **Lost your ID?** Report it and replace it, and check your lines.
- **SIM swap** - if your phone suddenly loses signal and you didn't change
  anything, call your network straight away from another phone. A swapped
  SIM can be used to empty mobile money and bank accounts.
- **Set an M-Pesa PIN** no one can guess, and never share it or one-time
  codes.
- **Check your credit report** if you suspect loans in your name - see
  [Checking your CRB status](/guides/checking-your-crb-status).

See [M-Pesa fraud and scams](/guides/mpesa-fraud-and-scams-in-kenya).

## Registering a line

To register a new line, take your **ID** (or passport, for foreigners) to
a network shop or agent. See [Phones and SIM cards](/guides/phones-and-sim-cards).

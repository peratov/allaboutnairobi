Most Nairobi rentals come unfurnished, often without a fridge or cooker. This
is how people furnish a flat without overpaying.

## Furniture

- **Made to order.** Roadside furniture makers - many along Ngong Road and in
  the estates - make beds, sofas, tables and wardrobes to your design, usually
  for less than a shop. Agree the wood, the finish, the price and the delivery
  date in writing, pay a deposit, and check the work before paying the rest.
- **Shops and malls** for ready-made furniture, at higher prices.
- **Second-hand.** People leaving Nairobi sell whole households. Watch
  expatriate groups, Facebook Marketplace and Jiji.

A [[fundi]] can also make curtains, fit shelves and fix almost anything.

## Appliances

Fridges, cookers, washing machines and water dispensers are sold in
supermarkets such as Carrefour and Naivas, specialist appliance shops, and
online. Ask about the warranty and whether delivery and installation are
included. A **voltage stabiliser or surge protector** protects expensive
appliances from power fluctuations.

## Water, power and gas

- **Drinking water:** a dispenser with refillable bottles, or a filter.
- **Tokens:** know how to buy electricity [[tokens]] on your first day.
- **Gas:** buy a cylinder and a burner or cooker. See
  [Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi#cooking-gas).
- **Backup:** a rechargeable lamp and a power bank for outages.

## Security

- Check the locks and grilles, and change the locks if you are unsure who has
  keys.
- Many households add an alarm linked to a **security response company** for a
  monthly fee.
- Get to know the [[askari]] at the gate.

## The first-week list

- [ ] Bed and mattress
- [ ] Curtains - many flats have none
- [ ] Mosquito net, especially in the rainy seasons
- [ ] Gas cylinder and cooker
- [ ] Fridge
- [ ] Drinking water
- [ ] Buckets and a water container, for days the supply fails
- [ ] Electricity tokens
- [ ] Reusable shopping bags - plastic carrier bags are banned
- [ ] Internet installation booked

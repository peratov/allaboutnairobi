Kenyans abroad send billions of dollars home every year, and the cost varies
hugely between services. The difference is rarely in the fee you see - it is in
the exchange rate.

## What a transfer really costs

Every transfer has two costs:

1. **The fee** - shown up front.
2. **The exchange rate margin** - the gap between the rate you get and the
   **mid-market rate**, the one you see on Google or Reuters. A service with
   "zero fees" often makes its money here.

To compare, ask each service: **how many shillings will arrive for the same
amount sent?** That single number includes both costs.

## Your options

- **Online money transfer services** such as Wise, Remitly, WorldRemit,
  Sendwave and others are usually the cheapest, and can pay into M-Pesa within
  minutes. Wise uses the mid-market rate and charges a visible fee; others build
  more into the rate. Prices change and differ by country, so compare on the
  day.
- **M-Pesa Global** lets people in some countries send directly to M-Pesa
  wallets, and Kenyans to send out.
- **Traditional agents** like Western Union and MoneyGram have cash pickup
  across Kenya, useful for relatives without M-Pesa, but are often more
  expensive.
- **Bank transfers (SWIFT)** suit large sums, such as buying property, but carry
  fees on both ends and weaker exchange rates.

## Where the money lands

- **M-Pesa** - fastest and most convenient. Remember M-Pesa limits: KES
  {{ MPESA_MAX_TRANSACTION|shillings }} per transaction and KES
  {{ MPESA_MAX_DAILY|shillings }} held or moved per day.
- **A bank account** - better for large amounts and for paying developers,
  schools or advocates.
- **Cash pickup** - for relatives without an account.

## Tips

- **Check the rate at the moment you send.** Most apps show the amount
  received before you pay.
- **Send larger, less frequent amounts** if fees are flat.
- **First-transfer offers** can be good, but compare the normal price too.
- **Use the recipient's registered M-Pesa name** exactly; transfers to the
  wrong name are hard to reverse.
- **Keep records** of transfers for property purchases - your advocate and
  bank may need to show where the money came from.

## Watch out for

- **Requests for money from people you have not met**, or from a "relative" on
  a new number.
- **Paying for land or a building** to an individual rather than an
  advocate's client account. See
  [Kenyans abroad](/guides/kenyans-abroad-managing-things-from-afar).

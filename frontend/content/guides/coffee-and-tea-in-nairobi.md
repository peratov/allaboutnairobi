Kenya grows some of the world's most prized coffee and is one of the
largest tea exporters. Yet most Kenyans drink **tea**, not coffee. Nairobi
has both: sweet milky chai everywhere, and a growing café culture.

## Chai

**Chai** in Kenya is black tea boiled with milk, water and plenty of sugar,
sometimes with ginger or spices (**chai masala**). It is drunk at breakfast,
mid-morning and afternoon, at home, at work and at roadside kiosks, often
with a **mandazi** or **chapati**. Ask for "chai bila sukari" if you want
it without sugar.

Kenyan tea is mostly grown in the highlands west and east of the Rift
Valley, around Kericho, Limuru and the slopes of Mount Kenya.

## Kenyan coffee

- Kenyan coffee is grown on the slopes of **Mount Kenya**, the **Aberdares**
  and around **Kiambu**, near Nairobi, mostly by smallholder farmers in
  co-operatives.
- It is known for a bright, fruity, wine-like taste.
- Beans are graded by size - **AA** is the largest and best known, followed
  by **AB** and others.
- Most green coffee is sold through the **Nairobi Coffee Exchange** and
  exported. Kenyans have only recently started drinking much of it at home.

## The café scene

Nairobi has local café chains in every mall, independent cafés in Westlands,
Kilimani, Karen and the CBD, and coffee carts in office blocks. Many roast
their own Kenyan beans and sell them by the bag. Cafés are popular places to
work and meet. See [Working remotely from Nairobi](/guides/working-remotely-from-nairobi).

## Buying coffee and tea to take home

- **Coffee** - buy from roasters and cafés that state the **region** and
  **roast date**, or from supermarkets. Whole beans keep better than ground.
- **Tea** - supermarket brands of loose and bagged Kenyan tea are cheap
  and good; specialty shops sell **purple tea**, green tea and premium
  blends.
- Both make good, light gifts. Check your destination's rules on bringing
  food in.

## Visiting a farm

**Coffee farms in Kiambu**, just north of Nairobi, offer tours showing how
coffee is grown, picked, processed and roasted, usually with a tasting. Tea
estates around **Limuru** also run tours and lunches. Both make good
day trips. See [Day trips from Nairobi](/guides/day-trips-from-nairobi).

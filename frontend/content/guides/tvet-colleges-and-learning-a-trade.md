Not every good career starts at university. Electricians, plumbers,
mechanics, chefs, hairdressers, ICT technicians and many more train at
**TVET** (technical and vocational education and training) institutions -
and skilled tradespeople are in demand in Nairobi.

## Where to train

- **National polytechnics** and **technical training institutes** run by the
  government, offering certificates and diplomas in engineering, building,
  ICT, business, hospitality and more.
- **Vocational training centres**, run by county governments, for craft
  and artisan courses, often at low cost.
- **Private colleges** offering everything from beauty and fashion to
  accounting and IT.
- **Technical universities** for higher diplomas and degrees.

Check that an institution is registered with the **TVET Authority** (TVETA),
which publishes a list of accredited institutions.

## Levels

- **Artisan certificates** - practical, entry-level skills.
- **Craft certificates** - a step up.
- **Diplomas** and **higher diplomas** - technician level, and a route to
  degrees.

Many courses are now **competency-based** (CBET), assessed on what you can
do in practice, with certificates from the TVET Curriculum Development,
Assessment and Certification Council or KNEC.

## Getting a place

- **KUCCPS** places students in public TVET institutions on
  government-supported places, through the same portal used for universities.
  See [Applying to university](/guides/applying-to-university-in-kenya).
- You can also **apply directly** to most colleges, which take several
  intakes a year.

## Trade tests

If you already have skills learned on the job, the **National Industrial
Training Authority** (NITA) runs **trade tests** that certify artisans at
different grades. A trade test certificate helps you get jobs, contracts and,
in some trades, the licences you need - for example as an electrician.

## Paying for it

- Government-sponsored TVET students have been supported through
  **scholarships** and **HELB loans** for TVET. Check the current rules, as
  student funding is being reformed.
- County and constituency **bursaries** often cover TVET.
- Many courses are short and affordable.

## Apprenticeships and attachments

Most courses include an **industrial attachment** with an employer. Some
employers and programmes offer apprenticeships, where you earn while you
learn.

## Working afterwards

- Register any licences your trade requires - see
  [Finding a fundi](/guides/finding-a-fundi-in-nairobi) for electricians and
  builders.
- Many tradespeople start their own business. See
  [Small business ideas in Nairobi](/guides/small-business-ideas-in-nairobi)
  and [Registering a business](/guides/registering-a-business-in-kenya).

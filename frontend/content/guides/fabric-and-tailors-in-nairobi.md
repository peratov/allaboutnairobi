Getting clothes made is ordinary in Nairobi: a good tailor can copy a
picture, alter anything, and make a suit, dress or school uniform for less
than a ready-made one. It starts with choosing the fabric.

## Fabrics to know

- **Kitenge** - bold, colourful printed cotton, used for dresses, shirts
  and outfits for weddings and celebrations.
- **Kanga** - a printed cotton cloth with a border and a Swahili saying,
  traditionally worn as a wrap.
- **Kikoy** - a striped cotton cloth from the coast, used for wraps,
  sarongs and towels.
- **Maasai shuka** - the red or checked blanket cloth, now used for
  everything from blankets to jackets.
- **Suiting, shirting and lining** - imported fabrics for suits, uniforms
  and office wear.

## Where to buy fabric

- **Biashara Street** in the CBD - long known for kanga, kikoy, kitenge
  and household fabrics.
- **Eastleigh** - a huge range of fabric, wholesale and retail, often the
  cheapest, along with ready-made clothes.
- **Fabric shops in the CBD** around River Road and Moi Avenue, for suiting,
  lining and haberdashery - buttons, zips and thread.
- **Markets** such as City Market and the Maasai Market for kikoys, shukas
  and smaller pieces. See [Markets in Nairobi](/guides/markets-in-nairobi).
- **Malls** have some upmarket fabric and curtain shops.

Fabric is usually sold by the **metre** or by the **piece**. Bargain at
market stalls; less so in established shops.

## Finding a tailor

- **Ask around** - friends, colleagues and neighbours often swear by
  their own tailor.
- **Market and estate tailors** work from small stalls with a sewing
  machine, and are cheap and quick for alterations.
- **Fashion designers and boutiques** make higher-end and custom pieces.
- Many fabric shops have tailors nearby.

## Getting something made

1. **Bring a picture** of what you want, or a garment to copy.
2. **Get measured** properly, and ask the tailor how much fabric to buy
   before you buy it.
3. **Agree the price and the collection date**; pay a deposit, not the full
   amount.
4. **Go for a fitting** before it is finished, especially for suits and
   occasion wear.
5. **Allow extra time** before weddings, graduations and December, when
   tailors are busiest.

## Alterations and repairs

Hemming, taking in, replacing zips and repairing clothes are quick and cheap
at any market or estate tailor, often while you wait - and give new life to
[[mitumba]] finds.

Many Kenyan fund managers run a **US dollar money market fund** alongside
their shilling one. It works the same way - pooled savings in short-term,
low-risk debt, interest every day - but your money stays in dollars. This is
general information, not advice.

## What they pay

Dollar funds pay less than shilling funds, because dollar interest rates are
lower. In {{ MMF.period }} they averaged **{{ MMF.average.USD }}%** a year
before tax, against **{{ MMF.average.KES }}%** for shilling funds, with the
top dollar fund at {{ MMF.USD[0]['yield'] }}%. Compare them in the
[MMF comparer](/tools/mmf-comparer) under "Dollar funds".

## Why hold dollars at all

The lower yield buys protection against the **shilling falling**. Over long
periods the shilling has tended to lose value against the dollar, sometimes
sharply, and when it does, dollar savings are worth more shillings.

A dollar fund suits you if you:

- **earn in dollars** - freelancers, remote workers, people paid by
  companies abroad (see [Receiving PayPal, Payoneer and Wise payments](/guides/receiving-paypal-payoneer-and-wise-payments))
- **will spend in dollars** - school or university fees abroad, travel, a
  dollar loan or rent
- want part of your savings in a second currency

If you earn and spend only in shillings, and will need the money soon, a
shilling fund usually pays you more.

## The currency trade-off

The comparison is not just yield against yield. If the shilling holds steady,
the shilling fund wins. If the shilling falls by more than the gap between
the two yields over the time you hold, the dollar fund wins. Nobody knows
which in advance - which is why many people split savings between the two.

## Minimums and lock-ins

Dollar funds often ask for more to start and have stricter terms. From the
funds' own documents:

- **Etica's** dollar fund starts at USD 100 and holds new money for
  **30 days** before it can be withdrawn.
- **Cytonn's** dollar fund starts at USD 1,000, with top-ups from USD 10.

Ask any dollar fund about **lock-in periods**, **minimum withdrawals** and
**fees on dollar transfers**.

## Paying in and out

- **Paying in:** usually by bank transfer from a **dollar account**, in Kenya
  or abroad. Some funds accept shillings and convert them - check the
  exchange rate they use, which can cost more than the fee.
- **Paying out:** to a dollar bank account. Withdrawing to M-Pesa means
  converting to shillings.
- Bank charges on international transfers can eat a small balance's interest
  - send larger amounts, less often.

## Tax

Interest from a dollar fund is taxed the same way as from a shilling fund:
**{{ INTEREST_WHT_RATE|percent }}% withholding tax**, final for resident
individuals.

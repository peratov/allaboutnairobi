Kenya does not leave fuel prices entirely to the market. The **Energy and
Petroleum Regulatory Authority** (EPRA) sets the **maximum pump prices** for
petrol, diesel and kerosene every month, and those caps affect almost every
other price in the city - from matatu fares to food.

## The monthly review

- EPRA announces new maximum prices on the **14th of each month**.
- They take effect from the **15th** and run to the 14th of the next month.
- Prices differ by town: they are **lowest in Mombasa**, where fuel lands,
  and rise inland with transport costs.
- Stations may sell **below** the cap, but not above it.

Pump prices are published on EPRA's website and widely reported in the news
on review day.

## What makes up the price

A litre at the pump is built from:

- **The landed cost** of the fuel - the world oil price, shipping and the
  exchange rate of the shilling against the dollar.
- **Storage and distribution** costs, and the oil marketers' margins.
- **Taxes and levies** - [VAT](/guides/registering-for-vat) at
  **{{ VAT_RATE|percent }}%**, excise duty, the road maintenance levy and other
  levies. Together these are a large share of the pump price.

## What moves it

- **World oil prices** - conflict and supply disruptions abroad show up in
  Kenya within a month or two.
- **The shilling** - a weaker shilling makes imported fuel dearer.
- **Tax changes** - the annual Finance Act can raise or cut levies, usually
  from 1 July.
- **Stabilisation** - the government sometimes uses a fund to cushion
  prices, holding them steady for a month or more.

## What it means for you

- **Matatu fares** and **taxi fares** often rise soon after a big increase.
- **Food and goods** follow, as transport costs pass through.
- **Kerosene** price changes hit households that cook or light with it.
- If you drive, fill up before the 15th when a rise is expected.

See [Cost of living in Nairobi](/guides/cost-of-living-in-nairobi) for how
fuel feeds into household budgets.

## If a station overcharges

Selling above the EPRA cap is an offence. Note the station, date, time and
price - keep your receipt - and report it to EPRA. See
[Consumer rights in Kenya](/guides/consumer-rights-in-kenya).

Where you live in Nairobi decides how much of your day you spend in traffic.
Pick the area by where you will be at 8am and 6pm, then by budget. Every
neighbourhood below is on the [map](/map); rough rents are in
[Finding housing](/guides/finding-housing#rough-asking-rents).

## The CBD

The [Central Business District](/map#constituency/starehe) is the old city
centre: government offices, banks, [[Huduma Centre]]s, bus stages and markets.
Busy and useful by day, quiet and best avoided on foot late at night. Few
people choose to live here.

## Westlands and Parklands

[Westlands](/map#constituency/westlands) is Nairobi's second centre: offices,
malls such as Sarit and Westgate, restaurants and most of the nightlife.
Parklands next door is residential, with a long-established Kenyan Asian
community, the Aga Khan hospital and City Park. Convenient, busy, and
expensive for what you get.

## Kilimani, Kileleshwa, Lavington and Hurlingham

These are in [Dagoretti North](/map#constituency/dagoretti-north), just west
of the centre. **Kilimani** and **Kileleshwa** are dense with apartment blocks
and popular with young professionals: close to Westlands, Upper Hill and the
CBD, with shops and cafés along Argwings Kodhek and Ngong roads. **Lavington**
is leafier, with more houses and townhouses.

## Upper Hill

Office towers, hospitals and a few apartment blocks on the ridge south-west of
the CBD. A short commute for people who work there.

## Gigiri, Runda, Muthaiga and Rosslyn

North of the centre, in [Westlands constituency](/map#constituency/westlands).
Big houses, embassies, the UN offices at Gigiri, Village Market and Two
Rivers. Quiet and green, with long commutes into town on Limuru Road.

## Karen and Lang'ata

South-west, in [Lang'ata](/map#constituency/langata). **Karen** has large
plots, forest, riding and the Giraffe Centre; it feels semi-rural, and most
residents drive. **Lang'ata** is more mixed, with estates close to Wilson
Airport, Bomas and the national park.

## South B, South C, Nairobi West and Madaraka

Established middle-class estates south of the centre. Good value, close to
Mombasa Road, Strathmore University and the Industrial Area, and a reasonable
run to the airport.

## Eastlands

The big estates east of the centre: Buruburu, Donholm, Umoja, Kayole,
Embakasi and Utawala, across the five
[Embakasi constituencies](/map#constituency/embakasi-east) and
[Makadara](/map#constituency/makadara). This is where much of Nairobi lives.
Rents are lower and life is busy and local. Commutes into the centre can be
long. Utawala and Embakasi are handy for JKIA.

## Along Thika Road

[Roysambu](/map#constituency/roysambu) and
[Kasarani](/map#constituency/kasarani), with Garden City, the Kasarani
stadium, USIU and Kenyatta University. The Thika Superhighway made the area
popular with students and young families. Traffic at the Muthaiga and Pangani
end is bad at peak times.

## Kibera and Mathare

Two of Nairobi's largest informal settlements, in
[Kibra](/map#constituency/kibra) and [Mathare](/map#constituency/mathare).
Mathare is by far the most densely populated constituency on the map. Both
are home to hundreds of thousands of people, with strong communities and a
great deal of small enterprise, but with poor housing and services. Visit with
someone who lives there or a community-run tour, not as a spectator.

## Just outside the county

Many Nairobi workers live in satellite towns with lower rents: Ruaka and
Kikuyu to the north-west, Syokimau and Kitengela to the south-east, and Rongai
to the south. They are good value if your commute runs against the rush-hour
flow, and a hard slog if it runs with it.

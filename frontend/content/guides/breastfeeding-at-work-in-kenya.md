Going back to work doesn't have to mean stopping breastfeeding. Kenya's
**Health Act 2017** gives nursing mothers rights at work, and with some
planning many mothers keep breastfeeding or expressing milk.

## Your rights

- **Lactation stations** - employers are required to provide a private,
  clean room or space where mothers can breastfeed or express milk. It
  should have a place to wash hands, a fridge or cooling facility, a socket
  for a breast pump, a table and a comfortable seat - and it must **not be
  a toilet**.
- **Nursing breaks** - breastfeeding mothers are entitled to breaks to
  breastfeed or express, counted as **paid working time**, within the limits
  set by the law and guidelines.
- **No discrimination** - you must not be treated less favourably because
  you are breastfeeding.

The Ministry of Health has published **workplace guidelines** for employers.
Many workplaces still fall short; asking politely, in writing, often helps.

## Maternity leave

You are entitled to **maternity leave** on full pay, and annual leave can
often be added. See [Leave entitlements in Kenya](/guides/leave-entitlements-in-kenya).

## Planning your return

- **Talk to your employer** or HR before you return about where and when
  you'll express milk.
- **Ask about flexible hours**, a phased return or working from home for
  the first weeks.
- **Practise expressing** and feeding from a cup or bottle a few weeks
  before you go back.
- **Childcare near work** can let you breastfeed during breaks. See
  [Daycare and preschool in Nairobi](/guides/daycare-and-preschool-in-nairobi).

## Expressing and storing milk

- **Pumps** - manual or electric pumps are sold at pharmacies and baby
  shops. Bring yours, plus clean containers.
- **Storage** - keep expressed milk in a fridge or a cool bag with ice
  packs, and label it with the date and time.
- **Power cuts** - a cool bag with ice packs is a good backup.
- **Hygiene** - wash hands and sterilise pump parts.

Ask a midwife, nurse or lactation consultant for guidance on how long milk
keeps.

## If your employer refuses

- Raise it with **HR** in writing, quoting the Health Act.
- Speak to your **union** if you have one.
- Get advice - see [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya)
  and [Working in Kenya](/guides/working-in-kenya).

These are the four places most Kenyans keep savings. None is best for
everything: each trades return against access, risk and effort. This is
general information, not financial advice.

## Money market fund - for money you may need soon

The best home for an **emergency fund** and short-term goals: it earns
close to Treasury bill rates, you can add any amount whenever you like, and
you can usually withdraw within days. The rate is not fixed, and it is not
insured, so choose a large, CMA-approved fund. See
[Money market funds in Kenya](/guides/money-market-funds-in-kenya) and the
[MMF comparer](/tools/mmf-comparer).

## SACCO - for saving that leads to a loan

A SACCO's real value is **borrowing**: after saving regularly for a few
months, members can usually borrow several times their deposits, at rates
well below banks and mobile lenders. Returns can be good, but **deposits are
hard to withdraw** - often only by leaving the SACCO, after a notice
period - and the return is declared once a year. Check the SACCO is licensed
by **SASRA** if it takes deposits. See
[SACCOs in Nairobi](/guides/saccos-in-nairobi).

## Treasury bill - for a lump sum you won't need for months

A **fixed rate** from the government, for 91, 182 or 364 days. Bought
directly from the Central Bank through **DhowCSD**, with no fees in between.
You need the minimum, and the money is locked until maturity. Longer
**Treasury bonds** pay more for years at a time, and **infrastructure bonds**
are tax-free. See
[Buying Treasury bills and bonds](/guides/buying-treasury-bills-and-bonds).

## Fixed deposit - for simplicity at your bank

A bank locks your money for a term at an agreed rate. It is easy if you
already bank there, and deposits are covered by the **Kenya Deposit
Insurance Corporation** up to its limit. Rates are negotiable: ask, and
compare with Treasury bills, which often pay more. Breaking the deposit
early usually costs interest.

## Putting them together

Many people use more than one:

1. **An MMF** for three to six months of spending.
2. **A SACCO** if they will want to borrow - for land, a car or a business.
3. **Treasury bills or bonds** for larger sums they won't need for a while.
4. **[Pensions](/guides/pensions-in-kenya)** and [shares](/guides/investing-on-the-nse)
   for the long run.

Your phone number is your identity in Kenya. It is how you pay with
[[M-Pesa]], receive eCitizen codes, and how your bank, landlord and delivery
riders reach you.

## The networks

- **Safaricom** - the largest network, the best coverage outside cities, and
  M-Pesa. Most people have a Safaricom line even if they also use another.
- **Airtel** - usually cheaper data and calls, with Airtel Money.
- **Telkom** - the smallest of the three.

Many people carry two SIMs: Safaricom for M-Pesa and a second network for
cheaper data, and as a backup when one network is down.

## Getting a SIM

Buy from an official shop, a mall kiosk or an agent, including at the airport.
**SIM registration is compulsory**: bring your national ID, or your passport if
you are a foreigner. A SIM sold without registration will be switched off.

**eSIMs** are available from Safaricom and Airtel for phones that support them.
Set one up in a shop with your ID.

## Airtime and data

- **Top up** with M-Pesa, bank apps, or scratch cards from any shop.
- **Buy bundles** through the network's app or its USSD menu. Daily, weekly and
  monthly data bundles are far cheaper than paying as you go.
- **Home internet** is usually cheaper than mobile data for heavy use. See
  [Utilities in Nairobi](/guides/utilities-in-nairobi#internet).

## Keeping your number

You can move your number to another network (number portability) at the new
network's shop. Keep your line active: a SIM unused for months can be
recycled, taking your M-Pesa and eCitizen access with it.

## Roaming

Kenya is part of the **East Africa One Network Area**, which cuts roaming
charges on participating networks in Uganda, Rwanda and South Sudan. Elsewhere,
buy a roaming bundle before you travel, or a local SIM.

## If your phone is lost or stolen

1. **Call your network from another phone** and ask them to block the SIM.
   This protects your M-Pesa.
2. **Report it** at a police station and get an abstract - see
   [Reporting a crime and lost documents](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents).
3. **Replace the SIM** at a network shop with your ID. Your number, and your
   M-Pesa balance, come back with it.
4. **Change passwords** for email and banking apps.

## Scams

Nobody from your network will ask for your M-Pesa PIN or a code sent to your
phone. See [M-Pesa and mobile money](/guides/mpesa-and-mobile-money#scams).

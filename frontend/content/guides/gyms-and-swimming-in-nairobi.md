Nairobi has every kind of gym, from a room of weights above a shop in an
estate to hotel health clubs with pools and saunas. What suits you depends on
where you live, how much traffic you will face to get there, and how you
like to train. See also [Sport and fitness](/guides/sport-and-fitness-in-nairobi).

## The kinds of gym

- **Chain gyms** in malls and business districts - long hours, many
  machines, classes, and often several branches on one membership.
- **Neighbourhood gyms** in estates - cheapest, close to home, simpler
  equipment.
- **Hotel and country club health clubs** - pools, saunas and quiet floors,
  at a premium, sometimes with day passes.
- **Boutique studios** for CrossFit, boxing, spinning, yoga, pilates and
  dance.
- **Apartment gyms** - many newer blocks in Kilimani, Westlands and
  Kileleshwa have one in the service charge. Check it before paying for
  another.

## Choosing one

- **Location beats everything.** A gym 20 minutes away in Nairobi traffic is
  an hour round trip. Pick one near home or work, on your route.
- **Visit at the time you will use it.** Early mornings and after work are
  crowded everywhere.
- **Try before you sign** - most offer a free session or a day pass.
- **Check the power backup.** Treadmills stop in an outage without a
  generator.
- **Parking and showers**, if you go before work.

## Contracts and payment

Memberships are sold monthly, quarterly or annually, with discounts for
paying ahead. Before signing, ask:

- Can you **freeze** the membership when travelling?
- What is the **cancellation** notice, and is there a fee?
- Are **classes** and the **pool** included or extra?

Paying a year in advance at a new gym is a risk: if it closes, getting the
money back is hard. Pay by M-Pesa or card to a business account and keep the
receipt.

## Personal trainers

Trainers work for gyms or freelance, at the gym, at home or outdoors. Ask
about **certification**, ask for a trial session, and agree what a package
includes in writing.

## Swimming

Public pools are few. Most swimming is at **hotels, clubs, schools and gyms**,
many of which sell day passes or monthly swimming-only memberships. Ask
about water treatment and lifeguards, especially for children. Swimming
lessons for adults and children are widely offered.

## Fit for little or nothing

- **Run or walk** in [Karura and the city's forests](/guides/running-in-nairobi).
- **Outdoor bootcamps** in parks, at a fraction of gym prices.
- **Home workouts** - a mat, a skipping rope and resistance bands go far.
- **Hike** with a [weekend group](/guides/hiking-near-nairobi).

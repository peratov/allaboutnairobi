Most insurance disputes in Kenya come down to three things: a claim reported
late, a missing document, or an exclusion nobody read. This is how to avoid
those and what to do when a claim stalls. General information, not advice.

## Making a claim

1. **Tell the insurer quickly.** Policies set a time limit for reporting,
   often a few days. Report first, gather paperwork second.
2. **Get the documents the claim needs:**
   - a **police abstract or OB number** for theft, burglary and road accidents
     (see [Reporting a crime](/guides/reporting-a-crime-and-lost-documents))
   - receipts or proof of ownership and value
   - medical reports and invoices for health and injury claims
   - for a death claim, the **death certificate** and the beneficiary's ID
3. **Don't repair or replace first** without the insurer's agreement, except
   to prevent more damage. An assessor may need to see it.
4. **Keep copies** of everything sent, and the date you sent it.

## If the claim is delayed or refused

- Ask for the reason **in writing**, citing the clause of the policy.
- If you think it is wrong, write to the insurer's **complaints** or customer
  service head, with the claim number and your documents.
- If you bought through a **broker or agent**, ask them to push - that is
  part of what they are paid for.

## Complaining to the regulator

The **Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA)** licenses insurers, brokers and
agents, and handles complaints from policyholders. Complain to the IRA once
the insurer has had a chance to respond, with your policy, claim number and
the correspondence. The IRA also publishes which insurers, brokers and agents
are licensed - check before you buy.

Disputes that the IRA cannot settle can go to the courts, or to arbitration if
the policy provides for it.

## If an insurer collapses

When an insurer is placed under statutory management, the **Policyholders
Compensation Fund (PCF)** pays eligible claims up to
**KES {{ PCF_MAX_COMPENSATION|shillings }} per claim**. That is a ceiling, not
the value of your claim, so a larger claim is only partly paid. Watch for the
PCF's notice inviting claims, and file through it.

## Avoiding disputes

- **Disclose honestly** when you buy - a past illness, a previous claim, who
  drives the car. Non-disclosure is the commonest reason for refusal.
- **Read the exclusions and the excess.**
- Keep **sums insured** up to date.
- **Pay premiums on time**; a lapsed policy pays nothing.

Most cars in Nairobi are Japanese imports, and the city has a huge trade in
spare parts for them - new, used and fake. Knowing where to go and what to
ask saves a lot of money.

## Where to go

- **Kirinyaga Road and Grogan** - on the edge of the CBD, the city's best
  known cluster of spare parts shops and open-air garages, with mechanics,
  auto-electricians, panel beaters and upholsterers side by side. Almost any
  part for a common model can be found here.
- **Industrial Area** - larger parts dealers and distributors, especially
  around Baricho Road, often with better stock of genuine parts.
- **Franchise dealers** - the official dealers for each brand sell
  **genuine** parts with warranties. Dearest, but safest for newer cars.
- **Online** - marketplaces and parts shops that deliver. Check the seller
  has a physical shop and a return policy.

## New, used or copy

- **Genuine (OEM)** - made by or for the carmaker. Best quality, highest
  price.
- **Aftermarket** - made by other brands. Quality ranges from good to poor;
  buy known brands.
- **Ex-Japan used** - second-hand parts from Japan, sold from containers.
  Often good value for body parts, engines and gearboxes.
- **Fakes** - copies sold as genuine. Common with filters, brake pads, plugs
  and bearings. Very low prices are a warning sign.

## Buying well

- **Take the old part** or your car's **chassis number** - it identifies the
  exact part. Photos of labels help.
- **Compare prices** at two or three shops; the first price is rarely the
  last.
- For **safety parts** - brakes, steering, suspension, tyres - pay for
  quality.
- **Ask for a receipt** that names the part and whether it is genuine, and
  ask about returns before paying.
- Be wary of **touts** who lead you to a shop and add their cut, and of
  anyone who wants to swap parts out of sight.

## Mechanics

Many people buy the part themselves and pay a [[fundi]] to fit it - so you
know what went in. Ask friends for a trusted mechanic, and keep your old
parts until the job is done. See [Finding a fundi](/guides/finding-a-fundi-in-nairobi).

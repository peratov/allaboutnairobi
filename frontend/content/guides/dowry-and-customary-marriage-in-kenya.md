For many Kenyan couples, the wedding in a church or at the registrar comes
after - or alongside - **customary ceremonies** between the two families.
The best known is the payment of **dowry** (bride price). Customs differ
between communities and families, so treat this as a general outline and
ask the elders in both families.

## The usual stages

1. **The introduction.** The man tells his family, and a small group visits
   the woman's family to make their intentions known. Among the Kikuyu this
   first visit is known as *kumenya mucii* - "knowing the home".
2. **The negotiation.** Representatives of the two families meet to agree
   the dowry. The man's side usually brings gifts, and elders or chosen
   negotiators speak for each family. It can take one meeting or several.
3. **The payment and celebration.** The dowry - or part of it - is
   handed over at a ceremony with food, speeches, songs and blessings.
   Among the Kikuyu this is the **ruracio**; among the Luo, dowry is known
   as **nyombo**; among the Kalenjin, the ceremony is the **koito**.
4. **Further ceremonies.** Some communities hold another ceremony to
   complete the marriage, such as the Kikuyu **ngurario**.

## What dowry is

- Traditionally it was **livestock** - cattle, goats and sheep - with other
  gifts. Today it is often a mix of livestock, cash, and gifts such as
  blankets, clothes, utensils and drinks for particular relatives.
- The amount is agreed between the families. It is not a "price" in the
  commercial sense, but a sign of respect and a bond between the families.
- It is commonly paid **in instalments** over years.

## Practical tips

- **Agree a budget** as a couple first, and decide who will speak for you.
- **Let the elders lead** the negotiation: the couple often stay out of the
  room.
- **Keep records** of what has been paid.
- Expect the host family to cater for many guests; costs add up on both
  sides. Plan with the [budget planner](/tools/budget-planner).
- If the families come from different communities, agree early whose
  customs to follow, or how to combine them.

## Is it legally required?

No law requires dowry for a civil, Christian, Islamic or Hindu marriage.
Under the **Marriage Act 2014**, a **customary marriage** - one celebrated
under the customs of a community, which usually involves dowry - is a full
legal marriage, but it must be **registered** with the Registrar of
Marriages. Registration protects both spouses and any children. See
[Getting married in Kenya](/guides/getting-married-in-kenya).

## Foreign partners

A foreign partner marrying a Kenyan can expect to take part in the family
ceremonies. Ask the Kenyan family to explain each step and what is expected,
and see [Planning a wedding in Nairobi](/guides/planning-a-wedding-in-nairobi)
for the legal paperwork.

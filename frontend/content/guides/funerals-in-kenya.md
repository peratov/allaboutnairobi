Funerals in Kenya are large, communal events, often lasting days or weeks
from the death to the burial. Whether you are arranging one, contributing,
or attending for the first time, this explains how they usually work. For
the legal steps and the estate, see
[Death and succession in Kenya](/guides/death-and-succession-in-kenya).

## The first days

- **Notify the family** and, if the death was at home, the **police** and a
  doctor, who certify the death.
- **Move the body to a mortuary.** Hospitals have mortuaries, and Nairobi
  has public and private funeral homes. Mortuaries charge by the day.
- **Register the death** and get a **burial permit** from the civil
  registration office. The body cannot be buried or moved for burial
  without it.

## The funeral committee

Families usually form a **funeral committee** - relatives, friends,
colleagues - with a chair, treasurer and secretary. It meets, often in the
evenings in Nairobi, to:

- plan the service, the burial and transport
- set a **budget**
- raise money through a **harambee** - contributions from family, friends,
  colleagues, church and community

Contributions are increasingly sent by **M-Pesa** to a paybill or a named
treasurer. Check you are sending to the right account: fraudsters sometimes
circulate fake funeral appeals.

## Burial

- Many families bury their relatives **upcountry**, at the family's
  ancestral home, which means transporting the body - often with a convoy
  of mourners.
- In Nairobi, public cemeteries such as **Lang'ata** are very full, and
  space is limited. Private cemeteries and memorial parks outside the city
  offer plots.
- Muslim burials take place quickly, usually within a day.
- **Cremation** is available in Nairobi, mainly used by Hindu families but
  open to others.

## What it costs

The biggest costs are usually the **mortuary**, the **coffin**, **transport**
upcountry, **catering** for mourners, and the **burial** itself. Funerals can
be very expensive, which is why committees and harambees exist. **Last
expense** cover from an insurer, SACCO or employer pays a sum quickly. See
[Life insurance in Kenya](/guides/life-insurance-in-kenya).

## Attending a funeral

- **Condolence visits** to the family's home are common in the days after
  a death. Bring a small contribution if you can.
- **Funeral services** include tributes, a eulogy that tells the person's
  life story, hymns and speeches, and can be long.
- **Dress** in sober, dark or muted colours. Some families choose a theme
  colour; ask.
- Tell your employer: many give time off for close family funerals. See
  [Leave entitlements](/guides/leave-entitlements-in-kenya).

## Deaths of foreign nationals

Contact the person's embassy, which can advise on repatriation and
documents. See [Death and succession in Kenya](/guides/death-and-succession-in-kenya#deaths-of-foreign-nationals).

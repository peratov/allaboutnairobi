If you own land or a house in Nairobi, you owe **land rates** to Nairobi City
County every year, whether or not anything is built on the plot. Tenants do
not pay them; the owner does. This is general information.

## How the bill is worked out

From 1 January 2026 the county charges **{{ NAIROBI_LAND_RATE|percent(3) }}%
a year** of the plot's **unimproved site value** - the value of the land
alone, ignoring any buildings - as shown in the county's **2019 Draft
Valuation Roll**. The same rate applies to residential, commercial and
agricultural plots, under the National Rating Act, 2024.

So a plot the roll values at KES 10,000,000 pays about
KES {{ (10000000 * NAIROBI_LAND_RATE / 100)|shillings }} a year. Some areas are charged
a **flat rate** per plot instead.

**[Work out your plot's rates with the land rates calculator ➞](/tools/land-rates-calculator)**

Owners who **objected** to their valuation in the draft roll keep paying the
old amount until the Valuation Board decides their case.

## Finding your bill

You need the plot's **land reference (LR) number**, or the plot number the
county uses. With it you can see the bill and any arrears on the county's
online services. See [Nairobi County services](/guides/nairobi-county-services).

Apartment owners usually pay nothing directly: the rates for the whole block
are charged on the land and recovered through the **service charge**.

## Paying

Pay through the county's own online services or the official paybill shown
on the bill, and keep the receipt. Never pay rates into a person's account or
a number someone sends you.

## Arrears and penalties

Unpaid rates attract **penalties and interest**, and they stay with the
land. The county can sue for them, and in the end recover them from the land
itself. It periodically announces **waivers** of penalties for owners who
clear the principal - worth watching for if a plot has years of arrears.

## Selling or transferring

A transfer of Nairobi land needs a **rates clearance certificate** from the
county, showing nothing is owed. When you buy, make the seller clear the
arrears before completion - otherwise they become yours. See
[Buying property in Kenya](/guides/buying-property-in-kenya).

## Land rent is different

Leasehold land held from the national government also carries **land rent**,
paid to the Ministry of Lands, not the county. A transfer needs clearance on
both.

Most of your rights as a tenant in Kenya come from your **tenancy
agreement**. A clear, written one protects both you and the landlord, and
settles most arguments before they start. Read it all before paying the
deposit.

## What it should include

- **The parties** - the landlord's full name (or the company's) and yours,
  with contacts, and the agent if there is one.
- **The property** - the house number, building and what is included:
  parking, a store, furniture, appliances.
- **The rent** - the amount, the date it is due, how it is paid (paybill or
  bank account), and any penalty for paying late.
- **The deposit** - how much, what it can be used for, and **when it will
  be returned** after you leave.
- **The term** - a fixed period (often a year) or month to month, and what
  happens at the end.
- **Rent increases** - how often, and how much notice you get.
- **Service charge and utilities** - who pays for water, electricity,
  rubbish collection and security, and how they are billed.
- **Repairs** - which repairs the landlord is responsible for (usually the
  structure, roof, plumbing and wiring) and which are yours.
- **Notice to leave** - how much notice each side must give.
- **Rules** - pets, subletting, Airbnb, alterations, visitors and parking.

## Clauses to question

- A deposit that is **not refundable**, or with no date for its return.
- **Rent increases at any time**, or by any amount the landlord chooses.
- You being responsible for **all repairs**, including the structure.
- The landlord being able to **enter at any time** without notice.
- **Penalties** that are out of proportion, such as losing the whole
  deposit for a late payment.

You can negotiate: ask for changes in writing before you sign.

## Before you sign

1. **Inspect the house** with the caretaker or agent, and list existing
   damage - stains, cracks, broken fittings. Take dated photos, and attach
   the list to the agreement.
2. **Check meter readings** for water and electricity.
3. **Get copies** of the signed agreement and receipts for the deposit and
   first rent.
4. Make sure the person signing for the landlord has the authority to do so.
   See [Rental scams in Nairobi](/guides/rental-scams-in-nairobi).

## During the tenancy

- Pay rent by a method that leaves a record.
- Report repairs **in writing** - a message or email - and keep copies.
- Keep any notice of a rent increase or of ending the tenancy.

## Leaving

Give the notice the agreement requires, in writing. Do a final inspection
with the caretaker, compare with your move-in photos, hand over the keys
against a signature, and ask for the deposit back in writing. See
[Tenant rights in Kenya](/guides/tenant-rights-in-kenya) for what to do if it
is not returned.

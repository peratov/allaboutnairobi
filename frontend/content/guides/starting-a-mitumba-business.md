[[Mitumba]] - second-hand clothes and shoes, mostly imported in bales - is
one of Nairobi's biggest trades. Many people start small, selling from a
stall, a car boot or social media, and grow from there.

## How the trade works

Importers bring in compressed **bales** of clothes, graded by type and
quality. Wholesalers sell the bales at markets such as **Gikomba**, where
traders buy them to sell piece by piece. See
[Buying wholesale in Nairobi](/guides/buying-wholesale-in-nairobi).

## Buying bales

- **Choose a niche** - children's clothes, jeans, jackets, dresses, shoes,
  bedding or sportswear. Knowing one category well helps you buy well.
- **Know the grades** - higher grades have more good-condition pieces and
  cost more. Lower grades are cheaper but include more you can't sell.
- **Buy from trusted wholesalers** - many traders stick with one seller who
  gives them fair bales. Some let you see a bale opened.
- **Start small** - a single bale, or share one with another trader, while
  you learn what sells.
- **Opening day** - the first pieces from a fresh bale (the "first grade")
  sell for the most. Price the rest lower as they are picked over.

## Where to sell

- **Market stalls** in your estate or at a weekly market. You may need a
  county permit for the space - see [Nairobi business permit](/guides/nairobi-business-permit).
- **Online** - social media pages and marketplaces, with photos, sizes and
  delivery by boda. Many sellers now do most sales this way.
- **Door to door and offices** - especially for higher-grade items.
- **A small shop** once you have steady customers.

## Money

- **Costs** - bales, transport, stall rent or permit, packaging, delivery,
  and your time washing and ironing.
- **Margins** come from careful buying and pricing, and selling through
  each bale before the next.
- **Track every bale** - what you paid and what you earned - so you know
  which grades and sellers pay.
- Accept [[M-Pesa]] with a [[till number]] - see
  [M-Pesa and mobile money](/guides/mpesa-and-mobile-money).

## Risks

- A **bad bale** with damaged or unsellable items.
- **Competition** - many sellers in popular categories.
- **Policy changes** - import duties and rules on second-hand clothes have
  changed before and can affect prices.

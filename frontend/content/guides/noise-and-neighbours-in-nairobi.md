Loud bars next to apartments, all-night church services, construction at
dawn and parties next door are among the most common complaints in Nairobi,
especially in mixed areas such as Kilimani, Westlands and Lavington. The law
does set limits.

## The rules

- National **noise regulations** set maximum levels for residential areas:
  **{{ RESIDENTIAL_NOISE_DAY_DB }} decibels by day** and
  **{{ RESIDENTIAL_NOISE_NIGHT_DB }} decibels at night** - roughly a quiet
  conversation by day, and close to silence at night.
- Nairobi County's **public nuisance law** bans loud music and noise that
  disturbs people living nearby.
- Bars, clubs, churches, events and construction sites must keep within the
  limits, and some need **permits** for loud activities or night work.

## Start with a conversation

Most disputes are best solved directly:

1. **Talk to the neighbour**, calmly, at a good time - not in the middle of
   the noise. Many people do not realise how far sound travels.
2. In an apartment, raise it with the **caretaker, landlord or management
   committee**. Buildings usually have house rules on noise.
3. If you are a tenant, check your [tenancy agreement](/guides/tenancy-agreements-in-kenya).

## When that does not work

- **Keep a record** - dates, times, what the noise was, recordings on your
  phone, and other residents affected.
- **Complain in writing to Nairobi City County**, whose environment
  department handles most noise complaints. Several residents complaining
  together carry more weight.
- **Report to NEMA**, the National Environment Management Authority, which
  enforces the noise regulations and has run crackdowns on noisy clubs and
  churches.
- **Report to the police** at night, when noise is disturbing the peace.
- **Residents' associations** often take up noise from bars and events on
  behalf of a whole area.

The county and NEMA can inspect, measure noise levels, order a premises to
comply, and close it or prosecute if it does not. Residents have also gone to
the **Environment and Land Court**, which has ordered noise assessments.

## Other neighbour disputes

- **Boundaries and walls** - check the survey maps and title; see
  [Land search on Ardhisasa](/guides/land-search-on-ardhisasa).
- **Rubbish and drains** - see [Waste and recycling](/guides/waste-and-recycling-in-nairobi).
- **Building works** - new buildings need county approval; you can ask the
  county whether a project next door has it.
- **Shared spaces in apartments** - see
  [Sectional titles](/guides/sectional-titles-for-apartments-in-kenya).

Mediation - through a residents' association, the chief, or a professional
mediator - is often quicker and cheaper than court.

## Choosing where to live

Before renting or buying, visit at night and at the weekend, and check what
is nearby: bars, churches, event venues and building sites. See
[Neighbourhoods of Nairobi](/guides/neighbourhoods-of-nairobi).

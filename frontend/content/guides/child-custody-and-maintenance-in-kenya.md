When parents separate - or were never together - Kenyan law expects both to
care for and support their children. The **Children Act 2022** sets the
rules, and the **best interests of the child** come first in every
decision.

## Parental responsibility

- **Both parents** have parental responsibility, whether or not they were
  married.
- Both must **maintain** the child - food, shelter, clothing, education and
  health - according to their means.
- Responsibility does not end because a parent has no custody.

## Agreeing between yourselves

Most families do best with an **agreement** on where the child lives, when
the other parent sees them, and who pays for what. Write it down. A
mediator, church leader or family elder can help; courts also offer
**court-annexed mediation**. An agreement can be recorded by the court so
it can be enforced.

## Going to court

If you can't agree:

1. **Get advice** - see [Free legal aid in Kenya](/guides/free-legal-aid-in-kenya).
   Children's officers at the county children's office also help.
2. **Apply** to the **Children's Court** (a magistrate's court) where the
   child lives, for custody, access or maintenance orders.
3. In urgent cases, ask for **interim orders** while the case continues.
4. The court considers the child's welfare - their age, needs, wishes where
   old enough, stability, and each parent's ability and means.

## Custody and access

- **Custody** decides where the child lives and day-to-day care.
- **Access** sets when the other parent spends time with the child.
- Courts can grant **joint** custody where it works for the child.
- Young children often stay with their main carer, but there is no
  automatic rule in favour of either parent.

## Maintenance

- The court sets maintenance according to the **child's needs** and **each
  parent's income**.
- Payments can be ordered for school fees, medical costs and monthly
  upkeep, paid by [[M-Pesa]] or bank so there is a record.
- **Keep receipts** for what you spend on the child.
- If circumstances change, either parent can ask the court to **vary** the
  order.

## Enforcement

If a parent ignores a court order, go back to the court. Courts can enforce
orders, including by attaching income or property, and contempt can be
punished.

## Travel and documents

Agree in writing before a child travels abroad with one parent; a passport
for a child needs the parents' consent. See
[Applying for a Kenyan passport](/guides/applying-for-a-kenyan-passport) and
[Getting a birth certificate](/guides/getting-a-birth-certificate).

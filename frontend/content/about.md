All About Nairobi is a free, independent guide to living in Nairobi. It
explains the things that are obvious to everyone who already knows them and
hard to find out if you do not: how the eTA works, what comes off a Kenyan
payslip, how to buy electricity tokens, which matatu stage to stand at, and
where in the city you can afford to live.

It is modelled on [All About Berlin](https://allaboutberlin.com), which has
spent years proving that a city deserves one honest, well-maintained manual
rather than a hundred blog posts written once and abandoned.

## Who this is for

- **People moving to Nairobi** from anywhere, including Kenyans coming home
  after years away.
- **People who already live here** and have hit a specific wall: a payslip
  that does not add up, a deposit that never came back, an eCitizen form that
  will not submit.
- **People helping someone else** move here, who would rather send a link
  than write the same message again.

It is not a tourism site or a relocation agency. Nothing here is sponsored.

## How this site stays accurate

The hard part of a site like this is not writing it. It is stopping it from
going stale.

Kenyan figures move. Finance Acts change the tax bands, the NSSF limits rise
every February, and minimum wages are revised by legal notice. A guide written
in good faith in March can be wrong by August, and a reader has no way to tell.

So the figures are not typed into the guides. They live in one file,
`content/constants.yaml`, and each one carries:

- **where it came from** &mdash; the Act, the regulation or the agency that
  publishes it
- **when a person last checked it** against that source
- **when it should be checked again**

The guides pull their numbers from that file, so the PAYE bands in the tax
guide and the ones on the home page cannot disagree: there is only one copy.

The [map](/map) works the same way. Its boundaries, census figures and
landmark positions come from named public sources, listed under the map with
the date they were fetched.

## What this site cannot do for you

- **Fees at the counter are the real fees.** Published schedules and what you
  are asked to pay are not always the same. Confirm before you pay.
- **This is not legal, tax or immigration advice.** Knowing what the law says
  is useful leverage; it is not the same as having a lawyer or an accountant.
- **Offices change their minds.** Requirements vary between branches and
  sometimes between officers. Bring more paperwork than you think you need.

## Corrections

If something here is wrong, out of date, or missing the one detail that would
have saved you a wasted trip, [say so](/contact). Corrections from people who
have just been through the process are the most valuable thing this site gets.

When you sell property in Kenya, you pay **{{ CAPITAL_GAINS_TAX_RATE|percent }}%**
capital gains tax on the gain - not on the price. Every cost you can prove
makes the gain, and the tax, smaller.

{% tool "capital-gains-tax-calculator" %}

## What counts as cost

- **What you paid** for the property.
- **The costs of buying it** - [stamp duty](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator),
  advocate's fees, valuation and survey.
- **Improvements** - building the house, an extension, a borehole, a
  perimeter wall. Repairs and maintenance do not count.
- **The costs of selling** - the agent's commission and legal fees.

Receipts are everything. A seller who cannot show what a house cost to build
may be taxed as if the whole price were profit.

## Exemptions

No tax is due on your own home if you lived in it for the three years before
the sale, on transfers to a spouse or close family, on inheritance, and on
some small farms outside towns. The conditions are strict - see
[Capital gains tax on property](/guides/capital-gains-tax-on-property-in-kenya).

## Paying

The seller files and pays on **iTax** on or before the transfer, and the
land registry will not register it without proof. In practice, the advocate
handling the sale does it from the purchase money.

Every way of moving money between currencies charges twice: once in the fee
you see, and once in a rate a little worse than the real one. A service
advertising "zero fees" has usually moved the whole charge into the rate.

Type what a service quotes - its rate, or what it says will arrive - and its
fee. This compares it with today's mid-market rate and says what you lose, in
shillings and as a share of your money. Nothing you type leaves this page.

## What counts as a good rate

The **mid-market rate** is the midpoint between what banks pay for a currency
and what they sell it for. Nobody sells to the public at it, so some gap is
normal. The question is how big.

The best yardstick there is comes from the United Nations. One of its
Sustainable Development Goals, target 10.c, is to bring the cost of sending
money home **below {{ REMITTANCE_COST_TARGET|percent }}%** - fee and exchange
rate margin together - and to end any route that costs **more than
{{ REMITTANCE_COST_CEILING|percent }}%**. The checker judges every quote
against those two lines.

## How to use it well

- **Compare at the same moment.** Rates move through the day, and an app's
  quote is usually held for a few minutes only.
- **Compare what arrives, not the fee.** Two quotes for the same amount can be
  compared on the shillings delivered alone, which already counts both
  charges.
- **Check the amount.** Many services charge a smaller share on a larger
  transfer, so check the sum you will actually send.
- **Count the other end.** Withdrawing from M-Pesa, a bank's charge for an
  incoming SWIFT payment or a cash pickup fee is a cost too.

## Where the rate comes from

The mid-market rate is fetched by this site from an open exchange rate
service and kept for an hour, so your browser asks no third party anything.
The source and the time it was set are printed under every result. It is not
the Central Bank of Kenya's indicative rate; to compare against that, or
against another service's quote, open "Compare against another rate" and
type it in.

## How to read the table

- **Yield** is the fund's average for the month, as an effective annual rate,
  **after** the manager's fee and **before** tax. It is how funds publish
  their returns, so it compares like with like.
- **After tax** takes off the {{ INTEREST_WHT_RATE|percent }}% withholding tax
  that every fund deducts from interest. This is what you actually keep.
- **Earns a year** is the after-tax yield on your amount, if the rate stayed
  where it is - which it will not exactly. For daily and monthly figures and
  top-ups, use the [MMF calculator](/tools/mmf-calculator).
- **Minimum** and **fee** appear only where the fund's own factsheet,
  brochure or website confirms them. "Ask" means we could not confirm it.

## The top fund changes

The order shifts from month to month. Funds near the top often hold more
longer-dated or riskier paper, or are smaller and newer. A fund that stays in
the top third for a year tells you more than one that tops a single month.
The gap between the best funds is often a few tenths of a percent - on
KES 100,000, a few hundred shillings a year - so speed of withdrawal, the
manager and how you pay in can matter more.

## Beyond the yield

Before you choose, check:

- that the fund is **approved by the Capital Markets Authority**
- **how fast** withdrawals reach M-Pesa or your bank, and how many are free
- the **size** of the fund and the **manager** behind it
- any **lock-in** - some dollar funds hold new money for a period

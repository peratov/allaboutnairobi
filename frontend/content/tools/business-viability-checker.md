Every part of Nairobi is a different market. A kibanda that sells out by
lunchtime in Kamukunji could sit quiet on a leafy road in Westlands, and a
salon that charges Westlands prices would struggle in Mathare.

This tool ranks Nairobi's 11 census sub-counties for the trade you have in
mind. It uses real figures from the 2019 census, and shows you every figure
behind the answer.

{% tool "business-viability" %}

## What the score is, and what it is not

**It is a ranking.** Each measure becomes the sub-county's position among all
11. The score is a weighted average of those positions. 80 means *this
sub-county is near the top of Nairobi on the things this trade needs*. It does
not mean an 80% chance of anything.

**The weights are our judgement**, not data. We think a kibanda needs passing
trade more than wealthy neighbours. You may disagree. The breakdown shows each
factor's share of the score, so you can argue with a specific number rather
than the whole answer.

!!! warning "What no dataset can tell you"
    Whether four other people already sell the same thing on that street.
    Whether the water runs and the power stays on. Whether the landlord wants
    a big deposit. Whether the spot is visible from the road. Whether the
    last tenant failed there, and why.

    **Go and stand on the street at the hour you would be trading, and
    count.** This tool is for shortlisting areas before you spend a day
    doing that, not for replacing it.

## Where the figures come from

Every figure is from the **2019 Kenya Population and Housing Census**,
published by the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS) for Nairobi's 11
census sub-counties. They are the same figures shown on the [map](/map).

| Measure | Census figure |
| --- | --- |
| Passing trade, and space to operate | People per km² |
| Market size | Population |
| Spending power, and cost of premises | Share of households with a computer |
| Children, young adults, women | Share of people under 15, aged 20 to 34, and female |
| Renting households | Share of households that rent |
| Internet use | Share of people using the internet |
| Piped water | Share of homes on piped water |
| Cooking with gas | Share of homes cooking with gas or electricity |

The census publishes no incomes or shop rents for sub-counties. So
**computer ownership stands in for spending power**. It is a rough sign of
where money is, not a measure of it. The figures are from 2019, and fast-growing
areas have changed since.

Sub-counties are large, and most mix very different neighbourhoods. Westlands,
for one, takes in leafy estates and crowded ones alike. The score describes
the sub-county as a whole, not any one street.

## Use it with the map

The checker tells you *which* sub-counties rank well. The [map](/map) shows
*where* they are, the constituencies and neighbourhoods inside them, and the
same census figures for each.

A sensible order of work:

1. **Shortlist here.** Pick three or four sub-counties, not one.
2. **Open the map** and look at the neighbourhoods inside each one.
3. **Then go and look**, at the hour you would trade.

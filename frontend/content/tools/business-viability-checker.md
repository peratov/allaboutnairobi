Every district in Greater Accra is a different market. A kenkey stand that
sells out by ten in Ashaiman would sit untouched in Shai Osudoku, and a
boutique that works in Osu has no customers in Ada West.

This ranks all 29 district assemblies for the trade you have in mind, using
real census counts, and shows you every figure that produced the answer.

{% tool "business-viability" %}

## What the score is, and what it is not

**It is a ranking.** Each measure is turned into the district's position among
all 29 assemblies, and the score is a weighted average of those positions. 80
means *this district is near the top of Greater Accra on the things this trade
needs*. It does not mean an 80% chance of anything.

**The weights are our judgement**, not data. We think a barbershop needs
passing trade more than it needs wealthy neighbours; you may disagree, and the
breakdown shows every factor's contribution so you can argue with the specific
number rather than the whole answer.

!!! warning "What no dataset can tell you"
    Whether four barbers already work that street. Whether the water runs.
    Whether the landlord will ask for two years up front. Whether the spot is
    visible from the road. Whether the previous tenant failed there, and why.

    **Go and stand on the street at the hour you would be trading, and count.**
    This tool is for shortlisting districts before you spend a day doing that,
    not for replacing it.

## Where the figures come from

| Figure | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Population, age, sex, employment | Ghana Statistical Service, 2021 Population and Housing Census |
| People and premises per km² | Census counts and OpenStreetMap, divided by geoBoundaries areas |
| Major roads per km² | OpenStreetMap |
| Typical rents | A five-way ordinal band, from listing observation |

The rent figure is a **band, not an amount**. Ghana publishes no rent index at
district level, so there are deliberately no cedi figures per district
anywhere on this site.

## Use it with the map

The map is the other half of this. The checker tells you *which* districts
rank well; the map shows you *where* they are, which constituencies they hold,
and lets you shade all 29 by population, age, employment, informality or rent
at once.

A sensible order of work:

1. **Shortlist here.** Three or four districts, not one.
2. **Shade the map** by the measure that matters most to your trade &mdash;
   density for a food stand, rent for a boutique.
3. **Read the district panel** for the constituencies and the landmarks in it.
4. **Then go and look**, at the hour you would trade.

Your KCSE mean grade decides which KUCCPS courses you can apply for. Most
candidates sit eight or nine subjects, but only
{{ KCSE_SUBJECTS_COUNTED }} count.

{% tool "kcse-mean-grade-calculator" %}

## Which subjects count

Since the 2023 exam, KNEC builds the mean grade from:

- **Mathematics**, always
- **your best language**: English, Kiswahili or Kenyan Sign Language
- **your best five other subjects**, which can include your second language

Before 2023, more subjects were fixed, including both English and Kiswahili
and two sciences. That is why older calculators give a different answer.

## Points and grades

Each grade is worth points, from **A = 12** down to **E = 1**:

| Grade | Points | Grade | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 12 | C | 6 |
| A- | 11 | C- | 5 |
| B+ | 10 | D+ | 4 |
| B | 9 | D | 3 |
| B- | 8 | D- | 2 |
| C+ | 7 | E | 1 |

The {{ KCSE_SUBJECTS_COUNTED }} subjects' points are added up, out of 84. The
total gives the mean grade: 81 to 84 is an A, 74 to 80 an A-, 67 to 73 a B+,
and so on down to E.

## What the grade opens

A mean grade of **{{ KCSE_UNIVERSITY_ENTRY_GRADE }}** or above usually
qualifies you for a university degree through KUCCPS. Each course also sets
**cluster subjects** and its own cut-off, and competitive courses such as
medicine, law and engineering ask for far more. Below
{{ KCSE_UNIVERSITY_ENTRY_GRADE }}, diploma, certificate and TVET courses are
open, and many lead on to a degree later. See
[Applying to university in Kenya](/guides/applying-to-university-in-kenya).

The KNEC result slip is the official grade. This calculator is an estimate
from the grades you enter.

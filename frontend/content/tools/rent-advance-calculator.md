The rent is not the problem. The advance is.

## What the law allows

Section 25(5) of the [[Rent Act]] 1963 (Act 220) makes it an offence to demand
or receive more than **{{ MAX_LEGAL_RENT_ADVANCE_MONTHS }} months** of rent in
advance on a monthly tenancy.

Landlords in Accra routinely ask for {{ TYPICAL_RENT_ADVANCE_MONTHS }} months,
and often two years.

## Why it matters even though nobody enforces it

You will probably pay it. Housing is scarce, landlords set the terms, and the
alternative to paying is usually no house.

Knowing the number is still worth something:

- It is grounds for a complaint to the **Rent Control Department**, which
  mediates free of charge
- It is leverage when negotiating the length of the advance
- It matters a great deal if the tenancy later goes wrong

## Do not forget the rest

The advance is not the whole cost of moving in. Budget separately for the agent
commission (usually one month), the tenancy agreement, a polytank at around
&#8373;{{ POLYTANK_1000L_PRICE|cedis }} if the property has no water storage,
and prepaid meter credit.
